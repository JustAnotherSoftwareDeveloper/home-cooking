# Recipe Sources & Research Guide (Recipes Only)

**Scope:** How we find and vet **recipe** sources for ingredients, ratios, techniques, timing, and gear
**Out of scope:** nutrition/macros/health claims.
**Exception:** we still verify **food safety** and **technique correctness** (see §9).

---

## 1) Source Tiers (Use 5–7 per recipe)

**Tier 1 — Core, Individual, Hands-On**
- Small, clearly personal blogs written by an identifiable cook (home or pro) with detailed steps and process photos.
- Authentic social posts from real cooks: Reddit threads (e.g., r/Cooking, r/AskCulinary) with discussion; Instagram/TikTok/YouTube where the creator demonstrably cooks the dish and answers questions in comments.
- Community/church/regional cookbooks (including scans) with narrative notes and context.

**Tier 2 — Corroborating, Technique-Focused**
- Specialty forums and Q&A threads with multi-user agreement and dated replies.
- Chef/test-kitchen pieces with deep method discussion (not SEO listicles).

**Well-Regarded Recipe Sites (baseline/triangulation only; never the sole basis)**
- Method-driven sites with strong editorial/testing culture (e.g., Serious Eats, Cook’s Illustrated/ATK, King Arthur Baking, The Woks of Life).
- Use to confirm timings/temps/ratios or sanity-check techniques—**not** to replace Tier 1 voices.

---

## 2) What to Avoid
- Blogspam/AI-ish rewrites: generic photos, vague times, no temps, no troubleshooting.
- Networked “multi-site” content with identical ingredient orders/writing voice.
- “Best ever/ultimate” roundups that lack method detail or comments.
- Techniques that look implausible with no evidence, explanation, or demonstration.

---

## 3) Search Protocol (Authenticity-First)
1. Write **4–6 queries** combining the dish name with **authorship/authenticity modifiers**, not “goal/technique” fluff:
   - Authorship cues: \"family recipe\", `grandma`, `mom's`, \"passed down\", \"from my\", \"I've been making\", \"what I learned\", `notes`, `troubleshooting`, \"mistakes I made\", `backstory`, \"since 19\".
   - Home-cook signals: `home cook`, `my kitchen`, `at home`, `weeknight`, `from scratch` (use sparingly), `no sponsor`, `not sponsored`.
   - Evidence cues: \"step by step\", \"process photos\", \"how it looks\", \"texture cues\", \"why this works\".
   - Optional domain qualifiers: `site:reddit.com`, `site:youtube.com`, `site:wordpress.com`, `site:blogspot.com` (avoid over-filtering).
   - Optional negative filters to reduce spam: `-pinterest -buzzfeed -listicle -roundup`.
   - (If helpful) include common **synonyms/spellings** of the dish name; language variants are optional and secondary to authenticity.
2. Pull **8–12** candidates across blogs, forums, and video/social.
3. Shortlist **5–7** diverse sources: ≥1 personal blog/video **and** ≥1 forum/thread; avoid duplicates from the same network.
4. Scan creator comments/discussion for corrections, failure reports, ratio debates.
5. Record publish/update dates; save archives if practical.

---

## 4) Extract These Data From Each Source
- **Yield & vessel:** pan/tin dimensions, pot capacity, batch size.
- **Time/temperature:** preheats, simmer/bake ranges, internal targets when applicable.
- **Core ratios:** salt per lb (or %), hydration %, fat %, spice ratios, flour:liquid, etc.
- **Technique & sequence:** order of ops, rests/ferments/proofs, cut/grind sizes, mixing/emulsification steps.
- **Cues:** texture/visual/audio checkpoints (nappe, windowpane, bead size, sizzle level).
- **Cooking tools & materials:** required/optional gear (e.g., 12\" cast-iron vs clad steel, Dutch oven size, processor vs mortar, sieve mesh).
- **Distinctives:** choices to offer as variations (e.g., lard vs oil finish, hand-cut vs milled).

---

## 5) Reconcile & Decide
- Identify the overlapping **ratio band** (e.g., 1.6–2.2% salt; 65–70% hydration).
- Choose a grounded middle; surface authentic alternatives as **variations**.
- Convert to U.S. customary units; include parenthetical metric when the source is metric.
- If deviating from a popular method, include a one-line rationale (what issue it fixes).

---

## 6) Citation / Attribution (Recipe Footer)
List **2–4** top sources compactly:
- **Author or Site** — *Title* (Year/Date). For videos/social, add timestamps if a specific segment informed the method.

*Example:*
- Jane Doe — *Cast-Iron Skillet Biscuits* (2022).
- r/Cooking thread — “Buttermilk ratio debate” (accessed 2025-10-22).
- Serious Eats — *Foolproof Pan Sauce* (2021), timing baseline only.

---

## 7) Research Notes Template (Keep Outside the Recipe)

```yaml
recipe: <working title>
date: <YYYY-MM-DD>
core_question: <ratio/technique/timing you’re resolving>
sources:
  - author: <name or handle / site>
    title: <post/video/thread>
    url: <link>
    published: <YYYY-MM-DD or YYYY>
    accessed: <YYYY-MM-DD>
    notes: <specific contribution: ratio, cue, timing, tool, technique>
variant_matrix:
  yield: [...]
  time_temp: [e.g., 375°F 35–40m | 350°F 45m]
  key_ratios: [e.g., 1.8% salt; 30% fat; 68% hydration]
  technique: [e.g., mortar vs processor; autolyse 20m]
  tools: [e.g., 12" cast-iron; 5-qt Dutch oven; #20 mesh]
decisions:
  - <final ratio/technique/tools + rationale>
open_questions:
  - <what to test next>
```

---

## 8) Video & Social Specifics
- Prefer creators who **show hands/food**, list amounts, and respond to questions.
- Capture critical timestamps for technique cues; note pinned corrections/updates.

---

## 9) Safety **and** Correctness Checks
- **Food safety:** cross-check hazards tied to method (bean lectins, canning temps, sous-vide times, egg safety, cooling/holding).
- **Technique correctness:** flag and fix steps that are likely wrong (e.g., emulsions with inverted phase order, impossible bake temps, under-salting brines, high-heat searing in low-smoke oils) unless strong evidence supports them.
- When adjusting for safety/correctness, keep a brief note in research and a succinct explanation in the recipe.

---

## 10) Authenticity Signals (Home-Cook, Not Blogspam)
**Positive signals**
- Clear creator identity/voice; process shots that teach (cut size, dough feel, sauce texture).
- Concrete measurements **and** sensory cues; troubleshooting in comments; version updates.
- Reasonable pantry brands or substitutions a typical home kitchen might use.

**Red flags**
- Vague “cook until done,” no temps/cues, or contradictory steps.
- Identical phrasing/structure across multiple sites; obvious SEO filler.
- Pretty photos, zero technique; no discussion/feedback loop.

---

## 11) Final QA (Pre-Publish)
- 5–7 diverse sources logged (incl. ≥1 individual creator and ≥1 forum/discussion).
- Variant matrix complete (ratios, time/temp, technique, **tools**).
- Units standardized; rationale for key decisions captured.
- Safety/correctness issues reviewed; fixes documented.
- Compact attribution added to recipe footer.
