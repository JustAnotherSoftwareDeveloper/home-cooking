# Dried Cherry Red Wine Brownie Bars

## Title & Overview
These are fudgy, self-serve brownie bars with red wine built into both the dried cherry soak and the batter, so the flavor reads as dark fruit and chocolate rather than raw alcohol. The bars are baked in a parchment-lined light metal 8x8 pan, cooled completely, and cut into 16 small squares for low-mess movie-night snacking [1][2][6][7]. Dried cherries stay tucked inside the brownie instead of becoming a drippy topping, and the finish stays dry: flaky salt or a light cocoa dusting only. They hold well on a covered tray through a 45 to 90 minute snack window, then store airtight at room temperature [5].

## Ingredients
- 1/2 cup dried cherries **[pre-prep]**
- 1/2 cup red wine, divided: 1/4 cup for the cherry soak and up to 1/4 cup for the batter **[pre-prep]**
- 1/2 cup unsalted butter
- 4 ounces bittersweet chocolate, chopped
- 1 cup granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
- 1/4 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
- 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
- Optional flaky salt or cocoa powder, for dry finishing

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** dairy, wheat/gluten, egg, alcohol.
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Dried cranberries for dried cherries -> tangier fruit and slightly less cherry-wine depth -> use the same warm-wine soak and cooling method [2].
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - Fresh berries for dried cherries -> adds free water and creates a messier, wetter bar -> use dried fruit instead.
  - Ganache, glaze, or frosting -> can smear during self-serve snacking -> keep the finish dry with flaky salt or cocoa.
- **Diet toggles:**
  - Gluten-free: not tested here; use a tested gluten-free brownie formula rather than swapping flour blindly.
  - Dairy-free: not tested here; butter and chocolate structure the fudgy set.

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** fudgy chocolate brownie bars with wine-plumped dried cherries folded through the batter.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** set edges, a moist but not raw center, and a toothpick with moist crumbs rather than wet batter [5].
- **Workflow:** soak and cool the cherries first; while they cool, line the pan, melt the chocolate-butter base, and whisk the eggs and sugar.
- **Make-ahead:** the slab cuts cleaner after complete cooling and a 30 to 60 minute chill [3][7].
- **Serve window:** set the cut bars on a covered tray with napkins; they stay neat and snackable for 45 to 90 minutes at room temperature.
- **Primary risks:** too much free wine, underbaking to a fork-only center, cutting while warm, or using glass/dark pans without timing adjustments [3][6].

## Gather & Stage
- Measure and group all ingredients by phase; place them within reach.
- Line the Sheet / baking tray, 8x8, light metal with a parchment sling, leaving overhang on two sides.
- Set the Standard oven rack in the middle and preheat the oven as directed in Step 2.
- Complete the dried cherry wine soak and let it cool before mixing it into the batter.
- Set out the cutting board, 9-in chef knife, clean towel, serving tray, and napkins for later service.
- **Checkpoint:** everything needed for Step 1 is ready and within reach.

## Instructions

1. Soak the dried cherries
   - **Warm** the dried cherries with 1/4 cup red wine in a small stainless steel pot over **low heat** for 3 to 5 minutes, until the wine is steaming, the cherries look glossy, and the pan does not smell sharply alcoholic [2][4].
     - Remove from the heat and cool 10 to 15 minutes, until warm but not hot.
     - Drain off any obvious free wine into a measuring cup; reserve it for the batter wine in Step 3.
     - *If the cherries look soupy -> drain until only glossy fruit remains -> the reserved wine should sit separately instead of pooling around the cherries.*
     - **Checkpoint:** the cherries are plump, glossy, and cooled enough that they will not scramble the eggs.

2. Heat the oven and melt the chocolate base
   - **Preheat** the Standard oven to **350 deg F** and keep the lined Sheet / baking tray, 8x8 nearby; 350 deg F and a 30 to 40 minute bake are common for 8-inch red wine brownies [1][2].
     - **Melt** the butter and chopped bittersweet chocolate in a small stainless steel pot over **low heat** for 3 to 5 minutes, stirring often, until mostly melted, glossy, and fluid.
     - Pull the pot off the heat and keep stirring 1 to 2 minutes, until the last pieces melt from residual heat; let cool 5 to 10 minutes, until warm and pourable but not hot.
     - *While this cools: whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, and fine salt in a small bowl.*
     - *If the chocolate smells scorched or shows dry flecks -> stop heating, transfer the glossy portion to a clean bowl without scraping the bottom -> the saved mixture should smell chocolatey, not burnt.*
     - *If the chocolate looks separated or oily -> keep stirring off heat until glossy -> the mixture should fall from the spatula in a smooth ribbon.*
     - **Checkpoint:** the pan is lined, the oven is hot, and the chocolate base is warm, glossy, and pourable.

3. Mix the brownie batter
   - **Whisk** the sugar, eggs, and vanilla in the mixing bowl for 1 to 2 minutes, until the mixture looks slightly lighter and smoother.
     - **Whisk** in the warm chocolate-butter mixture.
     - Measure the reserved soak wine from Step 1, then add only enough fresh red wine to make 1/4 cup total batter wine; **whisk** that 1/4 cup total batter wine into the bowl [1][2].
     - **Fold** in the flour-cocoa-salt mixture just until no dry pockets remain.
     - **Fold** in the cooled soaked cherries, leaving behind any extra unplanned liquid.
     - *If reserved soak wine plus fresh wine would exceed 1/4 cup -> hold back the extra liquid -> the batter should be glossy and thick, not loose or pourable like cake batter.*
     - **Checkpoint:** the batter is thick, dark, and evenly speckled with cherries.

4. Bake to moist-crumb doneness
   - **Spread** the batter evenly in the prepared pan and smooth the top; add a small pinch of flaky salt only if using a dry finish.
     - **Bake** at **350 deg F** for 30 to 40 minutes, until the edges are set, the center looks moist but not uncooked, and a toothpick shows moist crumbs rather than wet batter [1][2][5].
     - Optional doneness cross-check: very fudgy brownies are often checked around 205 deg F internal temperature [6].
     - *If using the glass 8x8 Baking pan -> expect 5 to 10 extra minutes and longer cooling -> the center should reach moist-crumb doneness before cutting.*
     - *If using the dark 9x9 Baking tray -> reduce the oven temperature about 25 deg F or check 5 minutes early -> the edges should set without drying before the center is done.*
     - *If the edges are firm but the center is still wet -> continue baking 3 to 5 minutes -> the center should lose its shiny raw-batter wobble.*
     - **Checkpoint:** the brownie slab is baked to moist crumbs, not a clean dry toothpick and not liquid batter.

5. Cool, chill, and cut for movie-night service
   - **Cool** the brownies completely in the pan on Wire racks for at least 1 hour, then chill 30 to 60 minutes if time allows; cooling and chilling help melted butter and chocolate set for cleaner cuts [3][7].
     - Lift the slab out with the parchment sling and place it on a cutting board.
     - **Cut** into a 4 by 4 grid with a 9-in chef knife, heating and wiping the knife dry between cuts [4][7].
     - Return the bars to room temperature before serving so the texture eats fudgy rather than cold and firm.
     - Taste-adjust loop: taste one edge trimming; if it tastes flat, add a tiny pinch of flaky salt or a light cocoa dusting; if the wine note tastes sharp, let the bars stand uncovered 10 minutes before covering; if the chocolate tastes muted from chilling, serve at room temperature.
     - **Checkpoint:** the bars are clean-edged, portionable, and ready for napkin-only self-service.

## Common Issues
- **Fork-only center:** usually underbaked, cut warm, or overloaded with wine -> bake to moist crumbs and cool completely -> the center should slice without smearing.
- **Burned edges with raw center:** often dark metal, glass, convection, or too-hot oven -> use the light metal Sheet / baking tray, 8x8 and verify oven behavior -> edges set before drying out [6].
- **Ragged cuts:** slab is warm or knife is dirty -> chill briefly and wipe the hot knife between cuts -> each bar releases with defined edges [7].

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 4: cool completely in the pan, cover, and refrigerate the uncut slab.
  - Holds: 1 day before cutting for best texture and clean edges.
  - Serve/Reheat: cut cold using the hot-wiped-knife method, then return to room temperature until fudgy and not refrigerator-firm.
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: after Step 5, once bars are fully cooled and cut.
  - Portioning: wrap bars in layers with parchment between them, then seal airtight.
  - Freeze: not specified by the provided sources; use room-temperature storage for the validated plan.
  - Thaw: thaw covered at room temperature until no longer cold in the center.
  - Reheat: not needed; these are designed as room-temperature bars.

## Troubleshooting
- Brownies taste sharply alcoholic -> too much free wine or not enough bake-off -> keep soak wine drained and batter wine capped at 1/4 cup -> flavor reads fruity instead of raw [2].
- Cherries sink or create wet pockets -> cherries were too wet -> drain free liquid before folding -> batter stays thick and evenly speckled.
- Bars smear when served -> cut warm or skipped knife wiping -> chill 30 to 60 minutes and use a hot wiped knife -> edges look clean and self-serve ready [7].
- Edges dry before center sets -> pan or oven running too hot -> use the light metal Sheet / baking tray, 8x8 and check at the early end -> center reaches moist-crumb doneness [5][6].

## Geometry Notes
- Pan/pot size used: Sheet / baking tray, 8x8, light metal with parchment sling is the preferred baking vessel [1][2].
- Target thickness / layer depth: one even brownie layer, cut into a 4 by 4 grid for 16 small bars.
- Crowding rule: keep bars in a single layer on the Sheet / baking tray, 13x9 serving tray so guests can pick them up cleanly.
- Pan fallback rule: Baking pan, 8x8, glass may need 5 to 10 extra minutes and longer cooling; Baking tray, 9x9, dark metal bakes faster and darker, so reduce oven temperature about 25 deg F or check 5 minutes early.

## Scaling & Batch Size
- Scaling notes: do not double into the same 8x8 pan; the center will set too slowly.
- Batch strategy: for more bars, bake separate 8x8 pans and rotate positions only if your oven heats unevenly.
- Pan swaps: a 9x9 metal pan will bake thinner and likely faster; an 8x8 needs more time than a 9x9 in similar port-cherry brownie guidance [3].

## Special Notes
- Storage & reheat: keep cut bars airtight at room temperature; no reheating is needed for movie-night service [5].
- Serving ideas: set the bars in a single layer on a covered Sheet / baking tray, 13x9 with napkins, uncover during the movie snack window, then recover any leftovers.
- Serve-window strategy: cut before guests arrive, hold covered at room temperature, and avoid glaze, ganache, whipped cream, or fresh fruit so the bars stay tidy for 45 to 90 minutes.

## Variations
1. **Reduced-wine cherry bars:** Reduce 1/2 cup red wine over medium-low heat until it measures 1/4 cup and smells fruity, then use that reduced wine as the total wine for the soak-and-batter path; this gives a deeper fruit note with less raw-wine edge [2].
2. **Dried cranberry red wine bars:** Swap the dried cherries for 1/2 cup dried cranberries and use the same warm-wine soak for a tarter version [2].
3. **Port-style cherry bars:** Soak dried cherries in port-style wine, then strain before folding into the batter; expect a sweeter, darker fruit profile [3].
4. **Dry cocoa finish:** Dust the cooled, cut bars lightly with cocoa powder instead of flaky salt for a no-smear finish.
