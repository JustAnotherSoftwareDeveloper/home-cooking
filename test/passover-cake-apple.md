## Passover Apple-Walnut Matzo Cake with Charoset Spice and Wine

### Title & Overview
This is the Arthur Schwartz-style Passover apple cake, kept dense, moist, and apple-layered, but nudged more explicitly toward charoset. The structure stays the same: a very thick matzo-based batter, two apple layers, and a sugared walnut topping. Since you have **matzo meal** rather than **matzo cake meal**, the key adjustment is to **grind it finer and sift it first** so it behaves more like cake meal. The flavor stays the same; the main risk is texture, so the recipe now builds that prep step in explicitly.

### Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 9 to 12 servings
- **Active Prep:** 30 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 4 hours 10 minutes
- **Cook:** 1 hour 10 minutes to 1 hour 20 minutes
- **Total:** 5 hours 50 minutes to 6 hours
- **Make-ahead:** Best baked 1 day ahead. Cool completely, cover once cool, and serve at room temperature the next day. Tightly wrapped slices can be frozen for up to 1 month.

### Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** egg, gluten, nuts, alcohol
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - walnuts -> pecans -> softer, sweeter nut profile -> method stays the same
  - raisins -> omit -> cleaner apple-cake profile -> less explicit charoset flavor
  - optional cloves -> omit -> more restrained spice profile
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - egg substitute -> weakens lift and structure too much -> choose a different Passover dessert rather than forcing this batter
  - non-gebrokts requirement -> not recommended -> this is a wet matzo dessert
- **Cross-contact note (optional):**
  - For actual Passover use, processed ingredients should be certified kosher for Passover. This is also a gebrokts dessert, which is acceptable at many tables but not all.

### Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** a dense, moist, apple-layered Passover cake with a walnut-spice top pushed toward charoset
- **Texture and doneness cues:** the batter should be **very thick**; the cake is done when the **sides pull slightly from the pan** and the topping has **begun to caramelize**
- **Workflow:** grind and sift the matzo meal first, plump the raisins with wine, mix the topping, whip the batter, then layer **batter -> topping -> apples** twice; **while the cake bakes, clear the workspace and set the wire rack for a full cool-down**
- **Make-ahead:** this cake improves after resting; day two is often better than day one
- **Primary risks:** under-whipping the eggs and sugar, leaving the matzo meal too coarse, slicing the apples too thick, letting excess wine into the pan, and judging doneness only by a toothpick

### Gather & Stage
- **Preheat** the **Standard oven** to **350 deg F** with a rack in the center position.
- Lightly oil the **Baking pan | 8×8 | Glass**.
- **Grind** the matzo meal in the **Food processor** until noticeably finer, then **sift** it.
  - Measure out **3/4 cup** of the finest portion for the batter.
  - If you come up short after sifting, regrind the coarse bits and sift again.
- In one bowl, combine the raisins and **1 tablespoon** sweet kosher red wine. Let them sit **10 minutes** so they plump slightly.
- In a second bowl, mix:
  - walnuts
  - **3/4 cup** sugar
  - cinnamon
  - ginger
  - allspice
  - nutmeg
  - optional pinch of cloves
- Peel, core, and slice the apples to **1/4-in thickness**.
- Measure the batter ingredients so they are ready to go in sequence.
- Set the **KitchenAid Stand Mixer with wire whisk attachment** in place and make sure the **Wire racks** are ready for a long cool-down.
- **Checkpoint:** pan greased, matzo meal ground and sifted, raisins plumped, topping mixed, apples sliced, batter ingredients measured, cooling rack ready.

### Instructions

#### 1. Prep the charoset-style elements
- **Mix** the walnut topping until the spices are evenly distributed through the sugar.
  - The aroma should read **cinnamon-first**, with the ginger, allspice, and nutmeg sitting behind it.
  - *If using cloves -> keep it to a tiny pinch -> cue it’s fixed: the blend still smells like apple-cinnamon, not clove-dominant spice cake.*
- **Check** the raisins after their 10-minute soak.
  - If the wine is absorbed, proceed as-is.
  - If a little wine remains pooled in the bowl, leave the excess behind; the raisins should be glossy, not wet.
- **Checkpoint:** the topping looks sandy and even, the raisins are plump but not dripping, and the apple slices are thin enough to lie down in layers.

#### 2. Make the batter
- In the **KitchenAid Stand Mixer with wire whisk attachment**, **beat** the eggs until well combined, then gradually add the batter sugar.
  - Use **medium to medium-high speed** for about **4 to 5 minutes**, until the mixture is pale, thick, and foamy.
  - With the mixer running, **stream in** the oil, then stop and scrape as needed.
- Remove the bowl from the mixer and **fold** in the **3/4 cup fine ground-and-sifted matzo meal** until fully blended.
  - The batter should be **very thick** and hold soft ridges rather than pour.
  - *If the batter seems suspiciously gritty -> your matzo meal is still too coarse -> regrind and sift a fresh portion -> cue it’s fixed: the batter feels thick and spreadable, not sandy.*
  - *If the batter seems suspiciously loose -> verify that you measured the sifted fine meal after grinding, not before -> cue it’s fixed: the batter is spreadable, not pourable.*
- **Checkpoint:** the batter falls in heavy dollops and does not flow like ordinary cake batter.

#### 3. Layer the cake
- **Spread** about half the batter into the prepared pan.
  - Do not expect it to level itself; use short pushes to make an even layer.
- **Sprinkle** about half the walnut-spice topping over the batter.
- **Arrange** half the apples over the topping.
  - Scatter the wine-plumped raisins over this layer.
- **Dollop** on the remaining batter and **spread** it carefully over the apples.
  - *If the second batter layer drags the apples around -> use short strokes with a lightly oiled utensil -> cue it’s fixed: the batter forms a patchy but even blanket over the apples instead of clumping.*
- **Arrange** the remaining apples on top, then **sprinkle** over the rest of the topping.
- **Checkpoint:** the middle apple layer is mostly covered by batter and the top is evenly shingled with apples and topping.

#### 4. Bake
- **Bake** on the center rack at **350 deg F** for **1 hour 10 minutes to 1 hour 20 minutes**, **uncovered**, until the topping has **begun to caramelize** and the sides pull slightly from the pan.
  - Start checking around **1 hour**.
  - A perfectly clean tester is not the best cue here because the cake is fruit-heavy and stays moist.
  - *If the top is browning too fast -> loosely tent with foil for the last 10 to 15 minutes -> cue it’s fixed: the top stops darkening aggressively while the center continues setting.*
  - *If using the **Baking tray | 9×9 | Metal | Dark** instead -> bake at **325 deg F** or begin checking about 5 minutes early -> cue it’s fixed: the topping caramelizes without the edges hardening first.*
- **Checkpoint:** no glossy raw batter is visible between the apple slices, the cake smells deeply spiced, and the center looks set rather than wet.

#### 5. Cool, rest, and taste-adjust
- **Cool** the cake completely in the pan on the **Wire racks**.
  - This should take **several hours**; for the best result, cover it only after fully cool and let it rest until the next day.
- **Slice** and serve at room temperature.
  - The top may still seem a little granular on day one.
  - By day two, it usually softens into a more moist, candy-like layer.
- Taste-adjust loop:
  - acid -> not usually needed in the cake itself; use unsweetened tea or coffee if the dessert reads heavy
  - salt -> do not add directly here; the balance comes from apples, spice, and portion size
  - heat -> skip; this is a warm-spice dessert, not a spicy one
  - herbs -> not relevant; instead, add a very light extra dusting of cinnamon only if the charoset note feels muted
- **Checkpoint:** slices lift cleanly, the topping looks settled rather than dusty, and the crumb no longer feels warm.

### Common Issues
- **Batter seems too thick:** this is normal -> spread in dollops rather than trying to pour it -> the layers will still bake correctly
- **Batter feels gritty:** matzo meal was not ground finely enough -> regrind and sift before mixing -> the batter should feel thick, not coarse
- **Toothpick stays wet:** expected for this style -> judge by slight side-pull and caramelized topping instead -> the cake should still look set
- **Top looks sandy right after baking:** common on day one -> cool fully and let it rest overnight -> the topping usually softens by the next day
- **Cake tastes less charoset-like than expected:** most often caused by omitting raisins or wine -> keep both and use walnuts rather than pecans -> the flavor reads more clearly Ashkenazi-charoset

### Make-Ahead Notes
- **Fridge plan:**
  - Stop after Step **5**: cool completely, then cover tightly
  - Holds: **3 to 4 days refrigerated** after cooling completely
  - Serve/Reheat: bring slices to room temperature for **30 to 45 minutes, until the crumb loses its chill**; then refresh with a very light cinnamon dusting only if the spice reads muted
- **Freezer plan:**
  - Best freeze point: after the cake is fully cool and sliced
  - Portioning: wrap slices tightly, then place in a freezer-safe bag or container
  - Freeze: up to **1 month**
  - Thaw: overnight in the refrigerator, still wrapped
  - Reheat: let thawed slices stand at room temperature for **30 to 45 minutes, until the topping softens and the crumb is no longer cold**; then add a few chopped walnuts if you want the texture reset toward crunch

### Troubleshooting
- Batter will not spread over the apples -> normal thickness plus uneven fruit layer -> use short spatula strokes and keep the apples level -> batter covers the fruit in a rough but even sheet
- Cake seems underdone at the short end of the range -> apples may be thicker than intended or your pan runs cooler -> give it 5 to 10 more minutes -> no glossy raw batter remains
- Raisin layer makes the cake feel wet -> too much unabsorbed wine went into the pan -> next time leave any puddled wine behind after plumping -> raisins taste winey without loosening the cake
- Spice profile tastes muddled -> cloves were too heavy or the topping was mixed unevenly -> reduce or omit the cloves and remix the sugar-spice blend -> cinnamon returns to the lead note
- Crumb is too rough -> matzo meal stayed too coarse -> grind finer and sift before measuring -> crumb becomes denser-smoother rather than pebbly

### Geometry Notes
- Pan/pot size used: **Baking pan | 8×8 | Glass**
- Target thickness / layer depth: two low apple layers inside a fairly thick square cake
- Crowding rule: keep the apples to about **5 cups** and **1/4-in-thick slices**; more fruit or thicker slices push the center toward wet rather than moist

### Special Notes
- This version is intentionally more charoset-coded than the base cake, but the structure remains the Arthur Schwartz cake family.
- The wine is kept restrained and applied to the raisin layer rather than poured into the batter, so the charoset note becomes clearer without compromising the bake.
- Because you are using **matzo meal**, the grind-and-sift step matters. Treat it as required, not optional.
- This is a **gebrokts** dessert. Many Passover tables are fine with that; some are not.

### Variations
1. **Cleaner apple-cake version:** Omit the raisins and wine, and replace the spice blend with **1 tablespoon cinnamon**. This pulls the dessert back toward the plain apple-cake version and away from explicit charoset flavor.

2. **Pecan version:** Replace the walnuts with pecans. The cake becomes rounder and slightly sweeter, but less recognizably Ashkenazi-charoset in flavor.

3. **Stronger charoset note:** Keep the walnuts, raisins, and wine, and do not omit the ginger or allspice. Those supporting spices are not the dominant Ashkenazi note, but they fit comfortably within broader apple-charoset practice.
