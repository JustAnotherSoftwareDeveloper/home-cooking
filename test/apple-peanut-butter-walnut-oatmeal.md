# Apple-Cinnamon Chia-Flax Oatmeal Pucks (Oat Milk, PB Powder, Walnut Meal)

## Title & Overview
This is a sweet, breakfast-style oatmeal cooked as one large batch, portioned into 1-cup freezer pucks, and reheated in the microwave into normal, spoonable oatmeal. Flavor is classic apple-cinnamon with a warm vanilla backbone, and the texture is thick-creamy (not bar-like) thanks to chia and ground flax. Macros are improved without any post-microwave steps by incorporating powdered peanut butter and walnut meal directly into the cooked oatmeal. It is make-ahead friendly because the batch is tuned to set up well as a puck and reheat evenly when covered.

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 10 pucks (1 cup each), plus up to 1 extra cup depending on apple size and reduction
- **Active Prep:** 15 to 20 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 25 to 45 minutes (cooling before freezing)
- **Cook:** 15 to 22 minutes
- **Total (not counting freeze solid):** 55 to 85 minutes
- **Make-ahead:** Freeze pucks up to 3 months for best quality. Reheat from frozen in the microwave, covered, until steaming hot throughout.

## Ingredients
- Unsweetened oat milk, 6 1/2 cups
- Honeycrisp apples, 2 large, peeled (optional) and diced small (about 3 cups, `1/4-in` dice) **[pre-prep]**
- Maple syrup, 1/2 cup
- Fine salt, 1/2 tsp (about 3 g)
- Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
- Vanilla extract, 2 tsp
- Powdered peanut butter (PB2-style), 3/4 cup
- Walnut meal (ground walnuts), 3/4 cup
- Old-fashioned rolled oats, 2 1/2 cups
- Chia seeds, 1/4 cup
- Ground flaxseed, 1/4 cup

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** peanuts (PB powder), tree nuts (walnuts)
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Honeycrisp -> Fuji or Granny Smith -> sweetness/tartness shifts, firmness stays good -> keep dice at `1/4-in`
  - Walnut meal -> almond meal -> milder flavor -> same amount, same step
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - Instant oats -> more likely to turn gluey after freeze-thaw -> if you must, reduce chia and flax by 1 tbsp each and shorten cook time
- **Diet toggles (optional; only if relevant):**
  - Lower sugar: reduce maple to 1/3 cup -> increase vanilla to 1 tbsp and cinnamon to 2 1/2 tsp to preserve perceived sweetness
  - Low-sodium: reduce salt to 1/4 tsp -> compensate with a bit more cinnamon/vanilla

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** Apple-cinnamon oatmeal that freezes into 1-cup pucks and reheats into normal oatmeal with no add-ins.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Cook until thick-creamy and spoonable; stop slightly looser than your ideal bowl because it tightens as it cools and after freezing.
- **Workflow:** Warm and perfume oat milk, whisk in powders, simmer oats with chia/flax, soften apples, rest briefly, then portion and freeze.
- **Make-ahead:** Cool until warm (not hot), portion into silicone molds, freeze solid, then bag to prevent freezer burn.
- **Reheat plan:** Microwave 1 puck covered until steaming hot throughout; rest covered 60 seconds to equalize heat; eat as-is.
- **Primary risks:** Scorching oat milk, clumpy powders, and reducing too far (pucks reheat too stiff or heat unevenly).

## Instructions

1. Flavor the base and start softening the apples
   - **Warm** the aromatics in the oat milk.
     - Add to the pot: oat milk, maple syrup, salt, cinnamon, and vanilla.
     - Cook on **medium heat** for 3 to 6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until steaming and fragrant (avoid a hard boil).
     - *While this warms: dice apples to `1/4-in` so they soften evenly in the puck.*
     - **Checkpoint:** The liquid is steaming and cinnamon-forward, and the pot bottom is clean when you scrape it.
     - *If the bottom starts catching -> drop to **medium-low heat** and stir more often -> catching stops and the aroma stays sweet, not toasty.*

2. Whisk in the macro builders (no clumps)
   - **Whisk** in powdered peanut butter and walnut meal until fully smooth.
     - Reduce to **medium-low heat** (gentle steam, no aggressive bubbling).
     - Whisk in PB powder in 2 to 3 additions until smooth, then whisk in walnut meal.
     - **Checkpoint:** No visible dry pockets; the liquid looks uniform and slightly thicker.
     - *If you see PB clumps -> pull the pot off heat for 30 seconds and whisk hard -> mixture turns smooth and clumps disappear.*

3. Cook the oats, then add chia and flax
   - **Simmer** to a freezer-ready oatmeal texture.
     - Stir in rolled oats first.
     - Cook on **medium-low heat** for 4 to 6 minutes, stirring every 30 to 60 seconds, until oats begin to plump.
     - Stir in chia seeds and ground flaxseed.
     - Continue cooking on **medium-low heat** for 2 to 4 minutes, stirring frequently, until thick-creamy but still spoonable.
     - **Checkpoint:** Drag a spatula across the bottom; the trail fills in within about 1 to 2 seconds.

4. Apple finish and set the final texture
   - **Fold** in the apples and finish the cook.
     - Stir in diced apples and cook on **medium-low heat** for 3 to 6 minutes, until apples are tender-crisp (not hard, not mush).
     - **Rest** off heat for 3 to 5 minutes so chia/flax finish thickening evenly.
     - **Checkpoint:** Oatmeal is cohesive and creamy, and apples bite cleanly without crunching.

5. Cool, portion, freeze, and taste-adjust
   - **Cool** briefly, then **portion** into pucks and **freeze**.
     - Cool 20 to 40 minutes, stirring once or twice as it cools to prevent a thick top layer.
     - Portion into 10 x 1-cup silicone cavities (any extra becomes a bonus bowl).
     - Freeze flat until solid, then pop out and bag.
     - Taste-adjust loop (before filling the last mold): sweetness (maple) -> salt -> cinnamon -> vanilla.
     - **Checkpoint:** Pucks release cleanly and feel fully solid before bagging.

## Common Issues
- **Scorched flavor:** Heat too high or stirring too infrequent -> lower heat and scrape the bottom more often -> pot bottom stays clean.
- **Too stiff after freezing:** Reduced too far -> next batch stop the cook earlier (Step 3) or add 1/2 cup more oat milk -> reheats creamier.
- **Powder clumps:** Added too fast at too-high heat -> whisk in gradually on medium-low -> mixture turns smooth.

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 4: cool, portion into containers
  - Holds: 3 to 4 days
  - Serve/Reheat: microwave covered until steaming hot throughout
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: Step 5 (fully cooked oatmeal)
  - Portioning: 1 cup per puck (silicone molds on a sheet pan so they freeze level)
  - Freeze: up to 3 months (best quality)
  - Thaw: optional overnight in the fridge for faster reheating
  - Reheat: microwave covered until steaming hot throughout; rest covered 60 seconds

## Reheat Plan
- **Best method:** Microwave (covered) -> 3 to 5 minutes on High (from frozen, typical home microwaves) -> until the center is steaming hot throughout
- **Texture reset:** None required by design; rest covered 60 seconds so the puck relaxes into bowl-like oatmeal
- *If edges are hot but the center is cool -> microwave 45 to 90 seconds more (still covered) -> center is steaming hot.*

## Troubleshooting
- Hot edges, cool center -> puck is heating unevenly -> keep covered and extend in 45-second bursts -> center steams.
- Reheated texture feels overly stiff -> base was reduced too far -> next batch add 1/2 cup oat milk or stop Step 3 earlier -> reheats creamier.
- Apples still feel firm -> dice was too large -> cut to `1/4-in` -> apples soften evenly in Step 4 and after reheating.

## Troubleshooting Table

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Quick Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Scorched notes | Heat too high; not enough stirring | Use medium-low and scrape bottom every 30 to 60 seconds |
| Too stiff pucks | Over-reduced before freezing | Next batch add 1/2 cup oat milk or shorten simmer by 2 minutes |
| Powder clumps | Whisked in during aggressive bubbling | Lower heat and whisk in slowly in multiple additions |

## Geometry Notes
- Pan/pot size used: 4 to 6 qt heavy-bottom pot
- Target thickness / layer depth: thick-creamy but spoonable before the final rest
- Crowding rule: not applicable

## Scaling & Batch Size
- Scaling notes: Halve cleanly (5 pucks). Double only if you have an 8 qt pot and can stir frequently to prevent scorching.
- Batch strategy: For larger batches, reduce heat slightly and increase stirring frequency.
- Pan swaps: A wide Dutch oven reduces hot spots compared to a tall thin pot.
