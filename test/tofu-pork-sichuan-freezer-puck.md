# Home-Style Tofu, Rebuilt as a Spicy Crumbled Tofu-Pork Ragu (Low-Sodium Freezer Pucks)

## Title & Overview
This is a freezer-puck-friendly remix of Sichuan "home-style tofu" technique: build flavor with pork + aromatics + a light sauce, then cook until the tofu absorbs and the sauce clings. [1][2]
Instead of tofu slabs, you crumble extra-firm tofu into a meaty "ragu" texture that reheats evenly and portions cleanly into 1-cup silicone molds.
The "healthy" angle here is simple: lots of tofu + vegetables for volume and protein, and heat driven by chili + Sichuan peppercorn instead of salty pastes.
Doubanjiang is optional and added only at the end so you can keep sodium under control. [1][2]

## Detailed Instructions
Notes: You will build "heat" primarily from chili flakes + Sichuan peppercorn (no salt), then use a tiny amount of soy sauce at the end for roundness. [1][2]
- Sauce dial: use 1/4 cup water for a drier, ragu-like puck; 1/2 cup for saucier bowls.
- Salt dial: start with 2 tsp low-sodium soy; add doubanjiang only in small tastes at the end because sodium varies by brand.

1) Toast and grind the Sichuan pepper (if needed).
   Toast peppercorns in a dry skillet over medium heat 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant, then grind.

2) Dry-fry the crumbled tofu.
   Heat a wide skillet (12-inch preferred) over medium-high. Add 1 Tbsp oil, then add tofu in an even layer.
   Let it sit 2 to 3 minutes at a time before stirring so moisture can steam off. Cook 10 to 14 minutes total until noticeably drier, lightly browned in spots, and the curds hold shape.
   Transfer tofu to a bowl.

3) Brown the pork.
   Add remaining 1 Tbsp oil. Add pork, break into small crumbles, and cook 6 to 8 minutes until no longer pink with browned bits on the pan.

4) Bloom aromatics and spices.
   Reduce heat to medium. Add scallions (whites first), garlic, ginger, red pepper flakes, ground Sichuan pepper, and optional fresh chili.
   Stir constantly 30 to 45 seconds until very fragrant (do not scorch the garlic). [1][2]

5) Cook vegetables down.
   Add mushrooms, celery, and bell pepper. Cook 6 to 8 minutes, stirring frequently, until softened and most released liquid has cooked off.

6) Deglaze, sauce, and thicken.
   Add Shaoxing wine and scrape browned bits (about 30 seconds). [1][2]
   Add water, vinegar, and sugar, then add tofu back in and simmer 2 minutes.
   Stir slurry again, drizzle in while stirring, and cook 60 to 90 seconds until glossy and clingy.
   Turn off heat and season: add soy sauce. If using doubanjiang, add up to 1 tsp in small increments, tasting as you go. Finish with optional sesame oil.

7) Cool, portion, and freeze.
   Spread mixture in a thin layer on a sheet pan or large plate(s) to cool faster.
   Get it into the fridge promptly and do not leave cooked food out more than 2 hours. [4]
   Once no longer steaming hot, pack into 1-cup molds (press firmly, level tops). Freeze solid, then pop out and store in freezer bags with a label/date.

8) Reheat.
   Microwave covered in rounds (for example 2:30, stir/break up, then 1:30 to 2:30 more) until piping hot throughout.
   Reheat leftovers to 165 deg F. [3]

- Make-Ahead Map: Steps 1 through 6 up to 3 days ahead (refrigerated); Step 7 freezes 2 to 3 months; Step 8 reheats from frozen.

## Special Notes & Variations
- Storage & Reheat:
  - Cool quickly and refrigerate promptly; do not leave cooked food out more than 2 hours. [4]
  - Reheat leftovers to 165 deg F; microwave covered and stir/rotate for even heating. [3]
  - For best texture, use frozen pucks within 2 to 3 months.
- Serving ideas:
  - Rice bowl: steamed rice + this puck + quick cucumber salad (vinegar + chili flakes).
  - Lettuce cups: spoon into butter lettuce with extra black vinegar.
- Dietary toggles:
  - Lower sodium (even further): use 1 tsp soy sauce (instead of 2 tsp) and skip doubanjiang; increase Sichuan pepper + chili flakes for intensity.
  - Low-FODMAP leaning (not strict): replace garlic with garlic-infused oil; use only scallion greens (skip whites).
  - Higher protein: increase pork to 1.5 lb, keep water at 1/2 cup, and add an extra 1 Tbsp cornstarch slurry if needed to keep it clingy.
