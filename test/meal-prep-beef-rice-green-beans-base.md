# Freezer Beef, Carrot, Green Bean, and Rice Base

## Title & Overview

This recipe produces approximately 14 freezer-ready meals, each containing 1 cup of ghee-seasoned long-grain white rice and 1 cup of a beef, carrot, and green-bean mixture. The rice is pressure-cooked, briefly dried in the oven, cooled, and frozen so the grains remain separate during reheating. Top round is cut into 1.5-inch chunks and pressure-cooked without browning for maximum ease, while the shredded carrots and frozen green beans are roasted in separate one-cycle oven runs.

The base is mildly seasoned and contains no onion, garlic, sauce, or aggressive spices. It can be eaten as-is or paired with different sauces and seasoning blends after reheating.

## Yield & Timing

- **Yield:** Approximately 14 meals
  - 1 cup cooked rice per meal
  - 1 cup beef-carrot-green-bean mixture per meal
- **Active Prep:** 45 to 55 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 80 to 100 minutes
- **Cook:** Approximately 2 hours 15 minutes, including overlapping appliance runs
- **Total:** Approximately 2 hours 45 minutes to 3 hours
- **Make-ahead:** Refrigerate 3 to 4 portions for near-term use and freeze the remaining portions for up to 3 months for best quality.

## Allergy & Dietary Notes

- **Contains:** Milk, through the ghee.
- **Easy swaps:**
  - Ghee -> 2 tbsp avocado oil -> less buttery flavor, with no change to the pressure-cooking method.
  - Beef cooking water -> onion- and garlic-free unsalted beef stock -> stronger beef flavor, with potentially more sodium.
- **Hard swaps:**
  - White rice -> brown rice -> requires different water, pressure time, and release timing.
  - Top round -> chuck roast -> produces a fattier and more naturally succulent result, with a different cooked yield.
- **Gluten-free:** As written, assuming the ghee and any serving sauces are verified gluten-free.
- **Lower-sodium:** Reduce the rice salt to 6 g and the beef salt to 5 g. Add salt to individual portions after reheating if necessary.
- **GI-oriented design:** Uses white rice, fully cooked vegetables, controlled fat, and no onion or garlic.

## Quick Overview

- **What you are making:** Approximately 14 freezer bowls containing 1 cup rice and 1 cup beef-and-vegetable mixture.
- **Instant Pot order:** Cook the rice first, remove it, rinse the inner pot, and then pressure-cook the beef.
- **Oven order:** Dry the rice at 200 deg F, roast the carrots at 425 deg F, and then roast the frozen green beans at 425 deg F.
- **Rice endpoint:** Fully hydrated, separate grains with a dry surface but a soft center.
- **Beef endpoint:** The chunks collapse into coarse fibers under light fork pressure.
- **Vegetable endpoint:** Tender and surface-dry with scattered browning, but not crisp or dehydrated.
- **Primary risks:** Exceeding the Instant Pot 1/2 line, retaining too much rinse water, undercooking the beef, crowding the vegetables, or over-drying the rice.

## Gather & Stage

- Measure all water, ghee, oil, and salt by component.
- Trim the top round and cut it into uniform 1.5-inch chunks.
- Set out both metal trays.
- Position oven racks in the upper and lower thirds.
- Set out the 14 meal-prep containers.
- Keep the frozen green beans in the freezer until their oven run begins.
- **Checkpoint:** The beef is cut, all ingredients are measured, and both appliances are ready.

## Instructions

1. **Rinse and pressure-cook the rice**
   - Place the rice in a fine-mesh strainer or large bowl.
     - **Rinse** under cool water for 2 to 3 minutes, or through several changes of water, until the runoff is mostly clear.
     - Drain thoroughly for **5 minutes** so retained rinse water does not materially change the 1:1 cooking ratio.
   - Add the drained rice, 5 cups cold water, 2 tbsp ghee, and 9 g salt to the Instant Pot.
     - Stir once to distribute the ghee and salt.
     - Level the rice without pressing or compacting it.
     - Verify that the contents remain below the Instant Pot's `1/2` fill line. Rice and other expanding foods must not exceed that line [1].
   - Lock the lid and set the valve to Sealing.
     - **Pressure Cook on High** for 4 minutes.
     - Allow a **10-minute natural release**.
     - Vent the remaining pressure and open the lid [2].
   - **Checkpoint:** The grains are fully hydrated and soft but still hold their shape.
   - *If the rice looks wet on top -> fluff it and continue to the drying step -> the excess surface moisture should dissipate on the trays.*
   - *If the contents exceed the 1/2 line before cooking -> remove part of the batch -> the liquid and rice sit clearly below the line.*

2. **Start the beef and spread the rice**
   - Set the Standard oven to **200 deg F**.
   - **Fluff** the rice immediately from the bottom of the pot.
     - Separate compressed sections without crushing the grains.
     - Divide the rice evenly between the two metal trays, approximately 7 cups per tray.
     - Spread it loosely, ideally no deeper than approximately 1/2 inch.
   - Rinse the Instant Pot inner pot to remove loose rice starch.
   - Add 1 1/2 cups cold water, the 3 lb beef chunks, and 8 g salt.
     - Stir once to distribute the salt.
     - No oil, browning, or saute step is used.
   - Lock the lid and set the valve to Sealing.
     - **Pressure Cook on High** for 40 minutes.
     - Allow a **full natural release**, generally 18 to 25 minutes.
   - The extended cycle is intentional because the target is fall-apart top round rather than ordinary stew-meat tenderness [3][4].
   - **Checkpoint:** The beef is pressure-cooking while the rice moves through drying and cooling.

3. **Dry and cool the rice**
   - Place both rice trays in the **200 deg F** oven.
     - **Dry** for 5 minutes.
     - Gently turn the rice with a spatula.
     - Swap the trays between racks and rotate them front to back.
     - Continue drying for another 2 to 5 minutes.
   - Remove the rice when:
     - visible steam has substantially decreased,
     - the grains separate when turned,
     - the surface feels dry rather than tacky,
     - and the centers remain soft when tasted.
   - Do not wait for browning, crisp edges, or hard grains.
   - Leave the rice spread across the trays for **15 to 20 minutes**.
     - Turn it once after approximately 10 minutes.
     - Measure 1 cup rice into each of the 14 containers.
     - Refrigerate the loosely covered containers while the remaining components cook.
   - **Checkpoint:** Approximately 14 containers each hold 1 cup rice.
   - *If the rice becomes firm or develops dry edges -> stop drying immediately -> the grains should bend and compress rather than snap.*
   - *If the finished rice yield is more than 14 cups -> freeze the excess separately -> do not overfill the meal containers.*

4. **Roast the shredded carrots in one oven run**
   - Increase the oven to **425 deg F**.
   - Toss all 30 oz shredded carrots with 2 tbsp avocado oil and 3 g salt.
     - Divide evenly between the two trays, approximately 15 oz per tray.
     - Break apart compressed clumps.
     - Spread the carrots into loose, shallow layers.
   - **Roast at 425 deg F for 10 minutes**.
     - Toss thoroughly.
     - Bring darker outside strands toward the center.
     - Swap the trays between racks and rotate them front to back.
     - Roast for another **6 to 10 minutes**.
   - Stop when the carrots are:
     - fully tender,
     - visibly reduced,
     - dry on the surface,
     - and lightly browned in scattered areas.
   - Do not roast until broadly crisp; thin carrot strands can become dry after freezing and reheating [5].
   - Transfer the carrots to a large mixing bowl and leave uncovered briefly so loose steam can escape.
   - **Checkpoint:** The carrots are tender, distinct strands rather than crispy shreds.
   - *If the carrots remain wet and pale -> spread them more evenly and roast another 3 to 5 minutes -> steam stops rising from the center of the trays.*
   - *If the tips darken before the centers soften -> reduce the oven to 400 deg F and toss -> the strands soften without further scorching.*

5. **Roast the frozen green beans in one oven run**
   - Keep the oven at **425 deg F**.
   - Toss the frozen green beans directly from the freezer with 1 3/4 tbsp avocado oil and 3 g salt.
     - Do not thaw or rinse them.
     - Divide evenly between the two trays, approximately 14 oz per tray.
     - Break apart frozen clumps.
     - Spread into loose, shallow layers.
   - **Roast at 425 deg F for 15 minutes**.
     - Toss the beans.
     - Swap the trays between racks and rotate them front to back.
     - Roast for another **5 to 10 minutes** [6].
   - Remove the beans when:
     - they are completely hot and tender,
     - no standing water remains,
     - their surfaces look dry,
     - and scattered edges are browned or wrinkled.
   - Transfer the beans to a clean container and leave uncovered briefly.
   - **Checkpoint:** The beans are cooked through and intact, with no pooled water.
   - *If liquid remains on either tray -> spread the beans apart and roast another 3 to 5 minutes -> the tray remains dry when tilted.*
   - *If the beans begin collapsing or becoming heavily wrinkled -> remove them immediately -> they remain intact when stirred.*

6. **Test and finish the beef**
   - Once the Instant Pot has fully depressurized, open the lid.
   - Remove one beef chunk and press it with a fork.
     - Twist gently.
     - The chunk should collapse into coarse fibers with minimal resistance.
   - *If the beef remains springy or resists shredding -> return it to the pot, Pressure Cook on High for 8 additional minutes, and natural-release for 10 minutes -> the fibers separate under light fork pressure.*
   - Transfer the beef to a wide container.
     - Coarsely shred or crush it with forks.
     - Add up to 1/4 cup of the cooking liquid and toss.
     - Add another 1/4 cup only if the beef still looks dry.
     - Drain off any free liquid before measuring.
   - Top round can become fall-apart tender, but it will remain leaner and less gelatinous than chuck [3][4].
   - **Checkpoint:** The beef is fall-apart tender and moist without pooled liquid.

7. **Build the 14-cup beef and vegetable mixture**
   - Measure the cooked components by volume:
     - **5.6 cups cooked beef**
     - **4.2 cups roasted shredded carrots**
     - **4.2 cups roasted green beans**
   - For easier household measurement, use:
     - **5 1/2 cups plus 1 tbsp beef**
     - **4 cups plus 3 tbsp carrots**
     - **4 cups plus 3 tbsp green beans**
   - Fold the components together gently.
     - Do not mash the carrots or beans.
     - Check the bottom of the bowl for free liquid.
   - The mixture should total approximately **14 cups** at the intended 40% beef, 30% carrots, and 30% green-bean ratio.
   - **Checkpoint:** The mixture is evenly distributed and contains no pooled liquid.
   - *If one component is short -> let that component determine the number of complete portions -> reserve excess rice or other components separately rather than distorting the mixture.*

8. **Assemble, cool, and taste-adjust**
   - Add **1 cup of the beef and vegetable mixture** to each container holding 1 cup rice.
     - Keep the rice and mixture side by side rather than compressing them together.
     - Leave the lids loose or vented until visible steam has stopped.
   - Taste one spoonful containing rice, beef, carrots, and green beans.
     - **Acid:** Keep out of the neutral base; add when serving.
     - **Salt:** Add only if the complete spoonful tastes clearly underseasoned.
     - **Heat:** Keep out of the base; add chile or sauce after reheating.
     - **Herbs:** Add fresh herbs at serving rather than before freezing.
   - Refrigerate 3 to 4 portions for near-term use.
   - Freeze the remaining portions once chilled.
   - Cooked food should be cooled promptly in shallow containers and refrigerated within 2 hours [7].
   - **Checkpoint:** Approximately 14 containers each hold 1 cup rice and 1 cup mixture.

## Common Issues

- **Rice is gummy:** Residual rinse water altered the effective ratio -> drain for the full 5 minutes -> grains separate after fluffing and drying.
- **Rice is hard after freezing:** It was over-dried -> stop once surface tackiness disappears -> grains remain soft before cooling.
- **Beef is chewy:** It has not tenderized sufficiently -> use the 8-minute corrective pressure cycle -> chunks collapse under light fork pressure.
- **Vegetables are watery:** Trays were crowded or the green beans thawed -> spread more thinly and continue roasting uncovered -> no liquid remains when a tray is tilted.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- **Fridge plan:**
  - Refrigerate 3 to 4 portions after cooling.
  - Holds: 3 to 4 days.
  - Reheat: Microwave until steaming throughout and reaching 165 deg F.
- **Freezer plan:**
  - Best freeze point: Fully assembled and chilled.
  - Portioning: One approximately 2-cup meal per container.
  - Freeze: Up to 3 months for best texture.
  - Thaw: Overnight in the refrigerator, or reheat directly from frozen.
  - Reheat: Add water before heating to replace moisture lost by the rice and lean beef.

## Reheat Plan

- **Best method from refrigerated:**
  - Add 1 tbsp water over the rice.
  - Cover loosely.
  - Microwave at 70% power for 2 minutes.
  - Stir the rice and mixture.
  - Continue at full power in 30- to 45-second intervals until steaming throughout and reaching 165 deg F [7].
- **From frozen:**
  - Vent the lid.
  - Microwave at 50% power for 4 to 6 minutes.
  - Break apart softened sections.
  - Add 1 to 2 tbsp water.
  - Continue at full power for 2 to 4 minutes, stirring at least once.
- **Texture reset:** Rest covered for 1 minute, then fluff the rice and stir the mixture.
- *If the beef or rice remains dry -> add 1 tbsp water, cover, and microwave another 30 seconds -> the surface appears moist rather than chalky.*

## Troubleshooting

- Rice exceeds the Instant Pot 1/2 line -> actual pot geometry or measurement differs -> remove part of the batch -> contents sit clearly below the line.
- Beef remains intact when pressed -> insufficient pressure tenderization -> pressure-cook 8 minutes longer -> chunks separate under light fork pressure.
- Carrots are crispy and dry -> roasted beyond the tender endpoint -> mix with a small amount of beef liquid -> strands soften without becoming wet.
- Green beans are mushy -> thawed or over-roasted -> shorten the second roasting period next time -> beans remain intact when stirred.

## Scaling & Batch Size

- Five cups dry rice is the practical full batch used for this 6-quart workflow, but the actual contents must remain below the Instant Pot's marked 1/2 line [1].
- Expected yield after pressure cooking and brief oven drying is approximately 14 cups.
- Actual rice yield can vary with rice variety, drainage, measuring technique, and drying duration.
- If the rice yield exceeds 14 cups, freeze the excess separately.
- If the finished beef and vegetable mixture is below 14 cups, produce fewer complete meals rather than reducing the amount of mixture per portion.

## Special Notes

- **Top sodium drivers:**
  - Added salt
  - Salted ghee, if used
  - Any sauce added after reheating
- **Sodium levers:**
  - Use unsalted ghee.
  - Reduce the rice and beef salt before cooking.
  - Keep sauces as measured serving-time additions.
  - Use herbs, acid, or chile before adding more salt.

## Variations

1. **All-avocado-oil rice:** Replace the ghee with 2 tbsp avocado oil. The rice remains rich and separate but loses the buttery flavor.

2. **Unsalted-stock beef:** Replace the beef water with onion- and garlic-free unsalted beef stock. This adds savory depth without changing the pressure time.

3. **Components kept separate:** Do not combine the beef and vegetables. Add approximately 0.4 cup beef, 0.3 cup carrots, and 0.3 cup green beans to each container.

## Safety & Correctness Notes

- The rice oven step is deliberately brief. It removes surface moisture but is not intended to dehydrate the grains.
- Top round is leaner than chuck. It can become fall-apart tender, but its fibers remain less succulent and gelatinous.
- Cool rice and assembled meals promptly in shallow containers, refrigerate within 2 hours, and reheat leftovers to 165 deg F [7].
