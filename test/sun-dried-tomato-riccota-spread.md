# Sun-Dried Tomato, Basil, and Walnut Ricotta Spread With Balsamic Glaze

## Title & Overview
This is a tomato-forward ricotta spread meant for sourdough toast: oil-packed sun-dried tomatoes for concentrated flavor, toasted walnuts for richness, and a sweet-tangy balsamic glaze finish that reads clearly through cold dairy. It eats like a savory, slightly sweet Italian-inspired dip with distinct tomato bits rather than a bland ricotta base. It is generally "healthy" in the practical sense that it is protein-forward and uses nuts for satisfying fats, while keeping the flavor impact high without relying on heavy cheeses. It is also make-ahead friendly: the flavor improves after a rest, and you can keep the glaze separate for easy per-slice tuning.

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 12 servings (about 2 to 3 tbsp each)
- **Active Prep:** 20 to 30 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 4 hours to overnight (recommended)
- **Cook:** 7 to 9 minutes (walnuts) + 12 to 18 minutes (balsamic glaze)
- **Total:** 40 to 55 minutes (+ rest)
- **Make-ahead:** Spread improves overnight; treat storage like ricotta (follow the tub date and quality cues). Glaze can be made ahead and kept refrigerated.

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** dairy, tree nuts (walnuts)
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Walnuts -> toasted almonds -> slightly cleaner, less-bitter nut note; toast time may be 1 to 2 minutes shorter [5].
  - Food processor method -> bowl method -> chunkier texture; flavors still work.
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - Part-skim ricotta -> can get pasty; if you must, add 1 to 2 tsp reserved tomato oil and rest longer.
- **Diet toggles (optional; only if relevant to you):**
  - Low-sodium: drain well, optionally quick-rinse then pat dry; do not add salt; rely on balsamic drizzle and black pepper to lift flavor.
  - Low-FODMAP: omit garlic powder or replace with garlic-infused oil (keep amounts small).

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** a tomato-forward ricotta spread with toasted walnuts and a balsamic glaze finish.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** walnuts are ready when fragrant and just deeper golden; glaze is ready when it coats the back of a spoon and slowly drips [4][5].
- **Workflow:** toast walnuts -> reduce balsamic -> drain/chop tomatoes while glaze cools -> mix -> rest.
- **Make-ahead:** spread tastes better after 4+ hours; glaze keeps separately and lets you tune each serving [4].
- **Reheat plan:** no reheat for the spread; toast bread fresh and spread cold (or let spread sit 10 minutes to soften).
- **Primary risks:** salty tomatoes (add salt last), over-reducing glaze, over-toasting walnuts.

## Instructions

1. Toast the walnuts (oven)
   - **Bake** walnuts on a sheet pan in a single layer.
     - Heat oven to **350 deg F**.
     - **Bake** 7 to 9 minutes, stirring once halfway, until **fragrant and lightly golden** [5].
     - Cool completely, then chop.
     - *While the walnuts toast: set up the saucepan for the balsamic glaze.*
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready to proceed when the walnuts smell nutty (not sharp) and are no longer pale [5].
     - *If they smell acrid or look very dark -> pull immediately and cool on a different surface -> they are salvaged if they taste pleasantly bitter, not burnt.*

2. Make balsamic glaze (homemade)
   - **Reduce** balsamic vinegar into a glaze.
     - Add 1 cup balsamic vinegar to a small saucepan.
     - Bring to a lively simmer over **medium heat**, then lower to **medium-low** so it gently bubbles.
     - **Simmer** 12 to 18 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it **coats the back of a spoon** and drips in slow ribbons [4].
     - Cool fully (it thickens more as it cools) [4].
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready when a spoon dragged through the glaze leaves a brief trail before it closes [4].
     - *If you over-reduce and it turns syrupy-candy thick -> warm gently and whisk in 1 tsp water at a time -> it should return to slow-ribbon consistency [4].*

3. Prep the oil-packed sun-dried tomatoes
   - **Drain** and chop for even distribution.
     - Drain tomatoes in a fine-mesh strainer; press lightly to shed excess oil.
     - Pat briefly with paper towels to avoid an oily, slick mouthfeel.
     - Chop to small, lentil-size pieces.
     - *While the glaze cools: chop tomatoes and walnuts.*
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready when tomatoes are chopped small and not dripping oil.

4. Build the ricotta base and mix
   - **Mix** ricotta with the flavor builders, then fold in texture.
     - In a bowl, combine ricotta, tomato paste, dried basil, oregano, garlic powder, black pepper, and red pepper flakes (if using).
     - Stir until there are no streaks of tomato paste.
     - Stir in 2 tsp balsamic glaze (from Step 2) [4].
     - Fold in chopped tomatoes and most of the walnuts (reserve a spoonful of walnuts for topping if you want crunch).
     - Optional depth technique: in a small skillet, warm 1 tsp reserved tomato oil over **medium-low** and **saute** tomato paste 1 to 2 minutes until darker; cool and mix in (skip if you want maximum simplicity) [1].
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready when the spread is cohesive and tomato is evenly distributed.

5. Rest, then finish and taste-adjust
   - **Chill** and adjust in a specific order.
     - Cover and **rest** in the fridge 4 hours minimum (overnight preferred).
     - Taste-adjust loop: add acid (more glaze) -> then salt (only if needed) -> then heat (flakes) -> then herbs (basil/oregano).
     - **Checkpoint:** Final spread tastes clearly tomato-forward, and the balsamic reads as a clean lift, not sweetness.
     - *If it tastes too salty -> stir in 2 to 4 tbsp more ricotta and rest 30 minutes -> salt edge should soften.*
     - *If it tastes flat but not salty -> add 1 tsp glaze and 1/8 tsp black pepper -> it should pop without needing salt [4].*

## Common Issues
- **Too salty:** oil-packed tomatoes vary -> add more ricotta and rely on balsamic drizzle -> check that you did not add salt before resting.
- **Walnuts softened:** expected if mixed in early -> reserve topping walnuts and add at serving.
- **Glaze too thick:** reduced too far -> warm and thin with water a teaspoon at a time [4].
- **Glaze too thin:** under-reduced -> simmer a few minutes longer -> it should coat a spoon [4].

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 4: cover airtight and refrigerate.
  - Holds: treat like ricotta; quality is best early and follow the container date/quality cues.
  - Serve: stir once, then top with fresh walnuts and a drizzle of glaze.
- Freezer plan:
  - Spread: not recommended (texture can go grainy after thaw).
  - Components: freeze extra toasted walnuts and keep glaze refrigerated.

## Reheat Plan
- **Best method:** Toast bread -> spread cold ricotta mixture -> drizzle glaze -> until the toast is crisp and the spread is cool-creamy.
- **Texture reset:** If the spread is stiff from the fridge, let it sit 10 minutes, then stir -> it should become spreadable.
- *If the toast gets soggy -> toast longer and add glaze after spreading -> the finish stays crisp.*

## Troubleshooting
- Too salty -> tomatoes were saltier than expected -> add more ricotta and rest -> salt edge softens.
- Too sweet -> glaze was heavy-handed -> reduce glaze inside the spread, use black pepper and chili to balance -> sweetness becomes background.
- Too oily -> not drained/patted enough -> fold in more ricotta and chill -> oiliness reduces.
- Bland after resting -> not enough acid/pepper -> add 1 tsp glaze and 1/8 tsp pepper -> flavor lifts quickly [4].

## Geometry Notes

- Target reduction: glaze should be spoon-coating and ribboning (not watery, not candy-thick) [4].

## Variations
1. **Almond swap:** Use toasted chopped almonds instead of walnuts for a cleaner nut flavor; keep the balsamic drizzle for contrast and reserve nuts for topping if you want crunch [5].
2. **Spicy-sweet:** Increase red pepper flakes and keep glaze as a finishing drizzle only; it eats more like a spicy tomato dip on toast.
3. **Herb-forward (no fresh herbs):** Double dried basil and slightly increase oregano; rest overnight so the dried herbs hydrate and read clearly through the ricotta [2].
