# Poor-Man’s Strawberry Shortcake Plates

## Title & Overview

This is the stripped-down version: **store-bought frozen pound cake**, the same **jammy strawberry component**, and **store-bought whipped cream**, served simply on a plate. It keeps the part that matters most—the strawberry topping—but drops the scratch cake, mascarpone cream, and cup assembly. The result is closer to a classic plated strawberry shortcake: a regular slice of pound cake, spooned-over strawberries, and a generous cloud of whipped cream. It is easy to make ahead because the strawberries improve after chilling and the final assembly takes only a minute per plate.

## Ingredients

### Jammy roasted frozen strawberries

- 2 lb frozen sliced strawberries, unsweetened preferred
- 3 tbsp granulated sugar, plus 1–2 tbsp more only if needed
- 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar
- 1/4 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or a pinch plus 1/16 tsp Morton kosher salt
- 1 lemon, zested; reserve 1–2 tsp juice for final adjustment
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

### Assembly

- 1 frozen store-bought pound cake loaf, about 10.75–16 oz, thawed enough to slice cleanly **[pre-prep]**
- 1 container store-bought whipped topping, thawed, or 1 can store-bought whipped cream **[pre-prep]**
- Chilled jammy roasted frozen strawberries **[pre-prep]**

## Allergy & Dietary Notes

- **Contains:** dairy, wheat/gluten, eggs.
- **Easy swaps keep technique intact:**
  - Balsamic vinegar -> lemon juice -> brighter strawberry flavor, less roasted depth.
  - Tub whipped topping -> canned whipped cream -> easier last-minute service, but less stable if plated early.
  - Pound cake loaf -> pound cake slices -> same dessert, faster portioning.
- **Hard swaps not recommended:**
  - Angel food cake -> too airy and gets soggy faster.
  - Fresh whole strawberries -> adds hulling and changes the soft, jammy topping you wanted to keep.
- **Cross-contact note:** Store-bought pound cake and whipped topping may contain stabilizers or shared-facility allergens depending on brand.

## Quick Overview

- **What you are making:** A plated shortcut strawberry shortcake with pound cake slices, jammy roasted strawberries, and store-bought whipped cream.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** The strawberries should be glossy and spoonable, not watery; the pound cake should slice cleanly and stay intact under the topping.
- **Workflow:** Roast and chill the strawberries first; while they chill, thaw the pound cake and whipped topping; then slice, plate, and top.
- **Make-ahead:** The strawberries can be made up to 3 days ahead and are the only cooked component.
- **Primary risks:** Watery strawberries, too much syrup soaking the cake, or assembling too far ahead.

## Gather & Stage

- Preheat the Standard oven to **375 deg F**.
- Set the frozen pound cake out to thaw according to package guidance, or thaw overnight in the refrigerator.
- If using tub whipped topping, thaw it in the refrigerator until scoopable.
- Set out the Baking pan — 13×9 glass and a spoon.
- **Checkpoint:** The pound cake is thawing, whipped topping is readying, and the oven is heated.

## Instructions

1. Roast the strawberry component  
   - **Roast** the frozen strawberries until the liquid reduces into a loose, glossy topping.
     - Add frozen strawberries, granulated sugar, balsamic vinegar, kosher salt, and lemon zest to the Baking pan — 13×9 glass.
     - Spread the berries into as even a layer as possible.
     - **Bake at 375 deg F** for 50–70 minutes, stirring every 15–20 minutes, until the berries collapse and the liquid changes from watery to syrupy.
     - Stir in the vanilla extract after roasting.
     - **Checkpoint:** The strawberries are jammy and spoonable, not soupy.
     - *If the pan still looks watery -> roast 8–12 minutes longer uncovered -> cue it’s fixed: the syrup coats a spoon.*
     - *If the syrup gets too thick or sticky -> stir in 1–2 tsp lemon juice or warm water -> cue it’s fixed: it loosens back into a spoonable topping.*

2. Chill and adjust the strawberries  
   - **Cool** the strawberry topping fully before serving.
     - Let the pan cool 10–15 minutes.
     - Transfer the strawberries to a bowl or shallow container.
     - Mash about one-third of the berries with a spoon, leaving the rest soft but visible.
     - **Chill** at least 1 hour, until cold.
     - Taste-adjust loop: acid -> salt -> sweetness -> vanilla.
       - Add a little lemon juice if the flavor tastes flat.
       - Add a pinch of salt if it tastes sweet but dull.
       - Add 1–2 tbsp more sugar only if it tastes too tart.
     - **Checkpoint:** The topping is cold, jammy, and thick enough to sit on cake without running everywhere.
     - *If the chilled berries separate into fruit and thin juice -> stir well and spoon off a little excess liquid -> cue it’s fixed: the topping clings together.*

3. Slice the pound cake  
   - **Slice** the thawed pound cake normally for plating.
     - Use a 9-in chef knife or serrated knife.
     - Cut slices about `1/2-in` to `3/4-in` thick.
     - Plan on 1 slice per serving.
     - *While this runs: set out plates and the whipped topping.*
     - **Checkpoint:** The cake slices are clean and intact, not crumbling apart.
     - *If the cake is still too firm to slice cleanly -> let it sit 15–20 minutes longer -> cue it’s fixed: the knife passes through without tearing.*

4. Plate and serve  
   - **Assemble** each dessert plate just before serving.
     - Place 1 slice of pound cake on each plate.
     - Spoon 2–3 tbsp jammy strawberries over the top and slightly around the slice.
     - Add a generous scoop or swirl of store-bought whipped topping or whipped cream.
     - Serve immediately, or hold plated desserts in the refrigerator for no more than about 30 minutes.
     - Taste-adjust loop: acid -> salt -> sweetness -> vanilla. If needed, adjust only the remaining strawberry topping, not plated servings.
     - **Checkpoint:** Final plates have a visible cake slice, a spoonable strawberry topping, and whipped cream that still looks fluffy.

## Common Issues

- **Soggy cake:** Too much syrup -> use more fruit solids and less liquid -> cake still looks like a slice, not mush.
- **Flat strawberry flavor:** Needs acid or salt -> add lemon juice or a tiny pinch of salt -> topping tastes brighter.
- **Messy plating:** Cake was too warm or too soft -> chill or thaw more evenly -> slices hold their shape better.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 2: Cool and refrigerate the strawberry topping in a covered container.
  - Holds: 3 days.
  - Stop after Step 3: Pound cake can be thawed and sliced, then wrapped well.
  - Holds: 1 day refrigerated.
  - Serve: assemble individual plates just before serving.

- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: strawberry topping.
  - Portioning: freeze in a small airtight container.
  - Freeze: up to 3 months.
  - Thaw: overnight in the refrigerator.
  - Reheat or reset: serve cold, or warm slightly and then rechill if you want a looser texture.

## Troubleshooting

- Strawberry topping too loose -> insufficient reduction -> roast longer uncovered -> syrup coats a spoon.
- Pound cake tearing while slicing -> not thawed enough or too cold in the center -> wait 15–20 minutes -> slices come away cleanly.
- Whipped topping sliding off -> plated too far ahead or strawberries too warm -> chill strawberries fully and top at the last minute -> cream stays fluffy.

## Geometry Notes

- Pan size used: 13×9 pan for strawberries.
- Target strawberry layer depth: shallow and even, so the berries roast instead of steaming.
- Pound cake slice thickness: `1/2-in` to `3/4-in`.
- Crowding rule: Do not pile 2 lb strawberries into a much smaller pan; they need room to reduce.

## Scaling & Batch Size

- Scaling notes:
  - Half batch: use 1 lb strawberries and half the sugar/vinegar.
  - Double batch: use two pans for the strawberries.
- Batch strategy:
  - Keep strawberries separate and plate to order.
  - Slice only as much pound cake as you expect to serve soon.
- Pan swaps:
  - Metal pan: check 5–10 minutes earlier because the syrup may reduce faster.

## Special Notes

- Serving ideas: This is best served cold or cool, straight from the fridge, with the cake at cool room temp or lightly chilled.
- Shortcut logic: The only homemade part here is the strawberries, which keeps the dessert feeling intentional instead of fully store-bought.
- Storage & service: Assembled plates are best eaten right away; the components hold better separately [4].

## Variations

1. **Bowl version:** Tear or cube the pound cake into a shallow bowl, then spoon over strawberries and whipped topping. This is messier but even more casual and very easy for self-serve dessert.

2. **Warm strawberry version:** Serve the strawberry topping slightly warm over cool pound cake and whipped cream. The contrast is nice, but plate right before serving so the whipped cream does not melt too fast.

3. **Extra-cheap version:** Use canned whipped cream and pre-sliced frozen pound cake if that is what you have. The dessert will be a little less neat, but it still works if the strawberry topping is good.

## Safety & Correctness Notes

Frozen strawberries work well here because they are being cooked down into a topping rather than used for fresh texture. The pound cake and whipped topping should stay refrigerated if held for later service, and plated desserts are best assembled close to serving time [4].
