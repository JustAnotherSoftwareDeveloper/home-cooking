# Oven-Baked Shawarma Arayes (Meat-Stuffed Pita) with Tahini-Lemon Yogurt Spread
## Title & Overview
Arayes (and the closely related Egyptian hawawshi) are pita pockets stuffed with a thin layer of spiced ground meat and baked until crisp. This revision is tuned to avoid the “crisp outside, undercooked inside” failure mode by baking **lower and longer** on a **wire rack** for airflow, then finishing to a verified internal temperature of **160°F (71°C)** for ground beef safety.  
Key changes vs the prior draft: **1 lb** ground beef while keeping the **same spice load** (intentionally bold), **no breadcrumbs**, **no water in the sauce**, **no broil**, and a firm **375°F / 15 + 15** baseline with thermometer-driven increments.

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 3 to 4 servings (8 pita halves; ~2 halves per person)
- **Active Prep:** 25 to 35 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 5 minutes (rest before serving)
- **Cook:** 30 to 40 minutes (30 min baseline + finish-to-temp if needed)
- **Total:** 60 to 80 minutes
- **Make-ahead:** Filling holds 24 hours refrigerated; sauce holds 3 to 4 days refrigerated.

- Tomato paste, 2 1/2 Tbsp (2 Tbsp + 1 1/2 tsp)

- Black pepper, 1 tsp

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** Rack-baked arayes—pita pockets stuffed with a thin shawarma-tomato kofta layer, served with a bright tahini-lemon yogurt spread.
- **Primary doneness constraint:** Ground beef must reach **160°F (71°C)**; color is not reliable—use a thermometer.
- **Cook plan:** **375°F for 15 minutes, flip, then 15 minutes**; then finish by temperature in short increments if needed.
- **Grease control:** Wire rack + spray-only oil (no brushing) so drips fall away and the pita doesn’t fry in its own fat.

## Instructions

1. Preheat and set up the rack bake
   - **Preheat** oven to **375°F** with a rack in the middle.
   - Set a wire rack on a rimmed sheet pan.
   - Lightly **spray** the rack so pitas don’t stick.
   - **Checkpoint:** Oven is fully preheated and the rack-on-pan setup is ready.

2. Make the tahini-lemon yogurt spread (no water)
   - **Whisk** tahini and lemon juice until thick (it may seize—normal).
   - **Whisk in** yogurt until smooth, then whisk in garlic, zest, salt, and optional sumac.
   - If it’s too thick to spread, loosen with **additional lemon juice** (1 tsp at a time) or a spoon of yogurt (no water).
   - **Checkpoint:** Bright, thick-but-spreadable sauce; tastes “alive” (acid + salt).

3. Build the filling (no breadcrumbs)
   - **Grate** the onion, then squeeze very dry in paper towels (this matters for texture and cook-through).
   - In a bowl, **mix** beef, squeezed onion, garlic, parsley, tomato paste, shawarma spice mix, pepper, and salt.
     - Mix only until cohesive—don’t overwork.
   - **Test patty (recommended):** Sear 1 to 2 tsp of mixture 60 to 90 seconds total and taste.
     - If it tastes aggressively salty, your shawarma mix is likely salty—reduce added salt next time (keep spice mix unchanged).
   - **Checkpoint:** Filling holds together and spreads like soft putty (not watery).

4. Stuff the pitas and spray (no brushing)
   - Open each pita half pocket and **spread** filling in a thin, even layer to the edges.
     - Target thickness: **~1/4 inch**. Thicker layers dramatically increase cook time and risk undercooking.
   - Arrange on the wire rack with space between pieces.
   - Lightly **spray** the outside surfaces (spray-only; no brushing).
   - **Checkpoint:** Even thickness, no bulging “mounds,” surfaces are not glossy-wet with oil.

5. Bake at 375°F (no broil; lower + longer)
   - **Bake** at **375°F for 15 minutes**.
   - **Flip** every piece.
   - **Bake** another **15 minutes** (30 minutes total).
   - **Temperature verification (required for safety):**
     - Probe from the **cut edge** into the **thickest meat zone** (avoid the pita wall and any air pocket).
     - You are done at **160°F (71°C)**.
   - If below 160°F:
     - Continue baking in **3-minute increments**, checking again after each increment, until 160°F.
     - If the pita is as browned/crisp as you want before reaching temp, **tent loosely with foil** and continue the 3-minute increments.
   - **Checkpoint:** Thickest point reads 160°F; pita is crisp and golden (not scorched).

6. Rest and serve
   - Rest 2 to 3 minutes on the rack (vents steam; preserves crispness).
   - Serve with tahini-lemon yogurt spread and lemon wedges.

## Common Issues
- **Undercooked center:** Meat layer was thicker than ~1/4 inch, filling started cold, or probe hit an air pocket. Fix: keep the layer thin and finish by temp with 3-minute increments to 160°F.
- **Overly crisp pita before meat hits 160°F:** Tent loosely with foil once color is right; keep baking to 160°F.
- **Filling feels dry:** 93/7 + no binder can dry if overbaked. Fix: pull promptly at 160°F, keep the meat layer thin, and serve with sauce.

## Make-Ahead Notes
- **Filling:** Mix up to 24 hours ahead; store airtight and cold.
- **Sauce:** 3 to 4 days refrigerated.
- **Bake from cold filling:** Expect to need a few extra 3-minute increments after the 30-minute baseline.

## Troubleshooting
- **Thermometer reads low but meat looks done:** Color is not a reliable indicator for ground beef—re-probe in a second location and cook to 160°F.
- **Bottom feels less crisp:** Ensure the rack is not blocked and the pan isn’t overcrowded; airflow is the point of the rack.

## Geometry Notes
- Pan size: 13x18 rimmed sheet pan with wire rack insert
- Target thickness / layer depth: ~1/4 inch, evenly spread to edges
- Crowding rule: single layer with gaps; bake in batches if needed

## Scaling & Batch Size
- For thicker/meatier arayes: use **3 pitas instead of 4** (thicker layer), but expect longer cook time and rely on the 160°F check.
- For a party batch: use two pans/racks and rotate positions at the flip.

## Special Notes
- Avoid microwaving leftovers if you care about crunch; reheat on a rack in a hot oven.

## Variations
1. **Sumac-forward finish:** Dust hot arayes with sumac and serve with extra lemon wedges for brighter, more aromatic acidity.
2. **More “hawawshi” profile:** Add 1/2 cup finely minced green bell pepper to the filling (squeezed/dabbed dry); expect slightly longer cook time.
