# High-Fiber Walnut Berry Overnight Oats (Freezer Pucks)

## Title & Overview
These overnight oats are built as a dense, freezer-friendly breakfast puck: thick, chewy oats bound with chia and flax, packed with walnuts and mixed berries, and softened with Greek yogurt and oat milk. The base leans heavily on high-fiber whole grains and seeds plus plant omega-3s, while the yogurt supplies a solid protein backbone so a 1 cup portion actually eats like a meal, not a snack.[1][2][3][4][5][6] The texture is deliberately firmer and less watery than a typical overnight oat, with more crunch from the extra walnuts and enough volume to comfortably yield eight full pucks even if you lose a little to sticking or tasting. Sweetness is modest and comes mostly from fruit plus a small amount of maple, so you can add more at the table if you need it.

## Yield & Timing
- Yield: 8 very full 1 cup pucks (about 1 cup / 240 ml each), plus 1–2 small extra jar servings
- Active Prep: 20 minutes
- Inactive Prep: 6 to 24 hours (chilling / freezing)
- Cook: 0 minutes (no-cook recipe)
- Total: 6 to 24 hours, mostly hands-off
- Make-ahead: Base mix holds 3 to 4 days refrigerated; frozen pucks keep up to 2 months; thaw overnight in the fridge or 30 to 60 minutes at room temp before eating.[9][10]

## Ingredients
(Listed in use order; U.S. units with metric in parentheses. Marked [pre-prep] are easy to prepare ahead.)

- 3 cups old-fashioned rolled oats (about 240 g)
- 1/3 cup chia seeds (about 55 g)
- 1/4 cup ground flaxseed (about 28 g)
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
- 2 cups unsweetened oat milk (about 480 ml), plus up to 1/4 cup (60 ml) more if needed after chilling
- 2 cups plain nonfat Greek yogurt (about 480 g)
- 2 tbsp pure maple syrup
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 3 cups frozen mixed berries (about 420 g), still frozen or just starting to soften [pre-prep]
- 1 1/2 cups chopped walnuts (about 150 g), lightly toasted and cooled if you like [pre-prep]

## Detailed Instructions

1) **Dry mix for structure**
   - In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the rolled oats, chia seeds, ground flaxseed, and fine sea salt until evenly distributed. This keeps the chia and flax from clumping once the liquid goes in.

2) **Add liquids and sweetener**
   - Pour in the 2 cups unsweetened oat milk, Greek yogurt, maple syrup, and vanilla extract.
   - Stir firmly for 1 to 2 minutes, scraping down the sides and bottom, until everything is fully hydrated and no dry pockets remain. The mixture should look quite thick but still stirrable, not pourable.

3) **Fold in berries and walnuts**
   - Tip in the frozen mixed berries and chopped walnuts.
   - Fold gently with a spatula until the berries and nuts are evenly distributed. The berries will start to chill and thicken the mixture a bit more as they sit.

4) **Initial rest and texture check**
   - Let the bowl sit at room temperature for 10 minutes so the chia and flax start to gel.
   - After 10 minutes, stir again from the bottom. You want a thick, scoopable mix that mounds on a spoon.
     - If it feels too loose or soupy, stir in 2 to 4 tbsp additional oats and 1 tsp chia, then rest another 5 minutes.
     - If it feels too stiff to scoop into molds, splash in 2 to 4 tbsp extra oat milk and stir to loosen slightly.

5) **Portion into molds or containers**
   - For freezer pucks: Spoon the mixture into silicone muffin molds, a silicone “soup cube” tray, or similar 1 cup wells, packing it down lightly and smoothing the tops. You should comfortably fill 8 wells very full and still have a little left for an immediate snack or a small jar.
   - For fridge-only portions: Pack into 1 cup glass jars or lidded containers, leaving a little headspace for stirring in the morning.

6) **Chill or freeze**
   - For fridge oats: Cover and refrigerate at least 6 hours or overnight, up to 3 to 4 days total. Stir each portion before eating and thin with a splash of oat milk if desired.[9][10]
   - For freezer pucks: Freeze in molds until solid, at least 4 to 6 hours or overnight. Once frozen, pop out the pucks and store them in a labeled freezer bag or container for up to 2 months.

7) **Thaw and serve**
   - Overnight thaw: Transfer a frozen puck to the fridge the night before; in the morning, stir and add a small splash of oat milk if you like it looser.
   - Same-day thaw: Let a puck sit at room temperature 30 to 60 minutes (depending on room temp), then break up with a spoon and stir in a little oat milk or yogurt as needed.
   - Finish with extra berries, a sprinkle of cinnamon, or a few more chopped walnuts on top if you want more crunch.

- Make-Ahead Map: Mix and portion (Steps 1–5) up to 3 to 4 days ahead if storing in the fridge or freeze immediately after Step 5 and hold pucks up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temp before eating.[9][10]

## Special Notes & Variations
- Storage & Reheat:  
  - Fridge: Keep mixed oats refrigerated and covered; for food safety, aim to eat within 3 to 4 days, in line with USDA leftover guidance for cooked or high-moisture mixed dishes.[9][10]  
  - Freezer: Frozen pucks are best within about 2 months for texture. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature up to 1 hour, stirring in a little cold oat milk if the texture feels too thick. Do not refreeze once fully thawed.
- Serving ideas:  
  - Top with extra berries and a few chopped walnuts for crunch.  
  - Add a light drizzle of maple syrup or a spoon of Greek yogurt on top if you want it slightly sweeter or creamier.  
  - Sprinkle with cinnamon or a pinch of nutmeg just before eating.
- Dietary toggles:  
  - Lower sugar: Cut maple syrup to 1 tbsp or omit; rely on berries for sweetness.  
  - Higher protein: Add 1 extra cup Greek yogurt and reduce oat milk slightly to keep the texture thick; this will raise protein and calcium per serving.[6]  
  - Dairy-free: Swap Greek yogurt for a thick, unsweetened plant yogurt and verify that your oat milk is truly unsweetened; protein will drop, so you may want to add a scoop of a plant protein powder and reduce oats slightly to keep the texture similar.  
  - Lower sodium: Halve the added salt and add a little vanilla or cinnamon to compensate for perceived flavor loss.
