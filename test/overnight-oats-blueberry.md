# Nutty Berry Omega Overnight Oats (Freezer Pucks)

## Title & Overview
These overnight oats are built to be actually healthy: rolled oats, plain Greek yogurt, berries, chia, flax, and walnuts with only a small amount of optional maple syrup. Oats bring beta-glucan soluble fiber, which is linked to better cholesterol and steadier blood sugar when eaten regularly.[1][4] Berries layer in vitamin C and polyphenols, while chia, flax, and walnuts add fiber and omega-3-rich fats.[2][5][9][10] The mix sets up in 1-cup silicone molds so you can freeze single-serving pucks, thaw one overnight in the fridge, and have breakfast ready with almost no work.

## Yield & Timing
- Yield: 8 x 1-cup pucks (about 8 light or 4 hearty servings)
- Active Prep: 15 to 20 minutes
- Inactive Prep: at least 4 hours in the fridge (or overnight) to hydrate; several hours to freeze solid
- Cook: 0 minutes
- Total: About 4 hours (mostly hands-off)
- Make-ahead:
  - Fridge: pucks (kept as a scoopable mix in jars) hold 4 to 5 days at or below 40 deg F.[3][6]
  - Freezer: frozen pucks are best within about 1 to 3 months for flavor and texture, consistent with leftover guidance of 3 to 4 months.[3][6]
  - Thaw: transfer from freezer to fridge 8 to 12 hours in advance; once thawed, use within 3 to 4 days.[3][6]

## Ingredients

### Base mix for 8 x 1-cup pucks
- 2 cups old-fashioned rolled oats
- 2 cups unsweetened fortified oat milk
- 1 1/2 cups plain nonfat Greek yogurt
- 1/4 cup chia seeds (4 tablespoons)
- 2 tablespoons ground flaxseed
- 2 tablespoons pure maple syrup (optional; reduce or omit for less added sugar)
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 1/2 teaspoon fine sea salt

### Berries and nuts
- 2 1/2 cups mixed berries, fresh or frozen
- 1/2 cup chopped walnuts [pre-prep] (about 2 tablespoons per serving)
- Optional for serving: extra berries and a pinch of ground cinnamon

## Instructions

### 1. Prep equipment and nuts
1. **Set up molds.** Place eight 1-cup (250 ml) silicone freezer molds on a rimmed sheet pan or other rigid tray so they are easy to move once filled.
2. **Chop the walnuts.** Roughly chop walnuts into small pieces and set aside in an airtight container. Toasting them lightly in a dry skillet over low heat, then cooling completely, will deepen flavor but is optional.[5]

### 2. Mix the liquid base
3. **Combine wet ingredients.** In a large mixing bowl, whisk together:
   - 2 cups oat milk  
   - 1 1/2 cups Greek yogurt  
   - 2 tablespoons maple syrup (if using)  
   - 2 teaspoons vanilla extract  
   - 1/2 teaspoon fine sea salt  
   Whisk until the mixture is smooth and there are no visible yogurt lumps. Dietitian-style overnight oat recipes typically blend milk, yogurt, and sweetener first for the best texture.[6][7]

### 3. Add oats and seeds
4. **Stir in the dry ingredients.** Add:
   - 2 cups rolled oats  
   - 1/4 cup chia seeds  
   - 2 tablespoons ground flaxseed  
   Stir until all oats are moistened and seeds are evenly distributed. Chia and flax thicken as they hydrate, helping create a creamy, pudding-like texture while adding fiber and omega-3 fats.[2][9][10]
5. **Rest briefly and stir again.** Let the bowl sit for about 10 minutes, then stir again to break up any chia or flax clumps. This short rest helps ensure even hydration.

### 4. Fold in berries and portion into molds
6. **Add berries.** Gently fold 2 1/2 cups mixed berries into the oat mixture. If using frozen berries, they can go in still frozen; they will release some juice as they sit, naturally sweetening and coloring the oats.[6][8]
7. **Fill molds.** Divide the mixture evenly among the eight 1-cup silicone molds. Each mold should be close to full but not overfilled; leave a few millimeters of headspace so the mixture can expand slightly as it freezes.
8. **Set before freezing (optional but helpful).** If you have time, slide the tray into the fridge for 30 to 60 minutes. This gives oats and seeds a head start on hydrating and improves texture after freezing and thawing.

### 5. Freeze the pucks
9. **Freeze solid.** Transfer the tray carefully to the freezer. Freeze until the pucks are completely solid, at least several hours or overnight.
10. **Store long-term.** Pop the frozen pucks out of the molds and transfer them to a labeled freezer bag or airtight container. Press out excess air and seal. For best quality, use within 1 to 3 months; this falls well inside the 3 to 4 month freezer window that USDA uses as a general quality guideline for leftovers.[3]

### 6. Thaw and serve
11. **Thaw overnight.** The night before you want to eat, place 1 frozen puck into a 12 to 16 oz jar or bowl, cover, and refrigerate 8 to 12 hours. Thawing in the refrigerator rather than at room temperature aligns with food safety guidance for leftovers.[3]
12. **Stir and adjust texture.** In the morning, stir the thawed puck thoroughly from bottom to top. If it seems thicker than you like, add a splash of oat milk and stir again until it reaches your preferred consistency. Oats and chia continue absorbing liquid over time, so a small top-up is normal.[2][6]
13. **Top and eat.** Just before serving, add:
    - About 2 tablespoons chopped walnuts  
    - A few extra berries and a pinch of cinnamon, if you like  
    Eat straight from the jar/bowl cold, or warm gently in the microwave in 15 to 20 second bursts (lid off), stirring between bursts. If you prefer it fully hot, reheat until steaming; USDA suggests 165 deg F as a safe reheating temperature for leftovers.[3]

## Nutrition (per serving)

One serving is defined as **1 thawed 1-cup puck plus 2 tablespoons chopped walnuts**.

- Calories: ~290 kcal
- Protein: ~12 g
- Carbs: ~31 g  
  - Fiber: ~7 g  
  - Sugars: ~10 g (about 3 g added sugar from maple; the rest from berries and dairy)
- Fat: ~14 g  
  - Saturated: ~1.5 g  
  - Unsaturated: ~12.5 g (mostly mono- and polyunsaturated from walnuts, chia, flax, and oat milk)

- Key micronutrients (approximate):
  - Calcium: ~250 mg (about 20 percent DV), mainly from fortified oat milk, Greek yogurt, and chia.[2][3][9]
  - Iron: ~2.1 mg (about 12 percent DV), from oats, seeds, nuts, and berries.[1][2][5][9]
  - Potassium: ~390 mg (about 8 percent DV), from oats, yogurt, oat milk, berries, and walnuts.[1][3][5]
  - Magnesium: ~95 mg (about 23 percent DV), largely from chia, flax, oats, and walnuts.[1][2][9][10]
  - Vitamin C: ~14 mg (about 15 percent DV), mostly from berries.[5]
  - Vitamin D: ~0.9 mcg (about 5 percent DV), depending on fortification level of the oat milk.[3]
  - Vitamin B12: ~0.5 mcg (around 20 percent DV), from Greek yogurt and fortified oat milk.[3]

- Assumptions:
  - Unsweetened fortified oat milk at about 70 kcal per cup with added calcium, vitamin D, and B12.
  - Plain nonfat Greek yogurt at about 80 kcal and 14 g protein per 3/4 cup.
  - Mixed berries with no added sugar.
  - 2 tablespoons chopped walnuts per serving.
  - Batch yields 8 pucks; each puck plus nuts equals 1 serving.

## Special Notes & Variations

- Storage & Reheat:
  - **Fridge (not frozen):** If you keep the mixture in jars instead of molds, store at or below 40 deg F and eat within 4 to 5 days, aligning with dietitian and USDA leftover guidance.[3][6]
  - **Freezer:** Keep pucks tightly wrapped or in a low-air container; for best quality use within 1 to 3 months, within USDA’s 3 to 4 month quality window for frozen leftovers.[3]
  - **Thaw:** Always thaw in the fridge, not on the counter. Once a puck is thawed, treat it like any other leftover and use within 3 to 4 days.[3]
  - **Reheat:** If heating, warm until steaming; 165 deg F is the standard reheating target for safety.[3]

- Serving ideas:
  - Add a spoonful of extra Greek yogurt on top for more protein.
  - Sprinkle on cinnamon, nutmeg, or cardamom for more flavor without added sugar.
  - Pair with black coffee or unsweetened tea for a low-sugar breakfast.
  - For a heartier meal, have 2 pucks plus nuts; that moves you into roughly 550 to 600 kcal and around mid-20s grams of protein.

- Dietary toggles:
  - **Lower sugar:** Cut maple syrup to 1 tablespoon for the batch or omit entirely; rely on berries and vanilla for sweetness.
  - **Higher protein:** Increase Greek yogurt to 2 cups and reduce oat milk to 1 1/2 cups, or add an extra tablespoon of walnuts per serving.
  - **Lower fat:** Use 1 tablespoon walnuts per serving and keep chia/flax as-is for fiber and micronutrients.
  - **Dairy-free:** Swap in a high-protein plant yogurt (soy or pea) and dairy-free yogurt-style cultures; keep fortified oat, soy, or pea milk for calcium and B12.
  - **Gluten-free:** Use certified gluten-free oats to avoid gluten cross-contact.
