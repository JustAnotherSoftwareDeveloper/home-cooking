# Spicy Miso-Doubanjiang Savory Oat Congee Pucks (Tofu, Edamame, Greens)

## Title & Overview
This is a savory, ramen-adjacent oat porridge designed for meal prep: cook one pot, portion into 2-cup silicone molds, freeze, then reheat from frozen in the microwave. The flavor backbone is **miso plus doubanjiang** (fermented chili bean paste) with ginger, garlic, and a little sesame richness, so it tastes punchy and intentional instead of "plain oats with soy sauce." Adding miso **off heat** preserves its aroma, and blooming doubanjiang in oil builds depth and a red, savory base.

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 8 pucks (2 cups each; about 16 cups total)
- **Active Prep:** 20 to 30 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 35 to 60 minutes (cooling before freezing)
- **Cook:** 35 to 55 minutes (realistic for this volume; most of the "extra time" is bringing 10 cups liquid to a boil and recovering after frozen add-ins)
- **Total:** 1 hour 30 minutes to 2 hours 25 minutes
- **Make-ahead:** Freeze pucks up to 2 to 3 months for best quality. Reheat from frozen, covered, with a mid-stir; add a splash of water/broth to restore creaminess.

## Ingredients
- avocado oil, 2 Tbsp
- scallions, 2 bunches, thin-sliced, whites and greens separated
- ginger, 2 Tbsp finely grated (or 3 Tbsp minced)
- garlic, 6 cloves, minced
- doubanjiang, 2 Tbsp (up to 3 Tbsp for more punch)
- low-sodium vegetable broth, 10 cups (hold back 1 cup if you want tighter control)
- old-fashioned rolled oats, 4 cups
- green cabbage, 6 to 8 cups thin-sliced or shredded
- frozen shelled edamame, 3 cups (about 12 oz)
- extra-firm tofu, 28 to 32 oz, drained and cut into 1/2-in cubes
- white miso paste, 1/2 cup (8 Tbsp)
- tahini (or Chinese sesame paste), 1/4 cup (4 Tbsp)
- rice vinegar, 2 Tbsp
- toasted sesame oil, 2 tsp
- nori, 2 sheets crumbled (optional)
- chili crisp or crushed red pepper, to taste (optional)

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** soy (tofu, edamame, miso), sesame (tahini/sesame oil), gluten possible (many doubanjiang contain wheat)
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - tahini -> Chinese sesame paste -> deeper toasted sesame flavor -> whisk with hot broth before adding
  - cabbage -> chopped kale -> sturdier greens -> simmer 2 to 3 minutes longer
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - doubanjiang -> generic hot sauce -> loses fermented depth -> if you must, use chili crisp for aroma plus a small pinch of sugar to round heat, then rebalance salt carefully

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** A spicy, savory oat porridge that eats like a creamy noodle-soup base with tofu, edamame, and cabbage.
- **Texture cue:** Oats should be creamy and suspended (not watery), but you stop slightly looser than "ready-to-eat" so it reheats creamy.
- **Workflow:** Bloom aromatics + doubanjiang, boil broth, whisk in oats, simmer to creamy, fold in veg/protein, then finish off heat with miso + tahini.
- **Make-ahead:** Cool, portion 2 cups per mold, freeze solid, then bag and label.
- **Reheat plan:** Microwave covered from frozen with a splash of water/broth, stir hard mid-way, rest 1 minute, then stir again until silky.

## Instructions

1. Prep and stage
   - Drain tofu and cut into 1/2-in cubes.
   - Slice scallions (separate whites/greens).
   - Mince garlic, grate ginger, shred cabbage.
   - Set out a whisk and keep broth measured and ready.

2. Build the spicy fermented base (do not scorch it)
   - Heat avocado oil in a large pot over medium until shimmering.
   - Add scallion whites; saute 1 to 2 minutes until softened.
   - Add ginger and garlic; saute 60 to 90 seconds until very fragrant.
   - Add doubanjiang; fry gently 1 to 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until the oil turns red and the paste smells toasted (not raw).
     - If it starts sticking or darkening too fast: lower heat and splash in 1 to 2 Tbsp broth to loosen.

3. Boil, then cook the oats to a loose congee
   - Add broth and bring to a **boil** over medium-high heat, 8 to 12 minutes.
   - Whisk in oats in a steady stream to prevent clumps.
   - Let it return to a simmer, 1 to 3 minutes, then reduce to medium-low.
   - Simmer 10 to 14 minutes, stirring every 1 to 2 minutes, until creamy and thickened.
     - Target: thick stew-like porridge, but slightly looser than final (it tightens as it cools and again after freezing).
     - If it thickens too fast: whisk in hot water/broth, 1/2 cup at a time.

4. Fold in cabbage, edamame, then tofu (and reduce back to cohesive)
   - Stir in cabbage and frozen edamame; keep at medium-low.
   - Cook 6 to 10 minutes total: recover back to a simmer, soften cabbage, and re-thicken.
   - Fold in tofu and cook 2 to 4 minutes, just to heat through.
   - If it looks watery after the frozen add-ins: simmer uncovered 2 to 6 minutes, stirring, until cohesive again.

5. Finish off heat and taste-adjust (do not boil miso)
   - Turn off heat.
   - In a bowl, whisk miso with 1 ladle of hot porridge liquid until smooth; stir into the pot.
   - In the same bowl, whisk tahini with another ladle of hot liquid until smooth; stir into the pot.
   - Stir in rice vinegar, toasted sesame oil, scallion greens, and nori (if using).
   - Optional: add chili crisp or crushed red pepper to taste.
   - Taste and adjust:
     - Add vinegar 1 tsp at a time for brightness.
     - Avoid adding extra salt until you’ve tried acid + sesame + scallions first.

## Make-Ahead Notes

### Cool + Freeze
- Cool in a wide container until warm (not steaming).
- Portion 2 cups into silicone molds; freeze flat on a sheet pan overnight.
- Pop out pucks and store in freezer bags/containers.

### Storage
- Fridge (if not freezing): 3 to 4 days.
- Freezer: best quality 2 to 3 months.

## Reheat Plan (Microwave Only)
- Put 1 frozen 2-cup puck in a microwave-safe bowl.
- Add 4 Tbsp water or broth around it. Cover with a vented lid/plate.
- Microwave on full power until steaming hot, stirring hard at least once mid-way.
- Rest 1 minute (standing time), then stir again until silky.
- If still too thick: add 1 to 2 Tbsp water and microwave 30 to 60 seconds more, then stir.

## Common Issues
- Bitter/harsh base: doubanjiang scorched -> next time lower heat; now add 1 to 2 Tbsp extra tahini + 1 tsp vinegar to soften.
- Flat flavor: add vinegar in small steps + more scallion greens; optionally add chili crisp.
- Microwave brick: batch was cooked too thick -> add more water during reheat and stir more aggressively.

## Scaling
- Halve: use a 4 qt pot; boil time is shorter, oat simmer time after returning to simmer is similar.
- Increase to 10 pucks: multiply ingredients by 1.25; use an 8 qt pot and expect longer boil and recovery times.
