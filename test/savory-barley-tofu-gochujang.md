# Gochujang-Scallion Pearl Barley Jook with Dubu-Jorim Tofu Crumbles and Edamame

## Title & Overview
This is a Korean-leaning savory barley porridge built to fix the main problems from the earlier oat version: more grain texture, tofu that actually carries flavor, and a louder savory-spicy base. Pearl barley gives the bowl chew and structure instead of turning smooth, while the tofu is browned first, then torn into rough crumbles and glazed in a dubu-jorim-style sauce so it stays distinct in the final bowl. The result eats like a thick, spicy grain stew crossed with jook, rather than breakfast porridge.

It is generally healthy in the practical sense: real protein from tofu and edamame, controlled fat, and a mixed meal structure that feels like lunch. It is also freezer-friendly because the barley can be stopped slightly loose before portioning, then brought back to a glossy spoonable texture with added liquid during microwave reheat.

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 8 servings; each serving is 1 frozen 2-cup puck
- **Active Prep:** 35 to 45 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 30 to 45 minutes
- **Cook:** 45 to 60 minutes
- **Total:** 1 hour 50 minutes to 2 hours 30 minutes
- **Make-ahead:** Holds 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Freeze pucks up to 2 to 3 months for best quality. Reheat from frozen in the microwave with added liquid until fully steaming hot.

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** soy, sesame, gluten possible if using regular soy sauce or some gochujang brands
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - tamari instead of soy sauce -> same savory profile -> keeps it gluten-free if your gochujang is also gluten-free
  - super-firm tofu instead of extra-firm tofu -> even chewier texture -> you can skip the freeze-thaw step
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - rolled oats instead of pearl barley -> moves the dish back toward the smoother texture you disliked -> if you must, treat it as a different dish and cut the cook time dramatically
- **Diet toggles (optional; only if relevant):**
  - Low-sodium: use unsalted or very low-sodium broth, keep soy at 4 Tbsp total, and push brightness with vinegar and extra scallion greens instead of extra salt
  - High-protein: increase edamame to 3 cups and use super-firm tofu
  - Gluten-free: use tamari and a gluten-free gochujang
  - Dairy-free: already dairy-free
- **Cross-contact note (optional):**
  - Some gochujang brands contain wheat; check the label if needed

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** A thick, spicy-savory barley porridge with chewy grain, bold tofu crumbles, and edamame built in.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Stop the barley when it is thick and spoonable but still a little looser than your final target; the tofu should be browned first, then glazed until tacky.
- **Workflow:** Brown tofu first, then make the glaze; while the barley cooks, finish the tofu; fold the edamame in near the end.
- **Make-ahead:** Freeze after the final combine, once the pot has cooled to warm; this dish is designed to be reheated from frozen with added liquid.
- **Reheat plan:** Microwave covered with 3 to 4 Tbsp water or broth, stir hard once mid-way, then rest 1 minute before the final stir.
- **Primary risks:** Reducing the barley too far before freezing, using a watery tofu sauce, or under-seasoning the barley base relative to the tofu.

## Gather & Stage
- Measure and group all ingredients by phase; place them within reach.
- Set out the Stainless steel pot, Stainless steel pan or carbon steel skillet, whisk or wooden spoon, and silicone molds.
- Complete all **[pre-prep]** items: thaw and squeeze tofu dry, mince garlic, grate ginger, slice scallions, and slice the onion.
- Mix the tofu braising sauce before turning on the heat.
- Set the silicone molds on the Sheet / baking tray, 13x9 so they can go straight to the freezer later.
- **Checkpoint:** everything needed for Step 1 is ready and within reach.

## Instructions

1. Prep the tofu and mix the braising sauce
   - **Slice** each tofu block into 3 thick slabs, then pat or squeeze them dry.
     - In a bowl, **Whisk** together 3 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 Tbsp gochujang, 2 Tbsp gochugaru, brown sugar, half the garlic, half the ginger, 1/2 tsp toasted sesame oil, and 1/2 cup water into a loose braising sauce.
     - The sauce should be pourable, not a thick paste.
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready to proceed when the tofu is dry enough to brown and the sauce is smooth.
     - *If the sauce looks pasty instead of pourable, then whisk in 1 to 2 Tbsp water; cue it's fixed: it pours in a ribbon, not a blob.*

2. Brown, tear, and glaze the tofu
   - In the Stainless steel pan or carbon steel skillet, **Heat** 1 Tbsp avocado oil over **medium-high heat** for 1 to 2 minutes, until shimmering.
     - **Brown** the tofu slabs 3 to 5 minutes per side, until golden on both sides.
     - Transfer the slabs to a plate and let them cool 3 to 5 minutes, then **Tear** them into rough crumbles.
     - Return the pan to **medium heat**, add the onion and scallion whites, and **Saute** 2 to 3 minutes until softened.
     - Add the tofu crumbles and the braising sauce, then **Reduce** 3 to 5 minutes until the sauce is glossy and mostly clinging to the tofu rather than pooling.
     - Finish with 1/2 tsp toasted sesame oil.
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready to proceed when the tofu is craggy and glazed, with just a little sauce left to carry into the porridge.
     - *If the tofu starts steaming instead of browning, then spread it out and leave it undisturbed for another 1 to 2 minutes; cue it's fixed: the edges look dry-golden instead of wet.*

3. Build the barley jook base
   - In the Stainless steel pot, **Heat** the remaining 1 Tbsp avocado oil over **medium heat** for about 1 minute.
     - Add the remaining garlic, remaining ginger, and half the scallion greens; **Saute** 60 to 90 seconds until fragrant.
     - Add the pearl barley and **Stir** 1 to 2 minutes until it looks glossy and lightly toasted.
     - Add the remaining 1 Tbsp gochujang and 1 Tbsp gochugaru; **Stir** 15 to 30 seconds, just until the paste loosens and smells fragrant.
     - **Pour** in the broth and remaining 2 Tbsp soy sauce, then **Boil** over **medium-high heat**.
     - Once boiling, lower to **medium-low heat** and **Simmer** 35 to 45 minutes, stirring every 5 to 10 minutes and more often near the end, until the barley is tender-chewy and the pot looks like a loose porridge.
     - *While this cooks, clean the tofu pan and get the molds ready on the tray.*
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready to proceed when the barley is tender but still has chew, and the base is slightly looser than you want to eat.
     - *If the bottom starts catching, then lower the heat and whisk in 1/2 cup hot water or broth; cue it's fixed: the spoon drags cleanly without scraping stuck grain.*

4. Fold in edamame and tofu
   - **Stir** in the edamame and **Simmer** over **medium-low heat** for 4 to 6 minutes, until the beans are hot through.
     - **Fold** in the tofu crumbles and any remaining glaze from the pan.
     - **Simmer** 2 to 3 minutes more, just until the whole pot is unified and spoonable.
     - **Checkpoint:** You are ready to finish when every spoonful gets barley, tofu, and edamame, and the mixture looks thick but not tight.

5. Finish, cool, and taste-adjust
   - Off heat, **Stir** in 1 Tbsp rice vinegar, the remaining scallion greens, sesame seeds, and the remaining 1 tsp toasted sesame oil.
     - For acid, add the second tablespoon of vinegar only if the bowl still tastes flat.
     - Taste-adjust loop: acid -> salt -> heat -> herbs.
     - For salt, use soy or kosher salt carefully; the main sodium drivers are soy sauce, broth, and gochujang.
     - For heat, add extra gochugaru or a little more gochujang if needed.
     - For aroma, add extra scallion greens.
     - Let the porridge cool until warm, then portion 2 cups into each silicone mold cavity.
     - Freeze flat on the tray until solid, then transfer the pucks to freezer bags or containers.
     - **Checkpoint:** Final dish is thick, spicy-savory, and still visibly textured, not smooth or paste-like.

## Common Issues
- **Too smooth:** Barley was cooked too long or reduced too hard -> add hot broth and stop earlier next batch -> you should still feel the grain.
- **Tofu tastes bland:** Sauce was too thin or added too early -> glaze after browning -> the tofu should look lacquered, not washed.
- **Microwave brick:** Frozen puck was packed too dry -> add liquid before reheating -> it should loosen after a hard stir.

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 5: cool in a wide container until warm, then refrigerate.
  - Holds: 3 to 4 days
  - Serve/Reheat: microwave with a splash of water or broth until steaming hot, then stir hard and re-check seasoning.
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: Step 5
  - Portioning: 2 cups per silicone cavity; freeze the mold flat on the tray, then bag the pucks once solid
  - Freeze: 2 to 3 months
  - Thaw: optional overnight in the fridge, but not required
  - Reheat: microwave from frozen with 3 to 4 Tbsp water or broth until fully steaming hot; stir at least once mid-way

## Reheat Plan
- **Best method:** **Microwave** on full power for 4 to 7 minutes total for 1 frozen 2-cup puck, covered, until fully steaming hot throughout
- **Texture reset:** add 3 to 4 Tbsp water or broth before heating, stir hard midway, then **Rest** 1 minute and stir again -> it should return to a loose, glossy porridge
- *If it reheats unevenly, then microwave in 60 to 90 second bursts with a stir between each burst; cue it's fixed: there are no cold dense pockets left.*

## Troubleshooting
- Too watery -> too much finish liquid or not enough reduction -> simmer uncovered 3 to 5 minutes -> spoon leaves a slow-closing trail
- Too tight after reheat -> not enough added liquid -> stir in 1 to 2 more Tbsp hot water and microwave 30 to 60 seconds -> grains relax and separate slightly
- Too salty -> broth or soy ran high -> add hot unsalted water and a small splash of vinegar -> the flavor tastes bright again, not harsh
- Not spicy enough -> the base was under-seasoned relative to the barley volume -> dissolve 1 tsp gochujang in a spoonful of hot broth and stir it in -> heat comes through without dry pepper grit

## Geometry Notes
- Pan/pot size used: 6 to 8 qt pot for the barley; 12-in skillet for tofu
- Target thickness / layer depth: thick, spoonable porridge that still slowly settles flat in the pot
- Crowding rule: if your skillet is smaller than 12 inches, brown the tofu in 2 batches

## Scaling & Batch Size
- Scaling notes: Halving works cleanly; doubling is possible but the barley pot should be at least 8 qt and the tofu should be browned in batches.
- Batch strategy: If you double, keep the tofu glazing separate and combine only at the end so the pan can still reduce properly.
- Pan swaps: A Dutch oven works better than a narrow stockpot because barley porridge needs occasional stirring and visible texture checks.

## Special Notes
- Storage & reheat: Reheat leftovers until steaming hot throughout.
- Serving ideas: good on its own, or with extra scallions or roasted seaweed on the side
- Sodium notes: Top sodium drivers are soy sauce, gochujang, and broth. Levers: use low-sodium broth, use tamari or soy sparingly in the finish instead of the base, add vinegar and scallion greens before adding more salt, and keep extra gochujang as a finish adjustment instead of adding it all to the pot up front.

## Variations
1. **Doenjang-Barley Version:** Swap the barley-base gochujang for 1 to 1 1/2 Tbsp doenjang and keep the dubu-jorim tofu spicy. This shifts the bowl from bright chile-forward heat to a deeper fermented savoriness while keeping the same freezer and reheat logic.

2. **More Jook, Less Stew:** Add 1 to 2 more cups broth during the last 10 minutes and reduce the tofu glaze a little less. That makes the bowl read more like a true porridge and less like a thick grain stew.

3. **Mapo-Leaning Finish:** Replace the rice vinegar with Chinese black vinegar and add a pinch of ground Sichuan pepper to the tofu glaze. It pushes the bowl away from Korean lunchbox flavors and toward a spicier, sharper profile.
