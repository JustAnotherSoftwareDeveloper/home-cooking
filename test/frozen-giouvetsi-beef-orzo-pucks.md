# Classic Baked Beef Giouvetsi Meal Pucks (20 cups; Dutch Oven Braise -> 2 Baking Dishes)

## Title & Overview
Giouvetsi (youvetsi) is a classic Greek beef-and-orzo bake where tender beef cooks in a tomato sauce scented with warm spices, then the orzo finishes in the oven until creamy and saucy. [1][2][3]
This version is built for freezer meal pucks: it is intentionally kept saucy (so it reheats without drying out), portioned into 2-cup silicone molds, frozen fully cooked, then reheated from frozen in the microwave. [1][2][4]
It is generally healthy in a practical sense because it is protein-forward, uses restrained added oil, and can be made more vegetable-forward without changing the core technique. (Cheese is optional and best added after reheating.) [2]

## Ingredients
- 3 3/4 lb beef chuck roast or stew meat, well-trimmed, cut into 1 to 1 1/4 inch cubes [pre-prep]
- 2 1/2 tsp kosher salt (start with 2 tsp; adjust at the end)
- 1 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 2 1/2 tbsp avocado oil or olive oil, divided
- 3 large yellow onions, diced [pre-prep]
- 4 medium carrots, diced [pre-prep]
- 4 celery stalks, diced [pre-prep]
- 10 garlic cloves, minced [pre-prep]
- 2 1/2 tbsp tomato paste
- 5 cups low-sodium beef broth, plus 3 to 5 cups extra kept hot for topping off before/during baking (see Step 9)
- 1 (28 oz) can crushed tomatoes
- 1 (15 oz) can tomato sauce or passata
- 2 1/2 tsp dried oregano
- 1/3 tsp ground cinnamon (see Step 7) [7]
- 3 bay leaves
- 1 1/4 tsp ground allspice
- 1/8 tsp ground cloves (optional)
- 20 oz orzo (about 3 1/3 cups)
- 3 tbsp lemon juice, plus more to taste [pre-prep]
- 6 oz baby spinach (optional)
- 5 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley (optional) [pre-prep]
- Pecorino Romano or Parmesan, grated, for serving (optional) [2]

## Prep Gotchas & QA
- Dry the beef thoroughly before searing; crowding or wet surfaces will steam instead of brown.
- Keep the orzo fully submerged and the mixture "too soupy" before baking; top off with hot broth as instructed so pucks reheat without drying.
- Rotate and stir the two baking dishes midway so orzo cooks evenly; add hot broth if either dish tightens early.
- Refrigerate cooked giouvetsi within 2 hours (faster if possible); reheat pucks to **165°F** throughout.
- Taste salt at the end after lemon and herbs—cheese adds salt if used.

## Detailed Instructions
1. Oven + equipment setup (scaled for 20 cups).
   - Heat oven to 325 deg F for the braise.
   - Set racks to upper-middle and lower-middle.
   - You will need:
     - 1 large Dutch oven with lid (for braising).
     - 1 large skillet (for searing).
     - 2 deep 9x13 baking dishes (for the orzo finish; 20 cups is too much for one dish to cook evenly).
   - Warm 3 to 5 cups extra broth in a pot or kettle and keep it hot (you will use it to keep the bake saucy).

2. Season and dry the beef (do not skip the drying).
   - Toss beef with 2 tsp of the salt and all the pepper.
   - Spread beef on a tray and pat very dry with paper towels. Dry surface = browning; wet surface = steaming.

3. Sear the beef in batches (detailed method).
   - Heat a large skillet over medium-high for 3 to 4 minutes.
   - Add 1 tbsp oil and heat until shimmering.
   - Add beef in a single layer with space between pieces (do 3 batches).
   - Do not move the beef for 2 to 3 minutes so it can brown deeply.
   - Flip and brown 1 to 2 minutes more.
   - Transfer browned beef to the Dutch oven as you go.
   Cues:
   - If liquid pools, the pan is crowded: remove some pieces and continue.
   - If fond is getting very dark, reduce heat slightly and keep batches moving.
   Goal: deep browning and good fond, not cooking the beef through.

4. Build the braise base in the Dutch oven.
   - Set Dutch oven over medium heat.
   - Add remaining 1 tbsp oil.
   - Add onions, carrots, and celery with a pinch of salt; cook 8 to 10 minutes until softened and lightly colored.
   - Add garlic; cook 45 to 60 seconds.

5. Bloom tomato paste and spices.
   - Stir in tomato paste; cook 1 to 2 minutes, scraping the pot.
   - Stir in oregano, cinnamon, allspice, and optional cloves; cook 30 seconds.

6. Braise.
   - Add 5 cups broth, crushed tomatoes, tomato sauce/passata, and bay leaves.
   - Add the seared beef (and any juices) to the Dutch oven.
   - Bring to a gentle simmer, cover, and braise in the oven at 325 deg F for 75 to 95 minutes, until beef is fork-tender.

7. Ground cinnamon note (replacing a cinnamon stick).
   - Ground cinnamon is more immediately intense than a stick simmered in liquid.
   - A common conversion guideline is to use about 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon per cinnamon stick; for this scaled batch, 1/3 tsp is a sensible starting point. Adjust next batch if you want it warmer. [7]

8. Toast the orzo (texture insurance for freezing + reheating).
   - While the beef braises, toast the orzo in a dry skillet or with a small drizzle of oil over medium heat.
   - Stir frequently until lightly golden and nutty, 3 to 6 minutes.

9. Combine, split, and set liquid level (this is the puck-success step).
   - Remove Dutch oven from oven. Discard bay leaves.
   - Increase oven temperature to 350 deg F.
   - Stir toasted orzo into the hot beef-and-tomato braise.
   - Divide evenly between the 2 baking dishes.
   - Before baking, the mixture should look "too soupy" and the orzo should be fully submerged.
     Multiple giouvetsi sources converge on a rule of thumb of about 3 cups of liquid per 1 cup of orzo, and specifically recommend topping off with hot liquid if the braise reduced. [1][3]
   - Add hot broth (from your reserved pot) to each dish until the orzo is fully submerged and the dishes look loose and saucy. [1][3]
   - Optional volume check: After topping off, you should be very close to 20 cups total across both dishes. If you are short due to evaporation, add additional hot broth now (staying saucy is the priority for microwave pucks). [1][3]

10. Bake (two pans; stir + rotate).
   - Cover both baking dishes tightly with foil.
   - Bake at 350 deg F for 20 minutes.
   - Remove foil, stir each dish thoroughly (scrape edges/corners), then swap rack positions (top dish to bottom and bottom dish to top).
   - Re-cover and bake 15 to 25 minutes more, until the orzo is tender and the mixture is still saucy.
   - If either dish tightens up early, stir in a splash of hot broth and continue baking. [1]

11. Finish.
   - Rest 10 minutes (it thickens as it sits).
   - Stir in 3 tbsp lemon juice.
   - Fold in spinach (if using) to wilt, then parsley (if using).
   - Taste and adjust salt (up to remaining 1/2 tsp, as needed).

12. Cool, portion, freeze (food-safe).
   - Spread the giouvetsi into shallow containers to cool faster.
   - Refrigerate within 2 hours. [6]
   - Once fully cool, portion into 2-cup silicone molds (10 pucks). Freeze solid, then pop out and store airtight.

13. Microwave reheat from frozen (simple and repeatable).
   - Put 1 frozen puck in a microwave-safe bowl.
   - Add 2 to 4 tbsp broth around the edges (prevents drying).
   - Cover and microwave on high in 3-minute bursts, stirring/rotating between bursts.
   - Allow 1 to 2 minutes standing time, then continue until the center reaches 165 deg F throughout. [4][5]
   - Finish with a small extra splash of lemon juice (optional) and grated Pecorino Romano or Parmesan (optional). [2]

Make-ahead map:
- Steps 1-6 (braise): up to 2 days ahead (refrigerate).
- Steps 8-11 (toast orzo + bake finish): day-of.
- Step 12 (portion/freeze): once cooled.
- Step 13: reheat from frozen as needed.

## Make-Ahead, Storage & Reheat (Optional)
- Braise (Steps 1-6) up to 2 days ahead; reheat to a simmer and proceed with orzo + bake day-of. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Leftovers are generally fine 3 to 4 days refrigerated or 3 to 4 months frozen (quality best within that window). Reheat to 165 deg F. [6][4]

## Serving & Variations
- Serving ideas: Extra lemon juice after reheating (brightens without extra fat); parsley; Pecorino Romano or Parmesan (optional). [2]
- Dietary toggles: Higher-protein tilt: reduce orzo to 16 oz and keep beef the same; keep the "soupy before bake" rule by topping off with hot broth. [1][3]
- Vegetable-forward: Add 8 to 12 oz chopped mushrooms to the onion/carrots/celery step; keep broth top-offs hot so the bake stays saucy.
- Lower-sodium: Use no-salt-added tomatoes and low-sodium broth; rely more on lemon juice and herbs for lift.

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** wheat (orzo); optional dairy (cheese)
- **Swaps:** Use gluten-free orzo or small GF pasta; omit cheese or use a lactose-free hard cheese if needed.
- **Cross-contact:** Keep cutting boards/knives for raw beef separate from vegetables and cooked components.

## Scaling & Batch Size
- The recipe is already scaled to 20 cups (10 × 2-cup pucks). If halving, use one 9x13 dish and reduce broth top-offs proportionally; keep the "soupy before bake" rule.
- If scaling up further, ensure enough surface area (more baking dishes) so orzo cooks evenly; maintain about 3 cups hot liquid per cup of orzo before baking.

## Troubleshooting
| Symptom | Likely Cause | Quick Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Orzo too dry after bake | Not enough hot liquid added before/during bake or overbaked | Stir in hot broth, cover, and rest; next time keep mixture looser and check earlier |
| Uneven orzo doneness between pans | No mid-bake stir/rotation or uneven oven heat | Stir and swap rack positions halfway; ensure both dishes look soupy before covering again |
| Beef not tender | Braise time too short or oven temp low | Return to oven covered and braise 10–20 minutes more until fork-tender |
| Puck dry after microwave | No splash of broth added or overheated | Add 2–4 tbsp hot broth around puck edges and reheat in shorter bursts, stirring |

## Safety & Correctness Notes
- Refrigerate cooked giouvetsi within 2 hours (1 hour if room is above 90 deg F) and reheat to **165 deg F** throughout. [4][6]
- Keep the mixture saucy before baking and when portioning; this is intentional for freezer pucks so they do not dry out when reheated.
