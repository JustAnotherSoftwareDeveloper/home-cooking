# Pastane-Style Revani (Light Metal Pan)
Turkish yogurt–semolina syrup cake with lemon-peel syrup, set overnight

## Title & Overview
Revani is a classic Turkish *şerbetli tatlı*: a tender semolina cake soaked with a citrus syrup until uniformly moist and sliceable. This version aims for a “pastane” (bakery) texture—light but cohesive, not gummy, and not soggy at the bottom—by using a light metal pan, staged syruping, and an overnight rest. Lemon zest is intentionally omitted; instead, lemon aroma comes from **lemon-peel–infused syrup** plus measured lemon juice in the batter. It’s best made the day before serving and improves noticeably after a full overnight set [1][2].

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 1 (9x9-in light-colored metal pan), ~12–16 pieces
- **Active Prep:** 25–35 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 8–16 hours (overnight set; required)
- **Cook:** 25–33 minutes (cake) + 12–15 minutes (syrup)
- **Total:** ~1.5–2 hours day-of + overnight
- **Make-ahead:** Best after overnight chill. Holds 3–4 days refrigerated, tightly covered. Serve at room temp (45–60 minutes out of the fridge) for best texture [1][2].

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** A semolina–yogurt tray cake soaked with lemon syrup until evenly moist and clean-slicing [1][2].
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Cake is **evenly golden**, springs back, and a center toothpick is clean; syrup is absorbed without a standing “lake” [1][2].
- **Workflow:** **Simmer syrup first** (cool it), **whip eggs + sugar**, mix batter, **rest batter 25–35 min**, **bake**, then **ladle syrup in passes** [1][2].
- **Make-ahead:** **Overnight** rest is the point—texture tightens and syrup distributes evenly [2].
- **Primary risks:** Overbaked edges (wrong pan), syrup too thick, or adding syrup too fast (pooling/soggy bottom) [2][4].

## Instructions
1. Syrup: lemon-peel infusion (cool completely)
   - **Simmer** the syrup.
     - Combine **1 1/2 cups sugar**, **1 1/2 cups water**, and **2 lemon peel strips** in a saucepan.
     - Bring to a boil over **medium heat**, stirring until dissolved; reduce to **low heat** and **Simmer** 10–12 minutes, uncovered, until slightly thickened but still very pourable [1].
     - Stir in **2 Tbsp lemon juice** and **Simmer** 2–3 minutes more; **remove peel** and cool syrup to **room temperature** [1][2].
     - *While this runs: grease the pan, pull eggs/yogurt out to take the chill off, and preheat the oven.*
     - **Checkpoint:** Syrup is fully cooled and pours in a thin stream.
     - *If syrup feels sticky/thick -> add 2–4 Tbsp water -> warm briefly to dissolve -> cooled syrup pours easily.*

2. Preheat and pan setup (light metal pan)
   - **Preheat** oven to **350°F**.
     - Place rack in the middle.
   - **Grease** a 9x9-in light-colored metal pan with **2 Tbsp oil** (from your measured total).
     - *Optional: line bottom with parchment for guaranteed clean release.*
     - **Checkpoint:** Pan is evenly greased; oven is fully preheated.
     - *If you only have a dark pan -> reduce oven to 325°F -> start checking earlier -> edges won’t overbrown as quickly [4].*

3. Batter: whip + mix
   - **Whip** eggs and sugar, then combine.
     - **Whip** **3 large eggs + 1 cup sugar** on high 5–6 minutes, until pale, thick, and expanded (ribboning off beaters) [2].
     - Beat in remaining **2 Tbsp oil** + **1 cup yogurt** just until smooth.
     - Add **semolina, flour, baking powder, vanilla, 2 Tbsp lemon juice, and a pinch of salt**; mix just until combined; scrape the bowl well.
     - **Checkpoint:** Batter is smooth and thick, no dry pockets.

4. Semolina hydration rest (Option A)
   - **Rest** the batter 25–35 minutes at room temperature (this is intentional).
     - This reduces “toothsome/crumbly” texture by fully hydrating semolina before baking [2].
     - **Checkpoint:** Batter thickens slightly and looks more uniform.
     - *If batter looks unusually stiff after resting -> whisk 1–2 Tbsp water or yogurt into the batter -> it should spread smoothly.*

5. Bake (light metal pan profile)
   - **Bake** at **350°F** for 25–33 minutes.
     - Pour batter into pan and smooth the top.
     - Bake 25–33 minutes, until **evenly golden**, center springs back, and a toothpick in the center comes out clean (a couple moist crumbs are fine) [1].
     - *While this bakes: ensure syrup is fully cooled; prep garnish; set a cooling rack.*
     - **Checkpoint:** Center is set and springy; top is golden (not dark brown).
     - *If the top/edges brown faster than the center sets -> tent loosely with foil -> continue baking 3–8 minutes -> center toothpick is clean.*

6. Syruping: staged absorption (don’t force 100%)
   - **Rest** cake briefly, then **ladle** cooled syrup in passes.
     - Let cake cool in-pan 10 minutes (warm, not blazing hot).
     - Confirm syrup is **cool/room temp** (not warm) [1][2].
     - **Ladle** syrup over the surface in 3–5 passes, pausing 30–60 seconds between passes so it absorbs evenly [2].
     - **Checkpoint:** Syrup level drops steadily; there is no persistent “lake” above the cake.
     - *If syrup pools and won’t drop after 5–10 minutes -> stop adding syrup -> cut the cake into pieces and poke a few holes with a skewer -> pooled syrup starts disappearing.*

7. Overnight set, finish, and taste-adjust
   - **Vent**, **chill**, then serve.
     - Let the syruped cake sit uncovered 20–30 minutes to vent steam, then cover tightly.
     - **Chill** 8–16 hours (overnight) for even distribution and clean slicing [2].
     - Before serving, rest at room temp 45–60 minutes.
     - Taste-adjust loop: acid -> salt -> heat -> herbs.
       - Acid: add a tiny squeeze of lemon to the plate if it tastes flat.
       - Salt: a tiny pinch on top can sharpen sweetness (optional).
       - Heat: next time, simmer a cinnamon stick in the syrup for warmth (remove before cooling).
       - Herbs: mint on the plate is a clean contrast (optional).
     - **Checkpoint:** Slices hold their shape, crumb is moist throughout, sweetness is balanced.

## Common Issues
- **Syrup pooling / “drowning”:** Too much syrup added at once -> pause, let absorb, then continue in passes -> surface looks glossy, not flooded [2].
- **Crumbly/toothsome:** Semolina under-hydrated -> keep the 25–35 minute batter rest -> bite becomes more cohesive [2].
- **Overbrowned edges:** Pan too dark / oven too hot -> use light metal pan or drop temp -> edges match center doneness [4].

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 7: vent 20–30 minutes, then cover and refrigerate.
  - Holds: 3–4 days, tightly covered.
  - Serve/Reheat: serve room temp after 45–60 minutes out of fridge; avoid reheating (can soften structure).
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: after Step 7 (fully soaked + fully chilled).
  - Portioning: slice; wrap pieces individually; store in a freezer bag.
  - Freeze: up to 2 months (best sooner).
  - Thaw: overnight in fridge.
  - Reheat: not recommended; bring to room temp to serve.

## Troubleshooting
- Syrup won’t absorb -> syrup too reduced or cake too cool -> pause syruping, cut/poke channels -> syrup level starts dropping within minutes [2].
- Gummy texture -> syrup/cake too hot at contact -> fully cool syrup and let cake rest 10 minutes -> crumb feels tender after overnight set [1][2].
- Dry perimeter -> slight overbake -> next time pull earlier and/or tent -> edge texture matches the center after soaking [4].

## Geometry Notes
- Pan/pot size used: 9x9-in light-colored metal pan
- Target thickness / layer depth: ~1 to 1.25 in baked height
- Crowding rule: not applicable (single layer)

## Scaling & Batch Size
- Scaling notes: For a 9x13 pan, scale to ~1.5x and start checking at the low end; keep staged syruping.
- Pan swaps: If forced to use a dark pan, reduce oven temp and check earlier to avoid overbrowning [4].

## Special Notes
- Syrup usage: Do not force the last portion of syrup. Use “absorption stops” as your endpoint; extra syrup can be reserved and drizzled per-slice [2].
- Lemon aroma without zest: Lemon peel in syrup gives aroma without zest bits in the crumb [1].

## Variations
1. **Portakallı-style (orange):** Replace the peel and juice in the syrup with orange peel + a splash of orange juice, and swap batter lemon juice for orange juice; the cake reads warmer and more floral-citrus while staying true to the revani format [2].
2. **Haşhaşlı (poppy-seed) revani:** Fold poppy seeds into the batter for a nutty crunch that breaks up the sweetness; keep the same staged syruping so it doesn’t pool [2].
3. **Coconut-pistachio finish:** Sprinkle a thin layer of coconut and pistachio right after syruping so it adheres; it adds contrast and a more “dessert table” vibe [1].
4. **Mini revani squares:** Bake thinner (larger pan) and cut smaller squares; thinner cake absorbs syrup faster and tends to feel less toothsome [2].

## Safety & Correctness Notes
- This version intentionally omits lemon zest and uses peel infusion + measured juice for flavor; zest-in-batter is common in other revani formulas [1].
- Pan color matters: light metal is preferred for even browning; dark pans can overbrown edges without shortening the center bake [4].
