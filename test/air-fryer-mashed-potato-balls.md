# Naked Duchess-Style Truffle Potato Balls (Air Fryer, Make-Ahead)

## Title & Overview
Unbreaded (“naked”) mashed potato balls built on a duchess-potato structure (egg yolk + stiff mash + a little starch) so they brown lightly in an air fryer without coating. Truffle butter is fully integrated: a baseline amount in the mash plus frozen micro-cubes for aromatic pockets. No brushing, glazing, or post-steps required.

- **Cook (Day 2):** 60–90 min total in batches (depends on fryer capacity)

## Ingredients (in use-order)
- 5 lb russet potatoes, peeled and cut into 2-inch chunks
- 2 tbsp kosher salt (for boiling water)
- 6 tbsp unsalted butter
- 8 tbsp black truffle butter, divided:
  - 2 tbsp black truffle butter (in mash)
  - 6 tbsp black truffle butter, cut into 1/4-inch micro-cubes and frozen solid
- 1/3 cup heavy cream, warmed
- 6 large egg yolks
- 1/4 cup potato starch (preferred) or cornstarch
- 2 tsp kosher salt (for seasoning mash), plus more to taste
- 1/2 tsp white pepper (or black pepper), plus more to taste
- 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
- Neutral oil spray (basket + very light mist on balls)

---

## Prep Gotchas & QA
- Steam-dry potatoes until matte before ricing; wet potatoes make gluey mash.
- Chill mash fully before folding in frozen truffle cubes so they stay distinct.
- Keep portions small (~1 1/4-inch); larger balls need longer and flatten more.
- Preheat the air fryer and avoid overcrowding; a faint oil mist boosts browning.
- Cook from frozen for shape consistency; probe a test ball to **165°F** and lock timing.

---

## Detailed Instructions

### Day 1: Build, chill, portion, freeze

1) Freeze the truffle micro-cubes
- Line a plate/tray with parchment.
- Cut 6 tbsp truffle butter into 1/4-inch cubes (pea-sized).
- Freeze until rock-hard, at least 2 hours (overnight ideal).

2) Boil potatoes
- Put potatoes in a large pot and cover with cold water by ~1 inch.
- Add 2 tbsp kosher salt to the water.
- Bring to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer.
- Simmer 15–20 minutes, until a knife slides in with zero resistance and edges look slightly crumbly.

3) Drain and steam-dry (do not skip)
- Drain in a colander for 1 minute.
- Return potatoes to the hot pot over low heat and shake/stir for 90 seconds.
- Cue: visible steam and potato surfaces look drier/matte (not glossy-wet).

4) Rice/mash smooth
- Rice hot potatoes into a large bowl (preferred), or mash until completely smooth.
- Cue: fluffy and dry, not gluey.

5) Build the duchess base (butter + cream + seasoning)
- Add 6 tbsp unsalted butter + 2 tbsp truffle butter to hot potatoes; fold until melted and absorbed.
- Add 1/3 cup warm cream, 2 tsp kosher salt, 1/2 tsp white pepper, and 1/4 tsp nutmeg.
- Mix until uniform.
- Cue: mash mounds (does not pour). If it looks loose, reduce future cream or add 1 Tbsp starch now.

6) Add egg yolks (structure)
- Let mash cool 10 minutes (warm is fine; steaming-hot is not).
- Add 6 egg yolks and mix until fully incorporated.
- Cue: mash becomes glossier and slightly more elastic, but still holds shape.

7) Add starch (stability + better browning)
- Sprinkle in 1/4 cup potato starch (or cornstarch).
- Mix until no dry pockets remain.
- QA: If a spoonful slowly slumps, mix in 1 additional Tbsp starch.

8) Chill to firm
- Cover and refrigerate at least 4 hours (overnight preferred).
- Cue: cold mash scoops cleanly and holds a rounded shape.

9) Fold in frozen truffle micro-cubes
- Work quickly.
- Add frozen micro-cubes and fold 10–15 strokes, just until distributed.
- Cue: distinct cubes remain; if you see smearing, re-chill 15 minutes.

10) Portion and roll
- Scoop ~1 1/2 Tbsp portions (#40 scoop) for ~1 1/4-inch balls.
- Roll smooth with cold hands and place on parchment-lined sheet pan.
- If balls crack while rolling, let mash sit 5 minutes at room temp and continue.

11) Freeze solid (flash-freeze), then store
- Freeze tray at least 2 hours (overnight best).
- Transfer to freezer bag/container, press out air, and keep frozen until Day 2.

---

### Day 2: Air fry in batches and serve (no post-finishing)

12) Preheat air fryer
- Preheat to 375°F for 5 minutes.

13) Load a batch
- Lightly spray basket/tray with neutral oil.
- Arrange frozen balls in a single layer with space (typical 5–6 qt basket: 12–14 balls).
- Mist tops very lightly with oil spray (just a faint sheen).

14) Air fry (repeatable program)
- Cook at 375°F for 15 minutes total.
- At 8 minutes: shake basket or flip with tongs; continue 7 minutes.
- Doneness cues:
  - browned spots; exterior feels set/firm when nudged
  - balls release easily from basket
- Temperature check (recommended):
  - target 165°F at the center of a test ball
  - if 160–164°F: add 2 minutes and re-check
  - if browning is ahead of temperature, run future batches at 365°F for 16–18 minutes (shake at 9)

15) Serve / hold briefly (optional)
- Best: serve immediately as each batch finishes.
- Short hold: keep on a rack over a sheet pan in a 200°F oven for up to 30 minutes.

## Make-Ahead, Storage & Reheat (Optional)
- Day 1: Build, chill, portion, and freeze solid. Chill mash at least 4 hours before rolling; freeze balls at least 2 hours (overnight best) before bagging.
- Storage: Freeze up to 2–3 months for best quality; keep sealed to avoid odor pickup. Refrigerate any Day-1 components within 2 hours.
- Reheat: Air fry from frozen at 375°F per program above; target **165°F** center, add 2–4 minutes if needed; hold finished batches in a 200°F oven up to 30 minutes.

## Serving & Variations
- Serving: Best hot out of the fryer; optional dip (sour cream or aioli) on the side; serve 2–3 per person as an appetizer.
- Size variation: For ~1 1/2-inch balls, start at 375°F for 17–19 minutes (shake at 10), then confirm with a thermometer.
- Flavor tweaks: Increase mash truffle butter from 2 tbsp to 3 tbsp for bigger aroma; swap white pepper for black if preferred.

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** dairy, egg.
- **Swaps:** Dairy-free—use plant butter and coconut cream (texture softer); Egg-free—may not hold shape as well; test a small batch.
- **Cross-contact:** Keep dairy/egg tools separate if serving mixed-allergy groups.

## Scaling & Batch Size
- Recipe yields ~72 (1 1/4-inch) balls. Halve all ingredients for ~36 balls; keep the same air-fry temps but check 1–2 minutes earlier.
- If doubling, air-fry in more batches; freeze on extra sheet pans to avoid crowding/flattening during the hard-freeze step.

## Troubleshooting
| Symptom | Likely Cause | Quick Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Balls flatten | Mash too wet or not frozen hard enough | Steam-dry potatoes longer; add 1–2 Tbsp starch; freeze longer |
| Butter leaks/smokes | Cubes too large or fryer too hot | Use 1/4-inch cubes; reduce to 365°F and extend time; don’t overcrowd |
| Not browning | No preheat, too little oil, or overcrowding | Preheat 5 min; faint oil mist; smaller batches |
| Center not hot | Balls larger than assumed or load too dense | Add 2–4 min; probe and lock timing for remaining batches |
| Truffle too subtle | Mild brand or overcooked | Increase mash truffle butter from 2 tbsp to 3 tbsp; keep cubes the same |

## Safety & Correctness Notes
- Cook from frozen for consistency; preheat air fryer and hit **165°F** at the center of a test ball.
- Chill mash and freeze portions promptly; refrigerate cooked or cooled components within 2 hours.
