# Cold Sour Cream Pound Cake Cups with Jammy Roasted Frozen Strawberries and Mascarpone Whipped Cream

## Title & Overview

These are simplified strawberry shortcake cups built from **scratch vanilla sour cream pound cake**, **jammy roasted frozen strawberries**, and **mascarpone whipped cream**. The cake is scaled for **two 9×5 loaves**, then chilled, cubed, and served cold so it stays soft but structured in small plastic cups. Frozen sliced strawberries remove the hulling step and roast down into a spoonable topping that lightly soaks the cake edges without flooding the cup. The finished dessert eats like a poor-man’s parfait: cold vanilla cake, jammy fruit, rich cream, and no fussy layered-cake handling.

## Yield & Timing

- **Yield:** about 40–48 small dessert cups, using 3–5 oz plastic cups
- **Active Prep:** 80–105 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 6 hours to overnight, mostly cake cooling/chilling
- **Cook:** with two loaf pans: 2 hours 5 minutes to 2 hours 45 minutes total; with one loaf pan: 3 hours 20 minutes to 4 hours 10 minutes total because the cakes should be mixed and baked as two separate half-batches
- **Total:** with two loaf pans: same-day 8–11 hours; with one loaf pan: same-day 10–13 hours
- **Make-ahead:** Pound cakes hold 2–3 days well wrapped at room temperature or 3 months frozen. Roasted strawberry component holds 3 days refrigerated or 3 months frozen. Mascarpone whipped cream is best day-of. Fully assembled cups are best 1–4 hours after assembly and acceptable up to 8 hours refrigerated.

## Ingredients

### Scratch vanilla sour cream pound cakes — makes two 9×5 loaves

- 1 cup plus 6 tbsp unsalted butter, room temperature (310g), plus 2 tbsp softened butter for greasing pans
- 4 cups granulated sugar (800g)
- 2 tbsp vanilla extract
- 8 large eggs, room temperature
- 1 7/8 cups full-fat sour cream, room temperature (450g)
- 4 cups plus 2 tbsp all-purpose flour (490g), plus 2 tbsp for flouring pans
- 2 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 1 tsp Morton kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp baking soda

### Jammy roasted frozen strawberries

- 4 lb frozen sliced strawberries, unsweetened preferred
- 6 tbsp granulated sugar, plus 1–3 tbsp more only if needed after roasting
- 2 tbsp balsamic vinegar
- 1/2 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 1/4 tsp Morton kosher salt
- 1 lemon, zested; reserve 1–2 tbsp juice for final adjustment
- 2 tsp vanilla extract

### Mascarpone whipped cream

- 2 1/2 cups cold heavy cream
- 16 oz mascarpone, softened but still cold
- 1 1/2 cups powdered sugar
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch kosher salt

### Assembly

- 2 chilled vanilla sour cream pound cakes, cut into 1/2-in cubes **[pre-prep]**
- Chilled jammy roasted frozen strawberries **[pre-prep]**
- Mascarpone whipped cream

## Allergy & Dietary Notes

- **Contains:** dairy, wheat/gluten, eggs.
- **Easy swaps keep technique intact:**
  - Balsamic vinegar -> lemon juice -> brighter strawberry flavor, less roasted depth.
  - Mascarpone -> sour cream-stabilized whipped cream -> tangier and lighter, but less rich.
  - Morton kosher salt -> Diamond Crystal kosher salt -> use about half as much Morton by volume because it is denser.
- **Hard swaps not recommended:**
  - Cream cheese pound cake -> changes the cake identity and pushes the dessert toward cheesecake.
  - Store-bought pound cake -> violates the scratch-pound-cake decision.
  - Fresh whole strawberries -> adds hulling/prep back into the workflow.
  - Angel food cake -> too airy and absorbent; turns wet faster in cups.
  - Canned whipped cream -> collapses quickly and does not match the mascarpone-cream decision.
- **Cross-contact note:** Check flour, mascarpone, and other dairy labels if nut/soy cross-contact matters.

## Quick Overview

- **What you are making:** Cold scratch sour cream pound cake cups with jammy roasted frozen strawberries and mascarpone whipped cream.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Pound cakes should be dense-tender and clean-testing; strawberries should be glossy and spoon-coating; mascarpone cream should hold soft-to-medium peaks.
- **Workflow:** Bake the pound cakes first; while the strawberries roast, cube chilled cake, stage cups, and prepare the mascarpone cream.
- **Make-ahead:** Best workflow is pound cakes and strawberries day-before, mascarpone cream and assembly day-of.
- **Primary risks:** Underbaked pound cake, watery frozen-strawberry topping, over-whipped mascarpone cream, or too much strawberry syrup making the cake soggy.

## Gather & Stage

- Bring butter, eggs, and sour cream to room temperature.
- Preheat the Standard oven to **330 deg F** for the pound cakes.
- Grease each Loaf pan — 9×5 metal light with softened butter, then flour it and tap out excess.
- If using two loaf pans, grease and flour both before mixing batter.
- Whisk all-purpose flour, baking soda, and kosher salt together in a bowl.
- Set out Wire racks for cooling.
- Confirm refrigerator space for two wrapped loaves, roasted strawberries, and trays of assembled cups.
- **Checkpoint:** Cake ingredients are room temperature, loaf pan or pans are prepared, dry ingredients are whisked, and cooling space is ready.

## Instructions

1. Make the sour cream pound cake batter  
   - **Cream** the butter, sugar, and vanilla until pale and fluffy.
     - Add room-temperature butter, granulated sugar, and vanilla extract to the KitchenAid Stand Mixer bowl with the flat paddle.
     - **Beat on medium-high speed** for 4–6 minutes, scraping once or twice, until the mixture looks lighter, softer, and visibly aerated.
     - Add eggs one at a time on **medium-low speed**, letting each egg incorporate before adding the next.
     - Scrape the bowl after the fourth and eighth eggs.
     - Add sour cream and **mix on low speed** just until combined.
     - **Checkpoint:** The batter base is pale, thick, creamy, and mostly smooth before the flour goes in.
     - *If the butter/sugar mixture still looks dense and waxy -> beat 1–2 minutes longer -> cue it’s fixed: it looks lighter and softer.*
     - *If the batter looks slightly curdled after the eggs -> continue with the dry ingredients -> cue it’s fixed: the batter smooths out once flour is incorporated.*

2. Add the dry ingredients  
   - **Fold** in the flour mixture gently to avoid a tough crumb.
     - Add the whisked flour, baking soda, and salt.
     - **Mix on low speed** only until most of the flour disappears.
     - Stop the mixer while faint flour streaks remain.
     - Finish folding with a flexible silicone spatula, or a large spoon as fallback, scraping the bottom and sides of the bowl.
     - **Checkpoint:** Batter is thick and smooth, with no dry flour pockets and no unmixed butter at the bottom.
     - *If you see flour streaks at the bottom -> fold by hand, not with more mixer speed -> cue it’s fixed: no white streaks remain.*
     - *If the batter gets stretchy or elastic -> stop mixing immediately -> cue it’s fixed: batter is just combined and not worked further.*

3. Bake two 9×5 pound cakes  
   - **Bake** the batter as two loaves.
     - **Two-pan path:** Divide batter evenly between two prepared Loaf pan — 9×5 metal light pans.
     - **One-pan path:** Do not let a full double batch wait through a bake cycle. Use the one-loaf half-batch quantities in **Scaling & Batch Size**, bake the first loaf, then mix and bake the second loaf fresh.
     - Each pan should be about two-thirds to three-quarters full.
     - Smooth the top of each batter portion and tap the pan once or twice to release large air pockets.
     - **Bake at 330 deg F** for 70–85 minutes per loaf, until the top is deep golden, the center crack looks set, and a Paring knife inserted into the center comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs.
     - If the top browns before the center is done, tent loosely with foil after 50–55 minutes.
     - **Checkpoint:** Each loaf is domed, cracked on top, golden, and clean-testing in the center.
     - *If the center is wet but the top is dark -> tent with foil and bake 8–12 minutes longer -> cue it’s fixed: the Paring knife comes out without wet batter.*
     - *If the sides are browning fast in a darker pan -> reduce oven temperature by 25 deg F and continue baking -> cue it’s fixed: edges stop darkening before the center finishes.*

4. Cool and chill the pound cakes  
   - **Cool** the cakes completely, then chill before cubing.
     - Let each loaf cool in the pan on Wire racks for 15–20 minutes.
     - Loosen the sides, turn out carefully, and cool completely on Wire racks.
     - Wrap cooled loaves tightly.
     - **Chill** at least 2 hours, preferably overnight, before cubing.
     - **Checkpoint:** Pound cake is fully cold and firm enough to cut into clean cubes.
     - *If the cake tears while cutting -> chill 30–60 minutes longer -> cue it’s fixed: cubes cut cleanly with defined edges.*

5. Roast the frozen strawberry layer  
   - **Roast** frozen sliced strawberries uncovered until their released water reduces into glossy syrup.
     - Increase oven to **375 deg F**.
     - Divide frozen sliced strawberries between the Baking pan — 13×9 glass and Sheet / baking tray — 13×9 metal light.
     - Add 3 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar, 1/4 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt or 1/8 tsp Morton kosher salt, and half the lemon zest to each pan.
     - Spread berries as evenly as possible; frozen clumps are fine at the start.
     - **Bake uncovered at 375 deg F** for 50–70 minutes, stirring every 15–20 minutes, until the berries collapse and the liquid changes from watery to glossy and syrupy.
     - Start checking the metal tray 5–10 minutes earlier than the glass pan.
     - Stir 1 tsp vanilla extract into each pan after roasting.
     - **Checkpoint:** The strawberry component is spoonable and loose-jammy, not berry soup.
     - *If the berries are still watery -> roast 8–12 minutes longer uncovered -> cue it’s fixed: bubbling slows and syrup coats a spoon.*
     - *If the syrup gets sticky or candy-like -> stir in 1–2 tsp warm water or lemon juice -> cue it’s fixed: sauce flows slowly from a spoon.*

6. Cool and adjust the strawberries  
   - **Chill** the roasted strawberries completely before assembly.
     - Let pans sit 10–15 minutes, just until aggressive bubbling stops.
     - Transfer berries and syrup to shallow containers.
     - Mash about one-third of the berries with a spoon, leaving the rest soft but visible.
     - **Chill** at least 1 hour, until fully cold.
     - Taste-adjust loop: acid -> salt -> sweetness -> vanilla.
       - Add lemon juice if the berries taste flat.
       - Add a tiny pinch of salt if they taste sweet but dull.
       - Add 1–3 tbsp sugar only if sharply tart.
       - Add a few drops of vanilla if the roasted flavor tastes harsh.
     - **Checkpoint:** The strawberry component is cold, jammy, and thick enough to mound slightly on a spoon.
     - *If the chilled sauce separates into thin juice and fruit -> stir well and spoon off excess thin liquid -> cue it’s fixed: fruit and syrup cling together.*

7. Make the mascarpone whipped cream  
   - **Whip** mascarpone cream until it holds soft-to-medium peaks.
     - Add cold heavy cream, powdered sugar, vanilla extract, and a pinch of kosher salt to the KitchenAid Stand Mixer bowl with the wire whisk.
     - **Whip on medium-high speed** for 1–3 minutes, until soft peaks form.
     - Add cold but spreadable mascarpone in several spoonfuls.
     - **Whip on medium speed** just until incorporated and thickened.
     - Stop at soft-to-medium peaks; do not push to stiff, grainy peaks.
     - **Checkpoint:** The cream mounds cleanly on a spoon and holds shape without looking broken.
     - *If mascarpone stays lumpy -> pause and press lumps against the bowl with a flexible silicone spatula, or the back of a large spoon, then whip briefly -> cue it’s fixed: cream looks smooth.*
     - *If cream turns grainy -> fold in 1–2 tbsp cold heavy cream by hand -> cue it’s fixed: texture looks smoother and less broken.*

8. Cube the cold pound cakes  
   - **Cut** chilled pound cake into small cubes.
     - Cut only as much cake as you need for the number of cups you are assembling.
     - Target 1/2-in cubes.
     - For each 3–5 oz cup, use about 1/4 cup cake cubes.
     - Keep unused cake wrapped and cold.
     - **Checkpoint:** Cake cubes are cold, clean-cut, and not compressed.
     - *If you are not using both loaves immediately -> wrap the second loaf tightly and refrigerate or freeze -> cue it’s fixed: cake stays moist and sliceable for later.*

9. Assemble the plastic cups  
   - **Layer** each cup with cold pound cake, chilled jammy strawberries, and mascarpone whipped cream.
     - Add 4–6 cubes cold pound cake to each cup.
     - Spoon **1 to 1 1/2 tbsp** cold jammy strawberries over the cake.
     - Add **2 tbsp** mascarpone whipped cream on top.
     - Finish with a tiny dab of strawberry syrup or one visible strawberry piece.
     - Do not compress the layers.
     - **Checkpoint:** Each cup has a visible cake base, a defined strawberry layer, and cream that sits on top rather than sinking.
     - *If the strawberry layer floods the cake immediately -> use less syrup and more berry solids in the next cups -> cue it’s fixed: cake edges glisten but cubes remain distinct.*

10. Chill and serve  
   - **Chill** assembled cups until cold and lightly settled.
     - Refrigerate 1–4 hours before serving.
     - Serve directly from the refrigerator.
     - For party service, put out one tray at a time and keep extras refrigerated.
     - Final taste-adjust loop: acid -> salt -> sweetness -> vanilla. Adjust only reserved strawberry topping or mascarpone cream, not already-assembled cups.
     - **Checkpoint:** Final cups are cold, spoonable, lightly soaked at the cake edges, and still visibly layered.

## Common Issues

- **Pound cake is gummy in the middle:** Underbaked loaf or overmixed batter -> bake longer and stop mixing earlier after flour -> center tests clean and crumb is dense-tender.
- **Pound cake crumbles when cubed:** Cake was too warm or not rested long enough -> chill before cutting -> cubes hold clean edges.
- **Watery strawberry layer:** Frozen berries were not reduced enough -> roast longer uncovered -> syrup coats a spoon before chilling.
- **Mascarpone cream breaks or turns grainy:** Over-whipped or mascarpone too cold/lumpy -> fold in cold cream and stop sooner next time -> cream looks smooth and moundable.
- **Cake turns mushy in cups:** Too much strawberry syrup or too much hold time -> use 1 to 1 1/2 tbsp strawberry layer per cup -> cake remains visibly cubed.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 4: Wrap cooled pound cakes tightly.
  - Holds: 2–3 days at room temperature; chill before cubing.
  - Stop after Step 6: Refrigerate roasted strawberry component in covered containers.
  - Holds: 3 days.
  - Stop after Step 7: Mascarpone whipped cream holds best same-day; refrigerate up to 24 hours, then briefly re-whisk by hand if needed.
  - Stop after Step 9: Fully assembled cups hold best 1–4 hours and are acceptable up to 8 hours refrigerated.
  - Serve: cold, directly from the refrigerator.

- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: wrapped pound cake loaves and roasted strawberry component separately.
  - Portioning: freeze whole loaves or half-loaves; freeze strawberries in shallow containers or bags.
  - Freeze: up to 3 months for best quality.
  - Thaw: thaw pound cake wrapped in the refrigerator overnight; thaw strawberries overnight in the refrigerator.
  - Texture reset: stir thawed strawberries. If watery, **Reduce** in Stainless steel pots and pans over **medium-low heat** for 3–6 minutes, until spoon-coating, then chill again before assembly.

## Troubleshooting

- Pound cake sticks in pan -> pan was under-greased/floured or unmolded at the wrong time -> grease and flour thoroughly and cool 15–20 minutes before unmolding -> loaf releases with intact sides.
- Pound cake is dense and rubbery -> batter was overmixed after flour or ingredients were too cold -> mix less after dry ingredients and use room-temperature dairy/eggs -> crumb is dense-tender, not elastic.
- Strawberry layer is too loose -> frozen strawberries released more water than expected -> roast uncovered 8–12 minutes longer or briefly reduce on the stove -> syrup coats a spoon.
- Mascarpone cream is lumpy -> mascarpone was too cold or added in large chunks -> soften slightly while still cold and whip briefly at medium speed -> cream looks smooth and cohesive.

## Troubleshooting Table

| Symptom              | Likely Cause                              | Quick Fix                                    |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Wet cake center      | Underbaked pound cake                     | Tent and bake 8–12 minutes longer            |

## Geometry Notes

- Pound cake pan used: Loaf pan — 9×5 metal light.
- Pound cake batch size: two 9×5 loaves, using the larger-loaf sour cream pound cake scale.
- Strawberry pans used: Baking pan — 13×9 glass plus Sheet / baking tray — 13×9 metal light.
- Target strawberry layer depth: shallow; frozen slices may overlap but should not form a deep pile.
- Cup size: 3–5 oz plastic cups.
- Target per-cup fill:
  - 1/4 cup cold scratch sour cream pound cake cubes
  - 1 to 1 1/2 tbsp jammy roasted frozen strawberries
  - 2 tbsp mascarpone whipped cream
- Crowding rule: Do not pile 4 lb frozen strawberries into one 13×9 pan. Use two pans so the berries roast/reduce instead of steaming.

## Scaling & Batch Size

- Scaling notes:
  - Full batch makes two 9×5 pound cakes and enough topping/cream for about 40–48 small cups.
  - Half batch makes one 9×5 loaf, about 20–24 cups’ worth of cake.
- One-loaf half-batch quantities:
  - 11 tbsp unsalted butter, room temperature (155g), plus 1 tbsp for pan
  - 2 cups granulated sugar (400g)
  - 1 tbsp vanilla extract
  - 4 large eggs, room temperature
  - 1 cup minus 1 tbsp full-fat sour cream, room temperature (225g)
  - 2 cups plus 1 tbsp all-purpose flour (245g), plus 1 tbsp for pan
  - 1 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 1/2 tsp Morton kosher salt
  - 1/4 tsp baking soda
- Batch strategy:
  - With two loaf pans, make the full batter once and bake both loaves together.
  - With one loaf pan, make one half-batch, bake it, cool/clean/grease the pan, then make the second half-batch fresh.
  - Assemble only the number of cups needed for service.
  - Keep extra cake, strawberries, and mascarpone cream separate and cold.
- Pan swaps:
  - Sheet / baking tray — 13×9 metal light for strawberries: check 5–10 minutes earlier; syrup may reduce faster.
  - Dark metal 9×9 tray for strawberries: reduce oven temperature by about 25 deg F or check 5 minutes early.
  - Glass loaf pan is not recommended here; if used, expect slower heat transfer and verify doneness with the Paring knife rather than time alone.

## Special Notes

- Storage & service: Keep assembled cups refrigerated. Perishable foods should not sit at room temperature more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour above 90 deg F [9].
- Cake usage: Two pound cakes may make more cake than you need depending on cup size and cube size. Freeze extra loaf or extra cubes tightly wrapped.
- Sweetness control: The pound cake and mascarpone cream are sweet, so the strawberry layer should stay tangy-jammy rather than candy-sweet.
- Best schedule:
  - Day before: bake pound cakes; roast strawberries.
  - Day of: cube chilled cake; make mascarpone whipped cream; assemble cups 1–4 hours before serving.

## Variations

1. **More classic strawberry cup:** Replace balsamic vinegar with lemon juice. The strawberry layer will taste brighter and closer to traditional strawberry shortcake.

2. **More adult roasted-balsamic cup:** Increase balsamic vinegar to 3 tbsp total and add a tiny pinch of black pepper after roasting. The berry layer becomes deeper, tangier, and less candy-like.

3. **Extra-low-sog cup assembly:** Put a thin layer of mascarpone cream under the cake, then cake, strawberries, and more cream. This creates a small fat barrier, but it is slightly fussier.

4. **One-loaf trial batch:** Make the half batch, use 2 lb frozen strawberries, and halve the mascarpone whipped cream. This is the best test run before making 40–48 cups.

## Safety & Correctness Notes

The pound cake must be fully baked before cooling and chilling; cold cake cuts cleaner, but chilling will not fix an underbaked center. Frozen strawberries release more water than fresh strawberries, so the recipe uses divided pans, a long uncovered roast, and a spoon-coating cue before chilling. Mascarpone whipped cream and assembled cups should stay refrigerated until service.
