# Qeema Daliya Pucks (Broken Wheat + Keema + Peas)

## Title & Overview
This is a freezer-friendly, South-Asian-style keema bowl that sets up thick enough to freeze into 2-cup silicone "pucks" and reheat cleanly in the microwave. It uses broken wheat (daliya, approximated well by dry bulgur) as the built-in complex carb, plus peas and a tomato-forward masala for a cohesive, scoopable texture. The workflow is optimized for batch size: brown the beef in a skillet for real flavor, then finish the full volume in a stock pot so it simmers evenly and thickens without scorching. The "bhuna" step (spice + tomato paste cooked until glossy) is the key to depth without needing long, stew-like reduction. [2]

## Prep Gotchas & QA
- Toast the daliya until nutty, then pull it off heat immediately to avoid scorching carryover.
- Brown beef in true batches; crowding steams and flattens flavor.
- Bhuna the tomato paste + spices until glossy and cohesive; if it sticks, splash water to keep it moving.
- Once daliya is in, stir every 2 to 3 minutes while thickening to prevent sticking on the pot bottom.
- Target texture before molding: thick, scoopable, loose risotto vibe—not soupy and not stiff.
- Cool in shallow pans and refrigerate within 2 hours before freezing; reheat pucks to **165°F** with a short stand time.

## Detailed Instructions
1) Mise en place (do not skip for this batch size).
   - Dice onions and carrots. Mince garlic, ginger, and chiles.
   - Mix the dry spices (cumin, coriander, paprika, garam masala, turmeric, cayenne, black pepper) in a bowl so you can add them all at once.

2) Dry-toast the daliya (helps keep it nutty and less mushy).
   - In a large stock pot (8+ qt) over medium heat, add daliya/bulgur dry and toast, stirring frequently, 6 to 8 minutes until it smells nutty and slightly deepens in color.
   - Immediately pour into a bowl to stop cooking. [1]

3) Brown the beef in the skillet (batching is mandatory at this volume).
   - Heat your deep-walled skillet over medium-high. Add about 1 tsp avocado oil only if the pan is very dry.
   - Add beef in 2 to 3 batches (about 1 to 1 1/4 lb per batch). Spread it out, leave it alone 2 to 3 minutes to brown, then break it up and continue cooking 5 to 7 minutes until deeply browned with some crisp edges (not gray).
   - Transfer each batch into the stock pot as it finishes. Leave the browned bits (fond) in the skillet.

4) Cook the aromatics in the same skillet.
   - Lower heat to medium. Add remaining avocado oil.
   - Add onions + carrots + a pinch of your measured salt. Cook 10 to 12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until onions are fully softened and just starting to pick up light brown color.
   - Add garlic, ginger, and chiles; cook 60 to 90 seconds until very fragrant.

5) Bhuna the masala base (this is where the depth comes from).
   - Add tomato paste and the pre-mixed spices to the skillet.
   - Cook 1 to 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until the paste darkens slightly and the mixture looks glossy/oily and cohesive (not dusty). If it threatens to stick, splash in 2 to 4 tbsp water and keep going. [2]

6) Build the full pot.
   - Scrape the skillet mixture into the stock pot with the beef (use a splash of water to fully dissolve the fond).
   - Add crushed tomatoes and 8 cups water (or water/broth blend).
   - Bring to a boil, then reduce to an active simmer.

7) Simmer, then add peas + toasted daliya.
   - Simmer uncovered 10 to 15 minutes to meld flavors and slightly reduce.
   - Stir in frozen peas and the toasted daliya.
   - Maintain a steady simmer 18 to 25 minutes, stirring every 2 to 3 minutes once it starts thickening (daliya can settle and scorch). You want daliya tender (no chalky center) and the mixture thick enough that a spoon leaves a trail.

8) Final thickness targeting for "pucks" (important).
   - Turn off heat and rest 10 minutes. The daliya will continue absorbing liquid and firm up.
   - Goal texture: thick and scoopable, like a loose risotto or thick chili, not soupy.
   - If too loose: simmer 5 to 10 more minutes, stirring often.
   - If too tight: stir in hot water 1/2 cup at a time.

9) Portioning and freezing into 2-cup molds.
   - For food safety and better pucks, cool fast: spread the mixture in one or two wide, shallow pans (or two large sheet pans with rims) for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring once or twice to vent steam. [3]
   - Pack into 2-cup silicone molds, pressing down to remove air pockets and ensure a flat top.
   - Refrigerate until fully cold, then freeze. (Once solid, you can pop pucks out and bag them to save space.)

10) Microwave reheat (from frozen).
   - Unmold a puck into a microwave-safe bowl. Cover (vented).
   - Microwave on High 4 minutes, stir thoroughly, then 2 to 4 minutes more until the center reaches 165 deg F.
   - Let stand 1 minute (standing time matters for microwaved foods). [4][3]

- Make-Ahead Map: Do Steps 1-8 on prep day; Steps 9-10 are your freeze-and-reheat workflow for the next 2 to 3 months.

## Serving & Variations
- Serving ideas: Eat as-is; designed to be complete. Optional no-cook add-ons: plain yogurt or sliced cucumber.
- Dietary toggles (kept from original):
   - Low-sodium: use no-salt-added tomatoes, skip broth, reduce salt by 25 to 40%, and add heat via chiles/cayenne instead of salt.
   - Higher-protein: use 93/7 beef (or increase beef by 1 lb and reduce daliya by 1 cup; simmer a few minutes longer to re-thicken).
   - Low-FODMAP-ish: replace onions/garlic with the green tops of scallions + garlic-infused oil; keep portions modest (wheat is not low-FODMAP).
   - Gluten-free: replace daliya with cooked brown rice or cooked quinoa stirred in at the end (texture will be softer; pucks still work, but simmer timing changes).

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** wheat (daliya/bulgur), beef; optionally dairy if serving with yogurt.
- **Swaps:** For gluten-free, use cooked brown rice or quinoa stirred in at the end; for dairy-free toppings, skip yogurt.
- **Cross-contact:** Use separate boards/utensils for raw beef; keep gluten and gluten-free gear separate if making a GF batch.

## Scaling & Batch Size
- Base batch yields about 10 × 2-cup pucks (20 cups). If halving, reduce pot size, keep stirring frequency, and check doneness a few minutes earlier after adding daliya.
- If increasing, ensure wider surface area (two pots or a larger rondeau) to prevent scorching; maintain frequent stirring once thickening starts.

## Troubleshooting
| Symptom | Likely Cause | Quick Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too loose/soupy for pucks | Not reduced enough or not rested | Simmer 5–10 more minutes, stirring; rest 10 minutes to tighten |
| Too thick/dry | Over-reduced or extra absorption during rest | Stir in hot water 1/2 cup at a time to loosen |
| Scorching at bottom | Stirring too infrequent once thick; heat too high | Lower heat, stir every 2–3 minutes, scrape bottom; transfer to wider pot if needed |
| Daliya undercooked/chalky | Simmer time too short after daliya addition | Simmer a few more minutes with a splash of hot water, stirring |
| Flat flavor | Bhuna not cooked enough or low salt/acid | Cook bhuna to glossy; add small pinches of salt and a squeeze of lemon if desired |

## Safety & Correctness Notes
- Cool in shallow pans and refrigerate within 2 hours (1 hour if above 90°F); then freeze once cold. [3]
- Reheat refrigerated or frozen portions to at least **165 deg F** and include a 1-minute stand time for microwave reheats. [3][4]
