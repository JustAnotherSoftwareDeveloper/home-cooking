# Overnight Spinach & Cheese Strata (9x13)

## Overview
Custardy, savory bread pudding loaded with spinach, onions, and melty cheese. Assemble the night before; bake the morning of. Slices cleanly for a buffet and holds well.

## Yield & Timing
- Yield: 12 generous squares (up to 16 smaller portions)
- Active time: 25-35 minutes
- Chill/soak: 8-24 hours (overnight)
- Bake time: 50-60 minutes + 10-15 minutes rest

## Equipment

## Ingredients (in use-order)
- 1 lb day-old crusty bread, cut in 1" cubes (about 10-12 cups loosely packed)
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter (for sauteing)
- 1 large yellow onion, finely diced
- 2-3 cloves garlic, minced (optional)
- 16 oz frozen chopped spinach, thawed and squeezed very dry
- 8 oz Gruyere or sharp white cheddar, shredded (about 2 cups), divided
- 3 oz Parmesan, finely grated (about 3/4 cup), divided
- 10 large eggs
- 3 cups whole milk
- 1 cup half-and-half (or heavy cream)
- 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 2 tsp kosher salt (use 1 1/4 tsp if Morton)
- 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- Pinch ground nutmeg (optional)
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, melted (for drizzling top)
- Red pepper flakes, a pinch (optional)

---

## Detailed Instructions

1) Prep the bread (5-10 min)
   - If bread is very fresh/soft, dry it: spread cubes on a sheet pan and toast at 300 F for 10-15 minutes until just dry at the edges (not browned). Cool.
   - If day-old and dry, skip to Step 2.

2) Saute aromatics (8-10 min)
   - In a large skillet over medium heat, melt 4 tbsp butter. Add onion and 1/2 tsp salt; cook, stirring, until translucent and lightly golden at edges, 6-8 minutes.
   - Add garlic (if using) and cook 30-60 seconds until fragrant. Remove from heat; cool 5 minutes.

3) Dry the spinach (5 min)
   - Place thawed spinach in a clean towel or paper towels; wring out as much liquid as possible. (Really squeeze -- excess moisture can make the strata watery.)

4) Build the strata (5-7 min)
   - Grease a 9x13 inch dish with butter or oil.
   - Layer 1: Spread half the bread cubes. Top with 1/2 the onions, 1/2 the spinach, 1/2 the Gruyere/cheddar, and 1/3 of the Parmesan.
   - Layer 2: Repeat with remaining bread, onions, spinach, and most of the remaining Gruyere/cheddar (reserve a small handful for topping). Sprinkle another 1/3 of the Parmesan.
   - Lightly toss layers with your hands so fillings are evenly nestled among cubes.

5) Whisk the custard (3-4 min)
   - In a large bowl, whisk eggs, milk, half-and-half, Dijon, remaining 1 1/2 tsp salt (or 3/4 tsp if you used Morton in Step 2), pepper, and nutmeg (if using) until smooth.

6) Soak overnight (8-24 hours)
   - Pour custard evenly over the pan, pressing gently with a spatula to help bread absorb.
   - Cover tightly with foil and refrigerate 8-24 hours.

Day-of Bake

7) Preheat & finish topping (10 min preheat)
   - Remove the pan from the fridge while the oven heats to 350 F (rack centered).
   - Uncover, scatter reserved Gruyere/cheddar and remaining 1/3 Parmesan over the surface; drizzle 2 tbsp melted butter over the top. Add a pinch of red pepper flakes if you like a mild kick.

8) Bake (50-60 min)
   - Cover loosely with foil for the first 30 minutes to prevent over-browning, then uncover and bake 20-30 minutes more, until:
     - the center puffs, edges are browned and crisp, and
     - an instant-read thermometer inserted in the middle reads at least 165 F, or a knife inserted near center comes out mostly clean.

9) Rest & serve (10-15 min)
   - Let rest to set, then cut into 12-16 squares. Serve warm; it also holds nicely at room temp on a buffet for about 1 hour.

## Troubleshooting
- Watery/loose center: Spinach was not squeezed dry; bread too fresh/soft; underbaked. Fix: Bake longer (tent with foil if browning); next time dry bread/toast briefly and wring spinach hard.
- Overly browned top before center sets: Tent with foil; consider lowering rack one notch.
- Bland result: Under-salted custard or mild cheese. Fix: Use sharp cheddar or Gruyere; keep Dijon; salt to taste (remember Parmesan adds salt).
- Rubbery/over-set texture: Overbaked or too little dairy. Fix: Pull at 165-175 F in center; keep milk/cream ratio.
- Soggy bottom: Bread packed too tightly or insufficient soak. Fix: Lightly toss layers; press bread into custard; allow full overnight soak.

## Notes & Variations
- Cheese swaps: Fontina, Comte, or a 50/50 sharp cheddar-Gruyere blend all work.
- Add veg: Sauteed mushrooms (8 oz) or roasted butternut cubes (2 cups; fully cooked) can replace half the spinach.
- Meaty version: Fold in 6-8 oz cooked, crumbled bacon or breakfast sausage (reduce salt in custard by 1/4-1/2 tsp).
- Heat level: A pinch of red pepper flakes or a minced jalapeno sauteed with onions keeps it brunch-mild but interesting.
- Scale up: Double the recipe into two 9x13 pans or one 13x18 (3/4-1 inch deep) sheet-pan casserole; start checking doneness around 40-45 minutes for a shallower pan.
- Hold & reheat: Keeps warm (loosely covered) in a 200 F oven up to 45 minutes. Reheat leftovers at 325 F, 15-20 minutes, until hot.
- Dietary pivots: Use sturdy gluten-free bread; swap in lactose-free whole milk and lactose-free cheddar; or make it fully dairy-free with oat milk + dairy-free cheeses (texture will be slightly less custardy).

---

## Safety/Correctness
- Bake to at least 165 F in the center for egg safety.
- Cool leftovers promptly; refrigerate up to 3 days. Reheat to 165 F.
- Freeze (tightly wrapped) up to 2 months; thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat covered at 325 F until hot.
