# Hamilton Beach 29890 French Bread Loaf (2 lb) — Less Dense, Better Release

## Title & Overview
This is the official Hamilton Beach 2-lb French Bread formula for the 29890, with two “real-world” upgrades that bread-machine users rely on: (1) weigh flour/water to avoid accidental dryness (dense loaf), and (2) do a quick mid-knead dough check so you can correct hydration before it’s too late. The result should be a light, sliceable loaf with a crisper crust than basic white bread. If your pan/paddle tends to stick, the workflow below bakes in release tactics.

## Yield & Timing
- Yield: 1 loaf, 2 lb / ~907 g (about 12 servings) [2]
- Active Prep: 5 to 10 minutes
- Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest: ~3 hours 33 minutes (machine does this) [2]
- Cook: included in cycle time
- Total: ~3 hours 40 minutes + cooling time
- Make-ahead: Cool fully, then bag. Room temp 2 to 3 days [4]. Freeze sliced bread for longer storage; Hamilton Beach notes bread can be frozen up to 6 months [2].

## Ingredients
(2 lb French cycle loaf — official ratios) [1][2]
- Water, 1 1/2 cups (360 g), lukewarm [2]
- Salt, 1 1/2 tsp (9 g) [2]
- Sugar, 1 1/2 tsp (6 g) [2]
- Bread flour, 4 1/2 cups (540 g) [2]
- Bread machine yeast, 2 tsp (7 g) [2]

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- Contains: wheat/gluten
- Easy swaps (keep technique intact): none recommended for this lean formula
- Hard swaps (not recommended / major change): All-purpose flour -> lower structure / different absorption -> increases risk of density unless you re-tune hydration [2][4]

## Quick Overview
- What you are making: A 2-lb “French bread” loaf baked directly in the machine—lean dough, crispier crust than basic [2].
- Texture and doneness cues: During knead you want a smooth, soft, slightly tacky dough ball (not crumbly; not soupy) [2][3].
- Workflow: Load ingredients → start French Cycle (Cycle 2) → do a 5–10 minute dough check and correct → let machine finish → cool before slicing.
- Make-ahead: Best same-day; freeze slices for longer storage [2][4].
- Primary risks: Dense loaf from excess flour / too little water [2]; sticking from paddle/pan issues [2].

## Instructions

1. Prep the pan and paddle
   - Make sure the kneading paddle is seated correctly.
   - If you’ve had sticking: wipe a thin film of neutral oil on the paddle (and around the paddle shaft area) after cleaning. Users report this reduces sticking dramatically. [6]

2. Load ingredients (in this order)
   - Add to the bread pan in order: water → salt → sugar → bread flour → yeast [1][2]
   - Optional but smart: create a small “well” in the flour and put yeast in that well so it stays dry until mixing begins (especially helpful if your kitchen is humid). [5]

3. Select settings and start
   - Choose French Bread / Cycle 2, 2.0 lb loaf size, and crust shade.
     - Hamilton Beach recommends Dark for this recipe [2].
     - If you’re fighting sticking or an overly thick crust: start with Medium and only go darker once release is reliable. [2]
   - Press Start. The 2 lb French cycle runs about 3:33. [2]

4. Dough check (this is the density fixer)
   - At about 5–10 minutes into kneading, open the lid and look at the dough. Checking during kneading is encouraged; don’t open during bake. [3]
   - You want a single cohesive dough ball that looks soft and supple (not shredded/dry; not batter). [2][3]
     - If dough is dry/crumbly: add water 1 Tbsp at a time until it becomes smooth and elastic. Hamilton Beach explicitly recommends adding water in 1 Tbsp increments until you reach a soft, supple dough. [2]
     - If dough is wet/smearing: add flour 1 Tbsp at a time until it stops puddling and starts forming a ball. [3]

5. Let the machine finish
   - Once the dough looks right, close the lid and let the cycle run.
   - Optional (paddle hole / sticking mitigation): some users remove the paddle after the final knead and put the dough back for the last rise + bake, but timing varies by machine and can backfire. If you try it, do it experimentally and write down the timing. [6]

6. Release the loaf cleanly
   - When done, use oven mitts and remove the pan. Let it cool 5–10 minutes on a wire rack before removing the loaf. [2]
   - Invert pan and gently shake.
   - If it doesn’t release:
     - Use a thin rubber/silicone spatula around the sides. [2]
     - If it still won’t release: wiggle the paddle from the bottom (carefully, hot) until the loaf loosens. [6]

7. Cool, slice, and “taste-adjust” for next time
   - Cool on a rack before slicing; cutting hot bread can mash the crumb. [2]
   - Taste and log one adjustment for your next loaf:
     - If it’s dense: you were likely too dry → weigh flour (540 g) and do the Step 4 water correction earlier next time. [2][4]
     - If it’s bland: increase salt slightly next loaf (e.g., +1/4 tsp).
     - If it’s too crusty / sticks more: use a lighter crust setting and remove the loaf promptly. [2]

## Common Issues
- Loaf is dense/heavy: Too much flour or not enough water -> weigh flour/water and correct hydration during knead -> dough becomes soft and supple. [2][4]
- Crust/paddle sticks: Release timing + worn nonstick + paddle adhesion -> rest 5–10 min, spatula edge, wiggle paddle, consider a thin oil film on paddle/shaft -> loaf releases without tearing. [2][6]
- Crust too dark: Too much sugar or crust setting too dark -> choose a lighter crust option -> crust is thinner and less tough. [2]

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Hamilton Beach notes cooled bread can be stored in a bag/foil and refrigerated up to 10 days, but many bakers prefer room-temp short-term because refrigeration can stale bread. [2][4]
- Freezer plan:
  - Slice, bag, and freeze. Hamilton Beach notes bread can be frozen up to 6 months. [2]

## Troubleshooting
- Dense loaf -> Excess flour / dry dough -> weigh flour and add water 1 Tbsp at a time during knead -> smooth dough ball forms and loaf rises better. [2][4]
- Loaf won’t come out -> Paddle/pan adhesion -> rest 5–10 min, rubber spatula, wiggle paddle from below -> loaf releases cleanly. [2][6]
- Paddle stuck in pan after removal -> Dough residue in paddle hub -> soak pan with warm water (HB suggests ~15 minutes) and work paddle loose -> paddle frees without force. [2]

## Scaling & Batch Size
Use the same French Bread / Cycle 2 but select loaf size and use the corresponding ingredient set from the 29890 manual. [2]

## Variations
1. Oven-finished baguette (less “blocky”): Use your bread machine to make the dough, then shape into baguettes and bake in the oven; BreadDad’s method is designed to avoid the “block loaf” limitation while still relying on the machine for mixing and rising. [5]
2. Softer “Italian-ish” crust: Add a small amount of fat (oil/butter); users report this pushes it away from classic “French” and toward a softer loaf. [5]
