# Classic Baked Nian Gao Slab with Red Date Chunks and Light Coconut

## Title & Overview
This is a classic baked nian gao in slab form, cut into chewy, mochi-like squares. Flavor is caramel-brown sugar with coconut richness. The topping plan balances authenticity and taste: **red dates (jujubes)** are the primary Lunar New Year cue, but instead of decorative halves/strips, you’ll distribute **date chunks** throughout (plus a visible scatter on top). A **light shredded coconut finish** adds aroma without overwhelming the cake.

Built for a **bring-over**: sturdy at room temp, slices cleanly after cooling, and reheats easily.

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 1 (9x13) slab; about 24 small squares (2-inch) or 12 larger squares
- **Active Prep:** 25–35 minutes
- **Cook:** 55–75 minutes
- **Rest / Cooling:** 2–4 hours (important for clean slices)
- **Total:** ~1:30–2:00 plus cooling
- **Make-ahead:**
  - Room temp airtight: 2–3 days
  - Refrigerated: 5–7 days (firmer chew)
  - Frozen: 2–3 months (wrap squares individually)

## Ingredients

### Pan + Toppings
- Cooking spray or neutral oil, for pan
- **10–14 dried red dates (jujubes), pitted**
  - Target output: **about 1/2 cup loosely packed date chunks**
  - Reserve: **2 Tbsp** chunks for the top
- **2–3 Tbsp glutinous rice flour (from the recipe), for dusting date chunks**
- **1/3 cup unsweetened shredded coconut or coconut flakes** (light but noticeable)
  - If using sweetened coconut: use 1/4 cup and add later (see Step 4).

### Batter
- 3 large eggs
- 1 cup packed dark brown sugar
- 1 cup full-fat canned coconut milk, well shaken
- 1 1/2 cups whole milk
- 1/2 cup neutral oil
- 2 cups glutinous rice flour (“sweet rice flour”)
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp baking powder (optional; skip for denser, more traditional chew)

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** dairy, egg, coconut
- Glutinous rice flour is typically gluten-free, but check packaging if cross-contact matters.

## Prep Gotchas & QA
- This cake **sets as it cools**. Cutting early is the #1 way to mistake it for underbaked.
- Use a **parchment sling + grease** or you’ll tear the slab.
- Coconut can scorch if added too early; add it mid-bake (or later if sweetened).

## Detailed Instructions

### A) Read Once (Plan)
- Make smooth batter → fold in date chunks at the end → bake until center is set (no wet batter) → add coconut mid-bake → cool fully → slice.
- Success cues:
  - Batter: thick pancake batter, pourable.
  - Doneness: skewer shows **no wet batter** (tacky crumbs ok).
  - Final set: noticeably firmer after 2–4 hours cooling.

### B) Cook Now (Steps)

#### 1) Stage and prep
- **Preheat** oven to **350 deg F** with a rack in the middle.
- Line a **9x13** pan with a parchment **sling** (overhang on two sides), then **grease** parchment and exposed sides well.

#### 2) Make red date chunks (food processor method)
- Pit the red dates.
- **Freeze 15–25 minutes** (firmer, not rock-hard).
- Add dates to the food processor with **2 Tbsp glutinous rice flour**.
- **Pulse in short bursts** until you get **pea-sized to small-raisin-sized chunks**.
  - Typical: 6–12 pulses, stopping once or twice to redistribute.
- If you see smearing/paste starting:
  - Add **1 more Tbsp glutinous rice flour** and pulse 2–3 more times.
- Measure your output:
  - Target **about 1/2 cup loosely packed** chunks total.
  - **Reserve 2 Tbsp** for the top.

#### 3) Mix the batter
- In a large bowl, whisk until smooth:
  - eggs, brown sugar, coconut milk, whole milk, oil, vanilla
- Whisk in dry ingredients gradually (not all at once):
  - glutinous rice flour, salt, and (optional) baking powder
- If batter is lumpy:
  - strain through a fine-mesh sieve.
- **Fold in** the date chunks (all but the reserved 2 Tbsp) with a spatula at the end.

#### 4) Bake (dates visible first, coconut later)
- Pour batter into the pan; tap 5–10 times to release bubbles.
- Scatter reserved **2 Tbsp** date chunks evenly across the surface and **press in ~1/8 inch** so they anchor.
- Bake at **350 deg F** for **55–75 minutes total**.
  - At **30–40 minutes**, when the surface is set (not liquid), quickly pull the pan and scatter coconut lightly over the top.
    - If using large coconut flakes, add closer to **40 minutes**.
    - If coconut is sweetened, add later (around **45–55 minutes**) to prevent scorching.
  - Finish baking until:
    - edges are deeply golden and set, and
    - a skewer in the center shows **no wet batter** (tacky crumbs ok).
- If coconut browns too fast:
  - tent loosely with foil.
- If center shows wet batter at 70+ minutes:
  - reduce to **325 deg F** and bake 10–20 minutes more.

#### 5) Cool, slice, pack
- Cool on a rack **at least 2 hours** (4 is better).
- Lift out using the parchment sling.
- Slice with a sharp knife lightly oiled between cuts.

## Make-Ahead Map
- Room temp: airtight 2–3 days.
- Fridge: 5–7 days (firmer chew). Reheat 10–15 sec microwave per square, or 300 deg F oven 8–12 min.
- Freezer: wrap squares individually; freeze 2–3 months; thaw at room temp 30–60 min; reheat briefly.

## Error-Proofing
- Wet/gummy center after cooling:
  - likely underbaked or cut early → cool longer first; if still wet, rebake at **325 deg F** 10–20 min.
- Date chunks turn into paste pockets:
  - too soft / over-processed → freeze longer next time; dust with more flour; pulse less.
- Coconut tastes bitter/dark:
  - added too early → add later next time; tent with foil.
