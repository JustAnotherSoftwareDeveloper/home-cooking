# Blotted Green Chile-Cumin Beef Patties — Assembly Only

## Title & Overview
These are **make-ahead green chile-cumin beef patties** built for later cooking by whatever burger method you choose. The flavor is beef-forward with mild roasted chile, cumin, garlic, onion, and black pepper inside the patty. The key technical move is using **well-drained, blotted diced green chiles** so the chile flavor is in the patty without making the beef loose; multiple green-chile burger formulas use about **1 drained 4 oz can green chiles per 1 lb beef**, which scales cleanly here to 2 cans for 2 lb beef [1][2].

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 8 quarter-pound patties from 2 lb beef
- **Active Prep:** 20 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 20 minutes minimum chill
- **Cook:** Not part of this artifact; use a separate cooking method later
- **Total:** 40 minutes same-day assembly; or 20 minutes assembly + overnight fridge hold
- **Make-ahead:** Assemble up to 24 hours ahead and refrigerate raw, unsalted patties. For multiple days ahead, freeze raw patties separated with parchment; FoodSafety.gov lists raw hamburger / ground meats at **1 to 2 days refrigerated** and **3 to 4 months frozen for best quality** [8].

## Ingredients
- 2 cans diced roasted green chiles, 4 oz each, drained very well and blotted dry
- 2 lb cold 80/20 ground beef
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp onion powder
- 1 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** beef
- **Easy swaps:**
  - Mild diced green chiles -> hot diced green chiles -> same method, more chile bite.
  - Hatch-style green chiles -> standard canned diced green chiles -> less smoky but structurally fine if blotted well.
- **Hard swaps not recommended:**
  - Salsa verde instead of diced green chiles -> too wet for make-ahead patties unless reduced into a thick paste first.
  - Lean beef under 85/15 -> drier and less forgiving once cooked later; 80/20 is a common burger recommendation for juiciness [4].

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** 8 raw green chile-cumin beef patties for later cooking.
- **Texture cue:** The raw mixture should look loose and craggy, not sticky, pasty, or wet.
- **Workflow:** Blot chiles first, gently fold seasonings into cold beef, form patties, then chill or freeze.
- **Make-ahead:** Night-before fridge storage is the primary plan; freezing is the multiple-days-ahead plan.
- **Primary risks:** Wet chiles, overmixing, and adding salt too early.

## Gather & Stage
- Drain the green chiles.
- Set out parchment paper, paper towels or a clean kitchen towel, and freezer bags or an airtight container if storing.
- Line one 13×9 metal tray for a **4-patty batch**, or line a larger roasting tray / second tray for all 8 patties.
- Keep the beef cold until the moment you mix.
- **Checkpoint:** The tray setup matches your storage plan, the chiles are draining, and all seasonings are measured before the beef comes out.

## Instructions

1. Prep the green chiles
   - **Drain and blot** the diced green chiles aggressively.
     - Spread the chiles in a thin layer on paper towels or a clean kitchen towel.
     - Press from the top until the towel picks up no obvious wet patches.
     - If the chile pieces are large, pulse them 2 to 3 times in the food processor; do not puree.
     - **Checkpoint:** The chiles should be small, damp, and loose, not saucy.
     - *If the chiles still leave puddles -> blot again with fresh towel -> they are ready when they clump lightly but do not weep liquid.*

2. Mix the patty base
   - **Combine** the cold beef, blotted green chiles, cumin, garlic powder, onion powder, and black pepper.
     - Use your fingers or a fork to gently fold the mixture together in the KitchenAid Stand Mixer bowl.
     - Do **not** use the mixer motor.
     - Do **not** add salt during assembly.
     - Stop as soon as the chile and spices look evenly distributed.
     - **Checkpoint:** The beef should look evenly speckled but still loose and craggy.
     - *If the beef starts smearing or feeling tacky -> stop mixing and chill 10 minutes -> it is fixed when the fat firms back up and the mixture handles cleanly.*

3. Portion and shape the patties
   - **Divide** the mixture into 8 equal patties.
     - Each patty should be about 4 oz.
     - Aim for roughly 4 to 4 1/2 inches wide and about 1/2 inch thick.
     - Press a shallow dimple into the center of each patty.
     - Avoid compressing the edges smooth; a slightly rough edge is fine.
     - **Checkpoint:** Patties hold together when lifted but do not look dense or packed.
     - *If a patty cracks badly when lifted -> press only the cracked edge back together -> it is fixed when it moves as one piece without becoming compressed.*

4. Chill, store, or freeze
   - **Chill** the patties on parchment for at least 20 minutes before storing or cooking later.
     - If using one 13×9 tray, chill or freeze only about 4 patties at a time in one flat layer.
     - For all 8 patties at once, use a larger Sheet / roasting tray or a second 13×9 tray.
     - For night-before storage, cover the tray tightly or stack chilled patties with parchment between them in an airtight container.
     - For freezing, freeze patties flat on parchment until solid, then transfer to a freezer bag with parchment between patties.
     - Label with the date and: “unsalted patties — salt exterior immediately before cooking.”
     - **Checkpoint:** Patties are cold, firm enough to move, and separated so they will not fuse together.
     - *If stacking patties makes them deform -> chill them flat for 20 to 30 minutes first -> they are ready to stack when they lift cleanly from parchment.*

5. Later-cook handoff
   - **Do not taste raw beef** to adjust seasoning.
     - Seasoning calibration happens later, after cooking a test patty or first batch by your separate cooking method.
     - Taste-adjust loop for the later cook: salt -> pepper -> cumin -> chile intensity.
     - Kosher salt is intentionally excluded from assembly; add it only to the exterior immediately before cooking later.
     - **Checkpoint:** The patties are labeled clearly enough that future-you will not mix salt into the raw beef by mistake.

## Common Issues
- **Loose patties:** Chiles were too wet -> blot harder before mixing -> raw mix should not weep liquid in the bowl.
- **Dense patties:** Beef was overmixed -> fold more gently and stop earlier -> raw patties should look craggy, not smooth.
- **Bland final burger risk:** No salt is mixed in by design -> salt the exterior right before whatever cooking method you use.
- **Patties freeze together:** Not enough parchment separation -> freeze flat first, then bag once solid.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- **Fridge plan:**
  - Stop after Step 5.
  - Store raw patties unsalted, covered, and separated with parchment.
  - Holds: up to 24 hours for best texture.
  - Later-cook handoff: salt the exterior immediately before cooking with your separate cooking method.

- **Freezer plan:**
  - Best freeze point: after Step 5, before any salt is added.
  - Portioning: 8 quarter-pound patties, parchment between each.
  - Freeze: up to 3 months for best quality.
  - Thaw: overnight in the refrigerator on a tray.
  - Later-cook handoff: salt the exterior after thawing, immediately before cooking with your separate cooking method.

## Troubleshooting
- Patties feel wet -> green chiles were not blotted enough -> blot surface moisture and chill uncovered 10 minutes -> patties feel tacky but not slick.
- Patties smear while shaping -> beef got too warm -> chill mixture 10 to 15 minutes -> fat firms and patties shape cleanly.
- Patties crack at the edge -> mix is slightly under-compressed -> press only the cracked edge together -> patty holds without becoming dense.
- Patties look packed and smooth -> overhandled -> stop shaping immediately -> next batch should be mixed and formed more loosely.

## Troubleshooting Table

| Symptom                | Likely Cause            | Quick Fix                                               |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Loose raw patties      | Chiles too wet          | Blot chiles harder; chill formed patties longer         |
| Dense raw patties      | Overmixed beef          | Stop shaping immediately; use a lighter fold next batch |
| Patties stick together | No parchment separation | Separate with parchment before chilling or freezing     |
| Weak chile aroma       | Mild canned chiles      | Use medium/hot Hatch-style chiles next batch            |

## Geometry Notes
- **Target patty size:** 8 patties, 4 oz each, about 4 to 4 1/2 inches wide and 1/2 inch thick.
- **Single-tray capacity:** One 13×9 metal tray fits about 4 patties in one flat layer.
- **Full-batch tray setup:** Use two 13×9 trays, one larger Sheet / roasting tray, or a two-wave chill/freeze plan.
- **Stacking rule:** Only stack after patties are chilled and firm; use parchment between every patty.
- **Freezing rule:** Freeze flat first, then bag once solid.

## Scaling & Batch Size
- **For 1 lb beef:** Use 1 can diced green chiles, 1 tsp cumin, 1/2 tsp garlic powder, 1/2 tsp onion powder, and 3/4 tsp black pepper.
- **For 3 lb beef:** Multiply by 1.5; form 12 quarter-pound patties.
- **Batch strategy:** Mix one 2 lb batch at a time rather than scaling into a huge bowl; smaller batches reduce overmixing.

## Special Notes
- **Salt is intentionally excluded from assembly.** Add salt to the exterior right before cooking later. Serious Eats’ testing found that salting ground beef before forming creates a tighter, more resilient, sausage-like texture; salting just before cooking preserves a looser burger texture [7].
- **Cooking is intentionally not covered here.** This artifact stops at raw patty assembly and storage.
- **Chile amount is assertive but controlled.** Two 4 oz cans for 2 lb beef tracks common green-chile burger ratios, but it only works reliably if the chiles are drained and blotted hard [1][2].
- **Local-style caveat:** New Mexico-style green chile cheeseburgers often use roasted chopped chile as a topping rather than mixing it into the patty; this version deliberately moves the chile inside the patty to satisfy the patty-only constraint [4][6].

## Variations
1. **Hot Green Chile Patties:** Use hot diced green chiles instead of mild. Keep the amount the same so the patties do not loosen.

2. **Smokier Green Chile Patties:** Add 1/2 tsp smoked paprika with the cumin. This makes the patties taste more grill-coded even before toppings.

3. **Green Chile-Pepper Jack Patties:** Fold in 3 oz small-diced pepper jack during Step 2. This adds richness but makes later cooking more prone to cheese leakage.

## Safety & Correctness Notes
Keep raw patties refrigerated until cooking or freezing. When these patties are eventually cooked, ground beef should reach **160°F internal temperature** checked with a food thermometer [8].
