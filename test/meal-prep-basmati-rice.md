# Loomi-Tomato Kabsa-Style Basmati Rice

## Title & Overview

This is an Instant Pot basmati rice base inspired by kabsa: tomato-stained rice, loomi/dried-lime tang, garlic, ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, coriander, turmeric, and a controlled amount of ghee. Traditional kabsa is a meat-and-rice dish built around spiced broth, rice, tomato, dried lime, and meat, but this version keeps the rice as a freezer-friendly component so it can plug into beef, lamb, kofta, chickpea, or roasted-vegetable bowls later [1][2].

The structure follows the same general meal-prep logic as your existing Instant Pot rice base: pressure cook, fluff, shallow tray-cool, optionally dry briefly in a low oven, portion flat, and reheat covered with water. The key correction in this version is stricter tomato-paste scorch control: the tomato paste is reduced slightly, Saute is cancelled before sealing, and the pot is deglazed with a full cup of measured broth before the rice goes in.

This is generally healthy in the practical sense: it is low added sugar, fat is measured, sodium is controlled by using lower-sodium broth, and the flavor comes from spice blooming, tomato paste, dried lime, and lime juice rather than a heavy sauce. It is make-ahead friendly because the rice is designed to freeze as a component and revive in the microwave with added water.

- **Yield:** about 10 to 12 cups cooked rice; 10 rice-base portions
- **Active Prep:** 15 to 20 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 25 to 35 minutes, including pressure build, natural release, steam venting, tray-cooling setup, and optional oven dry-out
- **Cook:** 6 minutes under pressure
- **Total:** 45 to 60 minutes
- **Make-ahead:** refrigerate up to 4 days, or freeze up to 2 months for best texture; frozen food remains safe longer if continuously frozen, but quality declines over time [7].

## Ingredients

- 4 cups basmati rice
- 2 tablespoons ghee, divided
- 1 1/2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tablespoon grated fresh ginger
- 2 teaspoons ground cumin
- 1 1/2 teaspoons ground coriander
- 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
- 3/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 3/4 teaspoon ground cardamom
- 1/2 teaspoon ground allspice
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1/16 teaspoon ground cloves, optional but useful
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 whole loomi / dried lime, pierced once or twice, or 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon ground loomi
- 3 3/4 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 3/4 teaspoon Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 1/2 teaspoon Morton kosher salt / table salt
- 1 to 2 teaspoons fresh lime juice

## Allergy & Dietary Notes

- **Contains:** dairy/milk from ghee; chicken broth.
- **Trace onion note:** Swanson 33% Less Sodium Chicken Broth is used as the nutrition proxy and lists onion juice concentrate. This recipe does not use meaningful onion as an aromatic base, but choose a different lower-sodium broth if that trace amount is unacceptable [8].
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Unsalted chicken broth -> lower sodium and cleaner control -> increase salt only after cooking.
  - Low-sodium vegetable broth -> keeps the method intact -> slightly less savory.
  - Avocado oil instead of ghee -> dairy-free -> less rich and less kabsa-like, but still functional.
  - Ground loomi instead of whole loomi -> easier sourcing -> use 1/4 teaspoon for a mild first test or 1/2 teaspoon for a stronger dried-lime note.
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - Fresh tomato instead of tomato paste -> adds too much water for this pressure-rice geometry -> use tomato paste in the base and fresh tomato as a post-reheat topping if desired.
  - Brown rice -> not a direct swap -> different liquid and pressure time.
  - Onion base -> not used here -> would change both GI fit and the project constraint profile.
- **Diet toggles:**
  - Low-sodium: use unsalted broth, reduce added salt by half, and finish individual bowls with lime juice or herbs before adding salt.
  - Dairy-free: use avocado oil instead of ghee.
  - Gluten-free: confirm the broth, tomato paste, and spices are certified gluten-free if cross-contact matters.

## Quick Overview

- **What you are making:** Kabsa-inspired tomato-loomi basmati rice for freezer-friendly Middle Eastern bowls.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Rice should be fluffy, separate, orange-red, lightly glossy, and tart-warm rather than wet or saucy.
- **Workflow:** Rinse and drain rice, bloom tomato paste and spices in ghee, deglaze hard with broth, pressure-cook, fluff, finish with lime juice, tray-cool, and portion.
- **Make-ahead:** Best freezer texture comes from shallow tray cooling and flat portioning.
- **Reheat plan:** Microwave covered with 1 to 2 tablespoons water per 2-cup portion until steaming, then fluff and rest covered 1 minute.
- **Primary risks:** Scorched tomato paste, mushy basmati from poor draining, bitter loomi from over-extraction, and unsafe slow cooling.
- **Component use:** For a complete 2-cup freezer bowl, use about 1 to 1 1/2 cups rice plus a protein portion and a cooked vegetable portion.

## Gather & Stage

- Measure the rice, ghee, tomato paste, broth, salt, spices, bay leaf, loomi, garlic, ginger, and lime juice.
- Rinse the rice:
  - Put rice in the Instant Pot inner pot or a large bowl.
  - Cover with cool water, swish gently, and pour off cloudy water.
  - Repeat 3 to 5 times, until the water is much less cloudy.
  - Drain aggressively for 5 to 10 minutes.
- Pierce the whole loomi once or twice with the tip of a knife.
  - Do not crush it.
  - If using ground loomi, measure it with the other spices.
- Set out the **Instant Pot - 6 QT**.
- Set out the **Sheet / baking tray - 13x9, metal, light** and **Sheet / roasting trays - Various, metal**.
- If using the oven finish, preheat the **Standard oven** to **250 deg F**.
- **Checkpoint:** rice is rinsed and well-drained; broth is measured; spices are grouped; trays are ready before sauteing starts.

## Instructions

1. Bloom the tomato-spice base
   - Set the **Instant Pot - 6 QT** to **Saute, low or normal**, then add **1 1/2 tablespoons ghee**.
     - Add tomato paste, garlic, and ginger.
     - **Saute** for 30 to 45 seconds, stirring constantly, until the paste smells savory and darkens only slightly.
     - Add cumin, coriander, turmeric, cinnamon, cardamom, allspice, black pepper, cloves if using, bay leaf, and ground loomi if using.
     - **Saute** for 15 to 20 seconds more, just until fragrant.
     - Press **Cancel** to turn off Saute.
     - Add **1 cup of the measured broth** and **scrape** the bottom until the tomato-spice paste loosens completely.
     - This deglaze step is mandatory because tomato products in pressure cookers are a common burn-warning trigger; user reports repeatedly point to tomato paste touching the bottom, insufficient moisture, and failure to deglaze as the main risk points [6].
     - **Checkpoint:** the pot smells warm, tomato-rich, and aromatic; the bottom feels smooth when scraped.
     - *If the paste looks dry or starts sticking -> add another splash of measured broth -> scrape until the bottom is clean and the paste is fluid.*

2. Add rice, remaining broth, and loomi
   - Add the drained basmati rice to the pot.
     - Fold gently for 20 to 30 seconds, just until the grains are coated.
     - Add the remaining broth and salt.
     - Add the pierced whole loomi if using.
     - Scrape the bottom again thoroughly.
     - Level the rice gently; do not pack it down.
     - **Checkpoint:** no tomato paste is stuck to the bottom, the liquid is evenly distributed, and the rice sits in a level layer.
     - *If the rice mound looks dry on top -> press it just under the liquid with a spatula -> do not stir hard or mash the grains.*

3. Pressure-cook the rice
   - Lock the lid and cook on **High Pressure for 6 minutes**.
     - When the cook time ends, let pressure release naturally for **10 minutes**, then quick-release remaining pressure.
     - Use **Pressure Cook / Manual**, not the Rice preset.
     - Basmati pressure-cook tests support 6 minutes high pressure with a 10-minute natural release as a reliable target, while careful rinsing and draining help keep the liquid ratio accurate [3].
     - **Checkpoint:** grains are fully cooked with no chalky center, but still separate.
     - *If the rice tastes slightly firm and there was no burn warning -> close the lid for 5 minutes off heat -> residual steam should finish it without more pressure cooking.*

4. Fluff, remove aromatics, and finish with lime juice
   - Open the lid and remove the bay leaf and whole loomi.
     - Do not squeeze, crush, or scrape the inside of the loomi into the rice.
     - Add the remaining **1/2 tablespoon ghee**.
     - **Fluff** from the bottom up with a fork or rice paddle.
     - Let the rice sit uncovered in the pot for 3 to 5 minutes to vent steam.
     - Add **1 teaspoon fresh lime juice**, fluff again, and taste.
     - Add the second teaspoon only if the rice still tastes flat or too heavy.
     - Taste-adjust loop: lime juice -> salt -> heat -> herbs/fat.
     - **Checkpoint:** rice is fluffy, separate, orange-gold, lightly glossy, and warm-tangy.
     - *If the loomi reads bitter -> remove it immediately if not already removed, add a tiny pinch of salt and stop at 1 teaspoon lime juice -> bitterness should move into the background.*

5. Spread and cool
   - Spread the rice across the **Sheet / baking tray - 13x9, metal, light** and **Sheet / roasting trays - Various, metal**.
     - Target depth: **3/4 inch to 1 inch max**.
     - Use two trays unless one larger tray can hold the rice at 1 inch depth or less.
     - Spread gently; do not mash.
     - **Checkpoint:** rice is in a shallow, even layer with no deep mound.
     - *If either tray is deeper than 1 inch -> split onto another tray or shallow container -> steam should escape quickly from the surface.*

6. Optional oven finish for freezer texture
   - Use this step only if the rice looks wetter than you want for freezing.
     - **Bake** at **250 deg F** for 8 to 12 minutes, stirring once halfway through.
     - Stop when the surface moisture has dropped and the grains separate more easily.
     - Do not bake until dry or crunchy.
     - **Checkpoint:** no visible moisture collects on the tray, but the grains remain flexible.
     - *If the edges start to harden -> pull the tray immediately and fluff -> carryover heat will even out the texture.*

7. Portion and finalize
   - Cool the rice shallowly, then portion promptly.
     - Portion into 10 rice-base portions, or into 1-cup and 2-cup packs depending on your bowl plan.
     - Flatten freezer bags or shallow containers so they freeze and reheat evenly.
     - For a full 2-cup freezer bowl, combine about 1 to 1 1/2 cups rice with a protein portion and a cooked vegetable portion.
     - Best pairings: beef kofta, lamb meatballs, shredded beef, chickpeas, roasted carrots, roasted green beans, or a yogurt/tahini sauce added after reheating.
     - Taste-adjust loop after reheating: lime juice -> salt -> heat -> herbs/fat.
     - **Checkpoint:** final rice is freezer-ready, fluffy, warm-spiced, tangy, and strong enough to support a bowl protein.

## Common Issues

- **Scorched bottom:** tomato paste or spices stuck before sealing -> cancel Saute, deglaze with measured broth until the bottom feels smooth -> pressure cycle starts normally.
- **Mushy rice:** poor draining, too much liquid, or over-stirring -> tray-spread immediately and use the optional oven finish -> next batch reduce broth by 2 tablespoons.
- **Bitter loomi:** whole loomi was pierced too heavily or left in too long -> remove before cooling and do not crush -> flavor should be tangy, not medicinal.
- **Flat flavor:** not enough acid, salt, or fat after freezing -> finish reheated bowls with lime juice, a tiny salt pinch, and a small spoon of yogurt or tahini sauce.
- **Burn warning after pressure begins:** do not automatically restart a full pressure cycle -> assess doneness first -> steam-finish gently if the rice is already partly cooked.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 5 or Step 6.
  - Cool rice shallowly before packing.
  - Holds: up to 4 days.
  - Serve/Reheat: microwave covered with 1 to 2 teaspoons water per cup rice until steaming; fluff and rest covered 1 minute.

- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: after Step 6, or after Step 5 if skipping the oven finish.
  - Portioning: 1-cup portions for sides; 2-cup portions for bowl bases.
  - Freeze: best quality within 1 to 2 months.
  - Thaw: not required.
  - Reheat: microwave from frozen with 1 to 2 tablespoons water per 2-cup portion, covered, until steaming throughout. For strict food-safety handling, reheat leftovers to **165 deg F** [7].

## Reheat Plan

- **Best method:** microwave from frozen -> add 1 to 2 tablespoons water per 2-cup portion -> cover -> microwave on high for 3 to 5 minutes -> stir/fluff -> microwave 30 to 90 seconds more as needed, until steaming throughout.
- **Fridge method:** microwave covered with 1 to 2 teaspoons water per cup rice for 1 1/2 to 3 minutes, stirring once, until steaming.
- **Stovetop method:** warm in **Ceramic nonstick pans** or **Stainless steel pots and pans** over **low heat** with 1 to 2 tablespoons water per 2-cup portion for 4 to 7 minutes, covered only if you have a fitting lid, until steaming and flexible. If you do not have a fitting lid, use the microwave method instead.
- **Texture reset:** fluff with a fork, then rest covered 1 minute.
- **Individual bowl acid reset:** after reheating, add **1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon fresh lime juice per bowl** if the rice tastes flat.
- *If rice is dry or clumped -> sprinkle in another 1 tablespoon water, cover, and microwave 30 to 60 seconds more -> grains should soften and separate.*
- *If rice tastes dull -> add a small squeeze of lime juice, then a tiny pinch of salt -> tomato-loomi flavor should come back.*

## Troubleshooting

- Rice is wet or clumpy after cooking -> excess retained water or trapped steam -> spread shallowly and use optional 250 deg F oven finish -> grains separate when fluffed.
- Burn warning appears before pressure is reached -> tomato paste stuck to the pot bottom -> cancel, open once safe, move rice out if needed, scrub/deglaze the bottom clean, return contents, add 2 tablespoons broth, and restart -> pot reaches pressure normally.
- Burn warning appears after pressure has built or rice is partly cooked -> do not run a full new 6-minute pressure cycle -> cancel, release pressure if needed, check rice, then rest covered off heat for 5 to 10 minutes or transfer to a covered stovetop pot with a splash of water to steam-finish -> grains finish without turning mushy.
- Loomi flavor is too sharp or bitter -> whole loomi over-extracted or ground loomi overused -> remove loomi and dilute with plain rice next batch -> tang becomes background.
- Rice tastes too mild for bowls -> seasoning was calibrated for side-dish use -> add post-reheat lime juice, herbs, yogurt sauce, or daqoos-style tomato sauce -> bowl reads brighter and fuller.

## Scaling & Batch Size

- **Half batch:** 2 cups basmati rice, 1 tablespoon ghee, 2 1/4 teaspoons tomato paste, half the spices, 1 7/8 cups broth, same 6-minute pressure cook and 10-minute natural release.
- **Maximum 6 qt batch:** stay at 4 cups dry rice for the first test because tomato paste increases scorch risk.
- **Do not double in one 6 qt pot:** 8 cups dry rice exceeds reliable geometry for cooking, cooling, and freezer texture.
- **Drier freezer test:** after one successful batch, reduce broth by 2 tablespoons if reheated rice is still too soft.

## Special Notes

- **Rice role:** this is a bowl base, not a complete meal by itself.
- **Full bowl target:** for each 2-cup freezer meal, use about 1 to 1 1/2 cups rice, 3 to 5 oz cooked protein, and 1/2 to 1 cup cooked vegetables.
- **Best protein pairings:** beef kofta, lamb meatballs, shredded beef, chickpeas, or a separate roasted beef component.
- **Best vegetable pairings:** roasted carrots, roasted green beans, zucchini if tolerated, or a small tomato-cucumber topping added fresh after reheating.
- **Sodium drivers:** chicken broth, added salt, tomato paste, and any salty toppings added later.
- **Sodium levers:** use unsalted broth, reduce added salt by half, keep yogurt/daqoos/tahini as controlled toppings, and finish with lime juice or herbs before adding more salt.
- **Trace onion caution:** if using Swanson 33% Less Sodium Chicken Broth, its label lists onion juice concentrate; choose a different broth if that trace is unacceptable [8].

## Variations

1. **Carrot-loomi version:** Add 1 cup finely grated carrot with the tomato paste and cook it for 2 to 3 minutes before adding rice. Reduce broth by 2 tablespoons because carrot adds moisture.

2. **Daqoos-finished bowl version:** Keep the rice as written, then spoon a separate tomato-garlic daqoos-style sauce over reheated bowls. This gives more kick without making the freezer rice wetter or sharper.

3. **Chickpea bowl-base version:** Fold in 1 1/2 cups drained, rinsed canned chickpeas after pressure cooking and before tray cooling. Keep the portion moderate so the bowl does not stack too much fiber at once.

4. **Unsalted-broth version:** Use unsalted chicken broth and keep the same method. After cooking, adjust with small salt pinches, lime juice, and herbs rather than trying to season aggressively before pressure cooking.

## Safety & Correctness Notes

Cooked rice needs shallow cooling and prompt refrigeration or freezing. FoodSafety.gov recommends small, shallow containers for faster cooling, freezing leftovers if keeping them longer than four days, covering microwave leftovers, adding liquid if needed, and reheating leftovers to **165 deg F** [7].
