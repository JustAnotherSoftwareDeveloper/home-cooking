## Red Wine Risotto with a Small Taleggio Finish

### Title & Overview
This version is built specifically to sit next to **NY strip steak** without behaving like a second main. The base stays pressure-cooker-friendly: shallot, risotto rice, dry red wine, hot stock, butter, and Parmesan. The Taleggio is deliberately **restrained**—enough to add a soft washed-rind aroma and a silkier finish, but not enough to turn the plate heavy or overly funky. That balance is an intentional adaptation from traditional Taleggio risotti and radicchio-Taleggio builds, using a smaller finish-cheese approach so the risotto remains a side dish. [1][2][3][5][6]

### Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 4 side servings
- **Active Prep:** 15 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 8 minutes
- **Cook:** 12 minutes
- **Total:** 35 minutes
- **Make-ahead:** Component prep only. Dice the shallot, grate the Parmesan, cube the Taleggio, and warm the stock up to 1 day ahead. Finished risotto is best fresh; leftovers hold 1 day and need hot stock or water to loosen on reheat. [1][2][3][4]

- 1 large shallot, very finely diced **[pre-prep]**
- 1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 1/2 cups arborio or carnaroli rice
- 3/4 cup dry red wine
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, divided
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper, plus more to taste
- 1/3 cup finely grated Parmesan cheese **[pre-prep]**
- 2 ounces Taleggio cheese, rind trimmed if thick and cut into very small cubes **[pre-prep]**

### Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** dairy, alcohol
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Chicken stock -> vegetable stock -> slightly cleaner, less beef-adjacent finish -> no method change
  - Taleggio 2 oz -> 1 oz Taleggio + a little extra Parmesan -> milder aroma, firmer Parmesan-led finish -> no method change
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - Arborio/Carnaroli -> long-grain rice -> breaks the creamy suspended-starch texture and the pressure timing -> use risotto rice
  - Large Taleggio increase -> pushes the side toward a richer standalone risotto -> if you want that path, do a stovetop Taleggio risotto instead of this steak-side build [5][6]

### Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** a red-wine risotto side dish with a small Taleggio finish, designed to stay glossy and supportive next to steak rather than dominating the plate.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** the final risotto should be **all’onda / wavy**—it should spread slowly on the plate, not sit in a stiff mound. [6]
- **Workflow:** sauté shallot, toast the rice, cook off the wine, pressure-cook briefly, then finish with butter and cheese while the steak rests. [1][2][3][4]
- **Make-ahead:** prep components ahead, but do not fully finish the risotto early unless you are willing to re-loosen it right before serving.
- **Primary risks:** raw wine flavor, too-stiff texture after sitting, or too much Taleggio turning the side into the loudest thing on the plate. [4][5][6]

### Gather & Stage
- Warm the stock in the **Microwave / convection oven combo** or in **Stainless steel pots and pans**; keep it hot but not boiling.
- Reserve **1/2 cup** of the hot stock separately for the finish.
- Finely dice the shallot.
- Grate the Parmesan.
- Cut the Taleggio into very small cubes so it melts quickly and evenly.
- Measure the rice, wine, butter, oil, salt, pepper, and optional vinegar.
- Set the **Instant Pot — 6 QT** to **Saute** and make sure the insert is dry.
- **Checkpoint:** everything needed for Step 1 is prepped, measured, and within reach.

### Instructions

1. **Build the base**
   - **Saute** the shallot in the olive oil and **1 tablespoon butter** on **Saute / medium-high** for **2 to 3 minutes**, stirring often, until softened and translucent but not browned.
     - Add the rice and **toast** for **1 to 2 minutes**, stirring, until the grains look glossy and some edges turn slightly translucent.
     - Add the red wine and **cook** for **1 to 2 minutes**, stirring and scraping, until the raw alcohol smell softens and the wine is mostly evaporated rather than pooling at the bottom. This matters more in pressure-cooker risotto because little evaporation happens after the lid is sealed. [1][2]
     - *If the shallot starts browning too hard -> drop in 1 tablespoon of hot stock and lower the heat briefly -> cue it’s fixed: the pot smells sweet and winey, not toasted or sharp.*
     - **Checkpoint:** the rice is glossy, the bottom is cleanly scraped, and the wine no longer smells raw.

2. **Pressure-cook the rice**
   - **Pour** in **3 1/2 cups hot stock**, add the salt and pepper, and scrape the bottom thoroughly so nothing is stuck.
     - Lock the lid, set the valve to sealing, and **Pressure Cook on High** for **5 minutes**.
     - **Quick-release immediately** when the timer ends.
     - Recipe references cluster around **5 to 6 minutes** at high pressure, with either immediate quick release or a very short natural release. This version stays on the shorter side because the add-ins are minimal and the final texture needs to remain side-dish-loose rather than heavy. [1][2][3][4]
     - *If you know your Instant Pot runs a touch cool, or you prefer a slightly softer rice -> use 6 minutes instead of 5 -> cue it’s fixed: the grains are still intact, but the risotto needs less finish-stock to loosen.*
     - **Checkpoint:** when you open the lid, the rice should look a little wetter than “done”; that is correct.

3. **Finish and adjust the texture**
   - **Stir** the risotto vigorously for **45 to 60 seconds** to activate the starch and even out the texture.
     - Add the remaining **1 tablespoon butter** and the Parmesan; **stir** until fully melted.
     - Add the Taleggio and **stir** just until mostly melted, about **30 to 45 seconds**.
     - Add the reserved hot stock, **1 to 2 tablespoons at a time**, until the risotto turns glossy and fluid. Radicchio-Taleggio risotto references explicitly correct final consistency with added hot liquid, and that same move keeps this pressure-cooker version from tightening up on the plate. [6]
     - *If the Taleggio flavor jumps out too aggressively -> add 1 to 2 tablespoons of hot stock and 1 tablespoon Parmesan -> cue it’s fixed: the finish smells creamy and savory, not pungent.*
     - **Checkpoint:** the risotto moves in a slow wave when you drag a spoon through it.

4. **Plate with the steak**
   - **Finish** the risotto while the NY strip is resting; that is the cleanest timing window.
     - If the steak is unsauced or only lightly finished, spoon the risotto onto warm plates first, then slice and set the steak partly beside or just over the edge.
     - If you are using a pan sauce, plate the steak first and spoon the sauce so it does **not** flood the risotto.
     - Add thyme if using, then taste-adjust in this order: **acid** -> a few drops of red wine vinegar only if it tastes flat or cheese-heavy; **salt** -> pinch if needed; **heat** -> black pepper; **herbs** -> thyme.
     - Serve immediately after loosening and final adjustment; several pressure-cooker versions explicitly note that risotto is best right away rather than held until stiff. [3][4]
     - **Checkpoint:** final dish is loose, glossy, and clearly a steak companion rather than the center of the meal.

### Common Issues
- **Bottom sticking during sauté:** not enough scraping after the wine or stock went in -> scrape thoroughly before sealing -> the bottom should feel smooth with no rice glued down. [1][3]
- **Too salty at the end:** stock plus Parmesan plus Taleggio stacked up -> use low-sodium stock and hold back extra salt until the finish -> the final seasoning should happen after the cheese is in. [5][6]
- **Sauce flooding the risotto on the plate:** steak sauce and risotto were plated without separation -> plate the sauce on the steak side only -> the risotto should stay glossy, not diluted.

### Make-Ahead Notes
- **Fridge plan:**
  - Stop before Step **1**: complete **Gather & Stage** fully, then refrigerate the diced shallot and cheeses separately and keep the measured rice, wine, and stock ready to go.
  - Holds: **1 day**
  - Serve/Reheat: finish from fresh; for leftovers, reheat gently in **Stainless steel pots and pans** or on **Saute / Low** with a splash of hot stock or water until glossy and loosened.
- **Freezer plan:**
  - Best freeze point: **not recommended**
  - Portioning: skip freezing if texture matters
  - Freeze: quality loss is noticeable
  - Thaw: overnight in the fridge if you must
  - Reheat: low heat with hot stock or water until loosened; expect softer rice than fresh

### Troubleshooting
- Risotto is stiff and mound-like -> it sat too long or finished too dry -> add hot stock 1 tablespoon at a time and stir vigorously -> it should settle into a soft wave. [6]
- Wine tastes harsh -> the wine did not reduce enough before pressure-cooking -> simmer the wine longer on Saute before sealing -> the aroma should shift from boozy to mellow. [1][2]
- Taleggio takes over the plate -> too much washed-rind character for a steak side -> keep Taleggio small and lean on Parmesan for structure -> the cheese should read as a finish note, not the whole identity. This is a deliberate adaptation from more Taleggio-heavy traditional versions. [5][6]

### Geometry Notes
- **Pan/pot size used:** **Instant Pot — 6 QT**
- **Target thickness / layer depth:** loose enough to spread slightly when spooned; not soupy, not scoopable like mashed potatoes
- **Crowding rule:** do not push this past **1 1/2 cups risotto rice** in this format if the goal is a polished steak-side texture

### Scaling & Batch Size
- **Scaling notes:** halve cleanly to **3/4 cup rice, 6 tablespoons wine, 1 3/4 cups stock in the pot, plus extra hot stock for finishing**.
- **Batch strategy:** for more than 4 side servings, a second batch is safer than scaling up aggressively.
- **Pan swaps:** not recommended; this is written specifically for the **Instant Pot — 6 QT**.

### Special Notes
- **Service plan:** get the steak into its rest window first, then finish Step 3 and plate immediately. That sequencing keeps the steak juicy and the risotto fluid at the same time.
- **Sodium notes:** the biggest salt drivers are the stock, Parmesan, and Taleggio. Keep the stock low-sodium and season at the end, not at the front.
- **Wine choice:** use a dry red you would actually drink. Avoid wines that are very tannic, very acidic, or aggressively oaky; those traits concentrate as the wine reduces. [5]

### Variations
1. **Cleaner Parmesan path:** Omit the Taleggio and increase the Parmesan to **1/2 cup** for a more neutral steakhouse-style red-wine risotto. This is the lowest-risk path if the strip is getting a rich pan sauce. [2][5]
2. **Radicchio edge:** Add **1 cup very finely sliced radicchio** during the shallot phase for a slightly bitter finish that cuts steak richness well. Keep the Taleggio at **2 ounces** and add hot stock at the end if needed. [6]
3. **Mushroom version:** Add **6 to 8 ounces sliced mushrooms** during Step 1 and sauté until they give up their moisture and take on color before the wine goes in. This makes the pairing read more overtly steakhouse. [1][2][3][4]

### Safety & Correctness Notes
Traditional Taleggio risotto for four can run much heavier on the cheese—La Cucina Italiana uses **12 oz Carnaroli rice** and **7 oz Taleggio**—while GialloZafferano’s radicchio-Taleggio version uses a much smaller **2.1 oz / 60 g** Taleggio finish plus Parmesan and added hot water to correct final consistency. This recipe deliberately follows the lighter finish-cheese direction, because as a side for NY strip the heavier traditional ratio would be more likely to compete with the steak. That is an inference, not a direct traditional prescription. [5][6]
