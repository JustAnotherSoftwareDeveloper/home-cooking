# Coconut-Milk Baked Nian Gao Slab with Red-Date (Jujube) Swirl

## Title & Overview
This is a baked nian gao (年糕) in **slab form**: chewy and mochi-like, with browned edges and a tender, bouncy center. The base batter follows the common baked-niangao “thin pourable batter” approach, but uses **coconut milk + dark brown sugar** for deeper caramel notes. A **jujube (red-date) puree swirl** adds a subtle fruity richness without any topping. It slices cleanly only after a full cool-down—hot, it will seem too sticky/soft, and then it firms as it stands. [1][2][3]

## Yield & Timing
- **Yield:** 1 (9x13-in) slab; ~24 (2-in) squares
- **Active Prep:** 25 to 35 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 2 to 4 hours (cool + set)
- **Cook:** 60 to 80 minutes [8][9][10]
- **Total:** ~1 hour 45 minutes, plus cooling
- **Make-ahead:** Holds tightly covered at room temp up to 3 days; refrigerate up to 1 week (firmer chew). Rewarm briefly to soften; letting it cool again restores chew. [1][4]

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** A coconut-milk baked nian gao slab with a jujube swirl—chewy, glossy, and sliceable into squares. [1][2]
- **Texture and doneness cues:** **No wet batter** on the skewer; edges browned; top set; it **firms as it stands**, so cooling is part of doneness. [1][2][3]
- **Workflow:** **Simmer + blend dates**, then **whisk batter**, **build swirl**, **bake**; *while it cools, prep your storage/serving tray.* [1][6]
- **Make-ahead:** Better after resting; stores well covered; brief rewarm softens, then cooling brings back chew. [1][4]
- **Primary risks:** Underbaked center (usually “cut too early”), paste swirl too thick (sinks/blobs), overbrowned top before center sets. [1][2][3]

## Instructions
1. Make the jujube puree (swirl base)
   - **Simmer** the dates until soft, then **blend**.
     - In a small saucepan, add the dates and cover with water by about `1/2 in`; **Simmer** on **low heat** 10 to 20 minutes, until dates are very soft and mashable. [6]
     - **Blend** with a few spoonfuls of the hot cooking water until thick and spoonable.
     - *Optional:* **Push** through a sieve for a smoother swirl. [6]
     - *While the dates simmer: line/grease the pan and preheat the oven (Step 2).*
     - **Checkpoint:** Puree is thick but spoonable; it ribbons off a spoon, not a stiff clump.
     - *If the puree is too thick -> blend in 1 tablespoon hot water -> cue it’s fixed: it ribbons smoothly.*
     - *If the puree is too thin -> **Simmer** 2 to 5 minutes to reduce -> cue it’s fixed: it mounds on a spoon.*

2. Prep the pan and oven
   - **Preheat** and prep for easy release.
     - **Oven:** `325 deg F`. [8][9][10]
     - Line a 9x13 pan with parchment (sling if possible) and lightly grease the parchment and sides. [1]
     - **Checkpoint:** Pan is fully prepared before you start mixing the flour (batter shouldn’t sit while you hunt for parchment).

3. Mix the coconut-milk batter
   - **Whisk** wet ingredients, then **whisk in** dry.
     - In a large bowl, **Whisk** eggs just to break yolks (avoid aerating/foaming). [1]
     - **Whisk** in brown sugar until glossy and uniform. [2]
     - **Whisk** in coconut milk, whole milk, oil, vanilla, almond extract, and salt.
     - **Whisk** in glutinous rice flour gradually until mostly smooth.
       - *If using Mochiko-style flour, whisk in the baking powder with the flour.* [1]
     - **Checkpoint:** Batter is pourable (thick pancake batter), with only small clumps at most.
     - *If you have stubborn clumps -> rest 5 minutes, then whisk again -> cue it’s fixed: clumps hydrate and mostly disappear.* [1]

4. Build the swirl and bake
   - **Thin the puree with batter**, swirl lightly, then **Bake**.
     - Scoop out about 1 cup of batter; **Whisk** it into the jujube puree until the swirl mixture is similar thickness to the base batter (so it marbles instead of sinking).
     - Pour about 2/3 of the plain batter into the pan, dollop the swirl batter, add remaining plain batter, then add a few more swirl dollops.
     - Swirl with 6 to 10 gentle S-strokes (stop early; over-swirling muddies the pattern).
     - **Bake** at **325 deg F** for 60 to 80 minutes, starting to check at 60, until: [8][9][10]
       - edges are browned, top is set, and a skewer comes out with **no wet batter** (tacky crumbs are fine). [1][2]
     - **Checkpoint:** Center shows no wet batter on the skewer; top is set, not glossy-wet.
     - *If the top is browning fast before the center sets -> tent loosely with foil -> cue it’s fixed: top stops darkening while center continues to set.* [1][2]
     - *If the center still shows wet batter at 80 minutes -> bake 5 to 10 minutes more -> cue it’s fixed: skewer is batter-free.* [8][10]

5. Cool, slice, and taste-adjust
   - **Rest** to set, then slice and finish.
     - Cool in the pan on a rack at least 2 hours (4 is best for clean slices).
     - Lift with parchment (if used) and slice with a lightly oiled knife.
     - Taste-adjust loop: acid -> salt -> heat -> herbs.
       - Acid: serve with citrus segments or berries if you want brightness.
       - Salt: a tiny pinch of flaky salt on your piece if it tastes flat.
       - Heat: next time add a small pinch of ground ginger if you want warmth. [4]
       - Herbs: treat “herbs” as aroma—nudge vanilla or almond up/down (you can go to 1/2 tsp almond if you want it louder). [1][5]
     - **Checkpoint:** Squares hold shape cleanly and have a chewy set (not molten).

## Common Issues
- **It seems underbaked when sliced:** Cut too early -> cool longer -> quick check: after 2+ hours, slices hold shape and feel bouncy, not wet-gooey. [1][3]
- **Swirl blobs/sinks:** Swirl puree too thick -> thin it with batter first -> quick check: swirl batter ribbons, not plops.
- **Top browns too much:** Brown sugar/coconut milk browns readily -> tent with foil -> quick check: center sets without top burning. [2]

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 4: cool fully, cover tightly in-pan or in an airtight container.
  - Holds: 3 days room temp; up to 1 week refrigerated. [1][4]
  - Serve/Reheat: warm at `350 deg F` for 6 to 9 minutes until just pliable; cool 5 minutes so chew returns. [4]
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: Step 5 (fully cooled and sliced).
  - Portioning: wrap squares individually; then bag.
  - Freeze: up to 3 months.
  - Thaw: room temp 30 to 60 minutes.
  - Reheat: brief microwave until softened; cool a minute for chew to rebound. [4]

## Troubleshooting
- Center is wet even after full cooling -> underbaked -> bake 5 to 15 minutes more at `325 deg F` -> recovery cue: skewer shows no wet batter and slices hold. [8][10]
- Swirl disappears/muddies -> over-swirled or swirl batter too thin -> fewer strokes + thicken swirl mixture -> recovery cue: visible ribbons remain after baking.
- Gritty/lumpy texture -> flour not incorporated -> rest batter 5 minutes and whisk again next time -> recovery cue: batter is smooth enough to pour in a continuous stream. [1]

## Special Notes
- Storage & reheat: This cake is best judged at room temp after it sets; reheating makes it softer, cooling brings back chew. [4]
- Serving ideas: Cut small squares and serve at room temp; the swirl shows best when the cake is fully cooled.

## Variations
1. **Red bean swirl:** Replace the jujube puree with sweetened red bean paste thinned with a little batter, and swirl the same way for a classic bakery-style version. [2][3]
2. **Ginger-forward:** Add 1 to 2 teaspoons ground ginger to the dry ingredients for a warmer, more spiced bite. [4]
3. **Coconut-only “classic”:** Skip the swirl and bake the coconut-milk batter plain; it’s simpler and highlights the browned edges. [2]

## Safety & Correctness Notes
This uses a quick **jujube puree** (cook dates, blend, optional sieve) rather than a fully cooked-down, sugared pastry filling paste; that keeps the swirl from becoming overly sweet and heavy while staying technique-consistent with traditional “soften + blend + sieve” date-paste workflows. [6]
