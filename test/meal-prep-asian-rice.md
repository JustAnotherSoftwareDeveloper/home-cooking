# Weekday Fried Rice Base

## Title & Overview

This is a freezer-oriented, weekday-style fried rice base for store-bought orange chicken. It is not wok fried rice. The method is engineered for scale: Instant Pot jasmine rice, oven-dried on sheet trays, roasted frozen green beans, roasted shredded carrots, soy-ginger-garlic seasoning, and cold-processed egg gravel.

The finished rice should eat savory, lightly glossy, and separate-grained, with soft vegetables and tiny egg pieces distributed through the batch. It is seasoned enough to avoid tasting like plain meal-prep rice, but it stays restrained because orange chicken will bring sweetness, sodium, oil, and sauce.

## Yield & Timing

- **Yield:** about 10 freezer portions, roughly 1.5 to 1.75 cups each
- **Rice target:** about 10 cups cooked rice before vegetables and egg
- **Active Prep:** ~75 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** ~60 to 90 minutes
- **Cook:** ~90 minutes, with overlap between rice, roasting, drying, and egg work
- **Total:** ~3 to 3.5 hours elapsed
- **Make-ahead:** Best made as a batch, portioned, chilled, then frozen. Fridge portions hold 3 to 4 days; freezer portions are best within 2 months, acceptable up to 3 months for quality.

## Ingredients

- 3 1/3 cups jasmine rice, rinsed well **[pre-prep]**
- 3 1/4 cups water **[pre-prep]**
- 24 oz frozen cut green beans
- 12 oz shredded carrots
- 6 large eggs
- 3 tablespoons avocado oil, divided
- 6 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tablespoons fresh ginger, grated or finely minced
- 3 tablespoons low-sodium soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
- 1 teaspoon granulated sugar
- 2 teaspoons toasted sesame oil
- 1/4 teaspoon fine sea salt, optional, only after tasting

## Allergy & Dietary Notes

- **Contains:** egg, soy, wheat/gluten if using standard Kikkoman Less Sodium Soy Sauce. Kikkoman lists wheat as an ingredient and reports 590 mg sodium per tablespoon [9].
- **Rice base only:** check the orange chicken separately for wheat/gluten, soy, egg, sesame, and shared-fryer or cross-contact warnings.
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Low-sodium soy sauce -> certified gluten-free tamari -> keeps soy flavor while reducing gluten exposure if certified.
  - Frozen cut green beans -> frozen peas -> sweeter and more legume-heavy; less GI-conservative.
  - 6 eggs -> 4 eggs -> lower egg texture risk after freezing, but less fried-rice character.
- **Hard swaps:**
  - Brown rice -> not recommended; rougher bran texture and worse GI fit for this use case.
  - Warm food-processed egg -> not recommended; high paste risk.
  - Regular soy sauce at the same quantity -> not recommended; sodium climbs fast.
- **Diet toggles:**
  - Low-sodium: reduce soy sauce to 2 tablespoons and add 1 extra tablespoon rice vinegar.
  - Gluten-free: use certified gluten-free tamari and verify the orange chicken.
  - Soy-free: use coconut aminos, but expect a sweeter and less classic flavor.
  - Egg-free: omit eggs entirely; add 1 extra tablespoon avocado oil only if the rice seems dry.

## Quick Overview

- **What you are making:** A scalable freezer fried-rice base for orange chicken.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Rice should be dry-surfaced and separate; vegetables should be roasted until not watery; egg should be cold pea-size gravel, not paste.
- **Workflow:** Roast vegetables while the rice cooks; then dry the rice at low heat, cook and chill egg sheets, pulse egg cold, and fold everything together.
- **Make-ahead:** The full batch is designed to be cooked, portioned, chilled, and frozen.
- **Reheat plan:** Microwave covered with 1 to 2 teaspoons water, stir halfway, and heat until steaming hot.
- **Primary risks:** Mushy rice, wet vegetables, egg paste, and over-seasoning before the orange chicken is added.

## Gather & Stage

- Rinse the jasmine rice until the water runs mostly clear; rinsing helps reduce surface starch and clumping in fried rice [2][3].
- Set out the **Instant Pot - 6 QT**, at least 3 x 13x9-equivalent tray surfaces, ceramic nonstick pan, food processor, and final mixing vessel.
- Preheat the **Standard oven** to **425 deg F**.
- Mince the garlic and grate the ginger.
- Whisk together soy sauce, rice vinegar, sugar, and toasted sesame oil in a small bowl.
- Keep frozen green beans frozen until roasting.
- Set out 10 freezer containers or silicone molds.
- **Checkpoint:** Rice, vegetables, egg setup, sauce, trays, and storage containers are ready before cooking starts.

## Instructions

1. Cook the rice in the Instant Pot
   - **Pressure-cook** the rice.
     - Add rinsed rice and water to the **Instant Pot - 6 QT**.
     - Lock the lid and cook on **high pressure** for **3 minutes**.
     - Let pressure release naturally for **10 minutes**, then quick-release remaining pressure.
     - This follows the official Instant Pot jasmine-rice pattern of 1:1 rice to water, 3 minutes pressure, and 10 minutes natural release; this version uses slightly less water because the rice will be oven-dried for fried-rice texture [1].
     - Fluff immediately with a rice paddle or fork.
     - **Checkpoint:** Rice is fully cooked but slightly firm, not wet or mushy.
     - *If the bottom is slightly compacted -> scoop gently instead of scraping hard -> the loose rice goes to the trays and the stuck layer stays behind.*

2. Roast the green beans while the rice cooks
   - **Roast** frozen green beans first.
     - Keep oven at **425 deg F**.
     - Spread frozen green beans across 2 tray surfaces, mostly in one layer with visible metal between clusters.
     - Toss with 1 tablespoon avocado oil.
     - Roast **18 to 25 minutes**, stirring once, until beans are hot, wrinkled, and no visible water is pooling.
     - Chop any long pieces smaller after roasting if needed.
     - **Checkpoint:** Green beans are surface-dry and soft enough to mix into rice.
     - *If water pools on the tray -> keep roasting uncovered -> it is fixed when the tray looks dry after stirring.*

3. Roast the shredded carrots
   - **Roast** carrots briefly.
     - Spread shredded carrots on a separate tray.
     - Toss with 2 teaspoons avocado oil.
     - Roast at **425 deg F** for **8 to 12 minutes**, stirring once.
     - Stop when softened and lightly dried, not crisp or stringy.
     - Remove vegetables from the oven and reduce oven temperature to **250 deg F**.
     - **Checkpoint:** Carrots are soft and no longer raw, but not browned into hay.
     - *If carrots are browning too fast -> pull them early and spread thinner next batch -> they are fixed when they taste sweet and soft, not burnt.*

4. Steam off and oven-dry the rice
   - **Dry** the rice on trays.
     - As soon as the rice is fluffed, spread it across at least 3 x 13x9-equivalent tray surfaces.
     - Keep rice no deeper than about 1/2 inch.
     - Let steam off at room temperature for **10 to 20 minutes**.
     - Once the oven has dropped to **250 deg F**, dry rice for **20 to 30 minutes**, stirring every 10 minutes.
     - Stop when the rice surface feels dry and clumps break apart cleanly.
     - Keep cumulative room-temperature handling for cooked rice and finished rice under **2 hours** before refrigeration or freezing; use shallow containers and prompt refrigeration [8].
     - **Checkpoint:** Rice is dry-surfaced, separate, and not browned.
     - *If rice still feels damp -> keep drying in 5-minute increments -> it is ready when grains separate without smearing.*

5. Cook and chill the egg sheets
   - **Cook** thin egg sheets.
     - Heat a **ceramic nonstick pan** over **medium-low heat**.
     - Add 1 teaspoon avocado oil.
     - Pour in a thin layer of beaten egg, just enough to coat the pan.
     - Cook **1 to 2 minutes**, until fully set.
     - Repeat until all eggs are cooked, using up to 2 teaspoons more avocado oil as needed.
     - Transfer egg sheets to a plate and refrigerate **20 to 30 minutes**, until cold and firm.
     - **Checkpoint:** Egg sheets are fully set, cold, and firm enough to cut cleanly.
     - *If eggs are soft or warm -> chill longer before processing -> they are ready when they tear instead of smear.*

6. Make egg gravel
   - **Pulse** the cold egg in the food processor.
     - Cut cold egg sheets into strips or chunks first.
     - Pulse in short bursts, 1 second at a time.
     - Stop at pea-size or smaller pieces.
     - Do not run continuously.
     - Egg gravel is a convenience-first freezer compromise. Cooked egg whites can become tough and watery after freezing; mincing the egg very small reduces how noticeable that texture change is, but it does not eliminate the risk [7].
     - **Checkpoint:** Egg is gravel, not paste.
     - *If egg starts smearing -> stop immediately and finish with a knife -> it is fixed when pieces are separate again.*

7. Bloom garlic and ginger
   - **Saute** the aromatics.
     - Heat 1 tablespoon avocado oil in a **stainless steel pan** over **medium heat**.
     - Add garlic and ginger.
     - Cook **45 to 90 seconds**, stirring constantly, until fragrant.
     - Do not brown the garlic.
     - **Checkpoint:** Aromatics smell cooked and sweet, not sharp or burnt.
     - *If garlic browns -> scrape it out immediately and lower heat -> it is fixed only if there are no dark bitter bits.*

8. Finish, taste-adjust, portion, and freeze
   - **Fold** everything together.
     - In the largest **stainless steel pot or pan**, combine dried rice, roasted green beans, roasted carrots, egg gravel, garlic, ginger, and the soy-vinegar-sesame seasoning.
     - Fold gently until evenly distributed.
     - Taste-adjust loop: soy sauce -> rice vinegar -> sesame oil -> salt.
     - Add the optional 1/4 teaspoon fine sea salt only if the rice tastes flat even after soy sauce.
     - Keep the final base slightly less salty than a stand-alone fried rice because orange chicken will add sauce.
     - **Checkpoint:** Rice tastes savory and lightly seasoned, with separate grains and no wet sauce pooling.
     - *If the rice looks wet -> spread it on trays and dry at **250 deg F** for 8 to 12 minutes -> it is fixed when no liquid remains on the tray.*
   - **Cool and portion** safely.
     - Portion into 10 shallow containers or silicone molds, about 1.5 to 1.75 cups each.
     - Keep total room-temperature handling of cooked rice and finished rice under **2 hours**.
     - Refrigerate promptly in shallow portions.
     - Once cold, cover tightly and freeze.
     - **Checkpoint:** Portions are shallow, sealed, labeled, and cold before long freezer storage.

## Common Issues

- **Rice is gummy:** Too much water, insufficient drying, or crowded trays -> dry at 250 deg F longer -> grains break apart cleanly.
- **Green beans make the rice wet:** Vegetables were not roasted dry enough -> return them to 425 deg F uncovered -> no liquid remains on the tray.
- **Egg turns pasty:** Egg was warm or over-processed -> chill fully and pulse briefly -> pieces stay separate.
- **Rice tastes too salty with orange chicken:** Base was seasoned like a complete meal -> reduce soy next batch to 2 tablespoons -> orange chicken sauce supplies the rest.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 4: rice can be cooked, oven-dried, cooled, and refrigerated overnight.
  - Stop after Step 6: egg gravel can be made 1 day ahead and refrigerated.
  - Holds: finished rice portions hold 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator.
  - Serve/Reheat: microwave covered until steaming hot; add orange chicken after the rice is hot.
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: after Step 8, fully cooked, cooled, and portioned.
  - Portioning: 10 shallow portions, about 1.5 to 1.75 cups each.
  - Freeze: best within 2 months; acceptable up to 3 months for quality.
  - Thaw: overnight in the refrigerator if convenient, or microwave from frozen.
  - Reheat: covered microwave reheat with 1 to 2 teaspoons water until steaming hot; mixed leftovers should reach 165 deg F when checked with a thermometer [8].

## Reheat Plan

- **Best method:** Microwave -> covered -> 2 to 4 minutes from thawed or 4 to 7 minutes from frozen -> stir halfway -> continue until steaming hot.
- **Texture reset:** Add 1 to 2 teaspoons water before microwaving; uncover for the last 30 to 60 seconds if the rice seems wet.
- **Orange chicken timing:** Reheat the rice first. Add hot orange chicken on top after the rice is steaming.
- *If the center stays cold, then break the puck apart, re-cover, and microwave in 45-second bursts -> it is fixed when steam rises from the center.*

## Troubleshooting

- Dense frozen puck -> portion packed too tightly or too deep -> use shallower molds and do not compress -> fork breaks rice apart after reheating.
- Egg texture is noticeable -> egg pieces were too large or too soft -> chill egg harder and pulse smaller next batch -> egg disappears into rice.
- Rice is flat -> not enough soy-acid balance -> add 1 teaspoon soy sauce and 1/2 teaspoon rice vinegar after reheating -> flavor becomes savory without tasting salty.
- Reheated rice is dry -> not enough steam in microwave -> add 1 to 2 teaspoons water and cover -> grains soften without turning wet.

## Geometry Notes

- Pan/pot size used:
  - **Instant Pot - 6 QT** for rice.
  - **Sheet / baking tray, 13x9** plus **Sheet / roasting trays, various** for drying and roasting.
  - Largest **stainless steel pot or pan** for final mixing.
- Target thickness / layer depth:
  - Rice drying layer: no deeper than about 1/2 inch.
  - Rice tray surface: at least 3 x 13x9-equivalent tray surfaces for this batch.
  - Green bean roasting: 2 tray surfaces, mostly one layer, with visible metal between clusters.
  - Freezer portion depth: shallow enough to reheat evenly; do not pack hard.
- Crowding rule:
  - Use multiple trays. Do not stack wet rice or frozen vegetables in a deep layer.
  - If tray space is limited, dry rice in two rounds rather than making the layer deeper.

## Scaling & Batch Size

- Scaling notes:
  - Half batch: 1 2/3 cups dry jasmine rice, 1 5/8 cups water, 12 oz green beans, 6 oz carrots, 3 eggs.
  - Double batch: do not double in one Instant Pot run unless you have already confirmed your pot handles that rice volume cleanly.
- Batch strategy:
  - This recipe is designed around roughly 10 cups cooked rice from one Instant Pot batch.
  - Final finished yield will be higher after vegetables and egg are folded in.
- Pan swaps:
  - If short on trays, dry rice first, transfer to a holding pot, then roast vegetables.
  - Do not use a deep casserole dish for drying rice; it traps steam.

## Special Notes

- Storage & reheat: Use shallow containers, refrigerate promptly, freeze once cold, and reheat until steaming hot. USDA FSIS states leftovers should be refrigerated within 2 hours and reheated to 165 deg F [8].
- Serving ideas: Top with orange chicken; keep extra sauce separate until after reheating if possible.
- Sodium notes:
  - Top sodium drivers: low-sodium soy sauce, orange chicken sauce, and any extra salt.
  - At 3 tablespoons Kikkoman Less Sodium Soy Sauce, the batch gets 1,770 mg sodium before orange chicken [9].
  - Sodium levers: reduce soy to 2 tablespoons, use gluten-free low-sodium tamari, skip the optional salt, and avoid adding orange chicken sauce until after reheating.
- Egg texture note:
  - Egg gravel is convenience-first. Egg-free or fresh-added egg has better texture, but egg gravel gives the best batch-prepped compromise.

## Variations

1. **Egg-Free Freezer Base:** Omit the egg gravel. The texture becomes cleaner and less fried-rice-like, but it freezes more predictably.

2. **Higher-Soy Version:** Increase low-sodium soy sauce to 4 tablespoons. This makes the rice taste more complete by itself, but it raises sodium and is more likely to clash with orange chicken sauce.

3. **Drier Sheet-Pan Finish:** After folding everything together, spread the finished rice on trays and dry at 250 deg F for 10 to 15 minutes. This is the best correction if the soy seasoning or vegetables make the batch seem wet.

4. **Fresh Egg Reheat Version:** Freeze the rice without egg, then scramble 1 egg fresh when reheating a portion and fold it in. This has the best egg texture but adds weekday work.

## Safety & Correctness Notes

- Do not leave cooked rice or finished fried rice sitting at room temperature beyond the 2-hour limit.
- Keep cumulative room-temperature handling from rice finishing pressure-cook through final refrigeration under 2 hours.
- Do not process warm egg in the food processor; it is more likely to smear into paste.
- This recipe uses oven drying and roasting to solve the two major freezer failures: wet rice and watery frozen vegetables.
