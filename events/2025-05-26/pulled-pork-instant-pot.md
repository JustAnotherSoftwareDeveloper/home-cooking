# One-Batch 6 lb Instant Pot Pulled Pork Butt

## Title & Overview

This is a **one-batch, 6 lb Instant Pot pulled pork butt** built for Memorial Day BBQ service. The pork is fully rubbed the night before, skillet-seared for browned flavor, then pressure-cooked until shreddable. The sauce is intentionally separate: this recipe produces deeply seasoned, moist pulled pork that can take your homemade BBQ sauce at service without becoming wet or muddy. Because this is a 6 QT Instant Pot capacity-push, the priorities are chunk size, fill level, restrained liquid, and careful jus reduction.

## Yield & Timing

- **Yield:** 12 to 18 servings; about 3.75 to 4.5 lb finished pulled pork from 6 lb raw boneless pork butt.
- **Active Prep:** 60 to 75 minutes total.
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 8 to 24 hours overnight rub; 20 minutes natural release; 10 minutes post-cook rest.
- **Cook:** About 2 hours 20 minutes to 2 hours 50 minutes day-of.
- **Total:** Overnight plus about 2.5 to 3 hours day-of.
- **Make-ahead:** Best path is rub the pork the night before, cook the next day, chill unsauced with reserved jus, skim fat, then reheat for service. Cooked leftovers should be refrigerated within 2 hours and used within 3 to 4 days [9].

### Timing Breakdown

| Phase                         |         Time |

## Equipment & Tools

- Instant Pot — 6 QT — required; this is a capacity-push batch, so the pork and liquid must stay below the pressure-cooking max-fill line.
- Cast iron skillet — preferred for browning the rubbed pork.
- Carbon steel skillet — substitute for cast iron; it heats faster, so reduce heat sooner if the rub darkens quickly.
- Stainless steel pots and pans — substitute for cast iron; preheat well and use enough oil to reduce sticking.

## Allergy & Dietary Notes

- **Contains:** pork; possible fish, gluten, or soy depending on Worcestershire sauce brand.
- **Easy swaps:**
  - Worcestershire sauce -> 1 extra tablespoon apple cider vinegar plus 1 teaspoon brown sugar -> less umami depth, but the pressure-cook method still works.
  - Stock -> water -> slightly less body in the jus, but fine because the pork and rub carry the flavor.
- **Hard swaps:**
  - Pork loin or tenderloin -> not recommended -> too lean for pulled pork and likely to become dry/stringy.
  - Bone-in 6 lb pork butt -> not recommended for one 6 QT batch -> the bone and irregular geometry make capacity and even cooking worse.

## Quick Overview

- **What you are making:** One-batch, overnight-rubbed, skillet-seared pulled pork butt for BBQ sandwiches or a self-serve tray.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Pork is done when the chunks shred with almost no resistance and the connective tissue feels soft and gelatinous.
- **Workflow:** Rub overnight; while the skillet preheats, slice onion and measure liquid; while pork pressure-cooks, prepare sauce, slaw, buns, and serving setup.
- **Make-ahead:** Best party workflow is cook unsauced pork ahead, chill with jus separately, skim fat, then reheat and sauce at service.
- **Primary risks:** Overfilling the 6 QT pot, scorching the sugar in the rub, and adding too much greasy cooking liquid back to the shredded pork.

## Gather & Stage

- Trim only loose hard fat from the pork; keep marbling and soft fat attached.
- Cut pork into **2.5- to 3-inch chunks**.
- Mix the overnight BBQ rub until evenly combined.
- Slice the onion.
- Measure the stock, apple cider vinegar, and Worcestershire sauce.
- Set out the Instant Pot — 6 QT, cast iron skillet, Sheet / roasting trays, and a clean holding area for browned pork.
- **Checkpoint:** pork is chunked, rub is mixed, onion is sliced, liquid is measured, and searing equipment is ready.

## Instructions

1. Rub the pork overnight
   - **Season** the pork chunks thoroughly with the full rub.
     - Coat all sides, including seams and fatty edges.
     - Arrange the pork on Sheet / roasting trays or two Sheet / baking tray — 13×9 trays.
     - *Optional:* Set the pork on Wire racks if refrigerating uncovered for better airflow.
     - **Chill** 8 to 24 hours, loosely covered or uncovered if refrigerator space allows.
     - The rub may look damp or pasty after resting; that is expected. Dry-brining works by letting salt draw out moisture, dissolve, and then season the meat more deeply over time [1].
     - **Checkpoint:** pork is evenly coated and no dry piles of rub remain on the tray.
     - *If rub collects in clumps -> massage it back over the pork surfaces -> the pork should look evenly stained and seasoned.*

2. Skillet-sear the pork
   - **Preheat** the Cast iron skillet over **medium-high heat** for 4 to 6 minutes, then add 1 tablespoon avocado oil.
     - Sear in batches with space between pieces.
     - For this 6 lb batch, do not chase perfect browning on every face; sear 2 sides per chunk, 60 to 90 seconds per side.
     - Move browned pork to a clean tray or directly into the Instant Pot — 6 QT as space allows.
     - Add the remaining oil as needed between batches.
     - **Checkpoint:** pork has browned patches and smells roasted/spiced, not burnt.
     - *If the rub turns black or smells bitter -> lower to medium heat and shorten each side to 30 to 45 seconds -> later batches should brown without heavy smoke.*
     - *If the pork steams instead of browns -> remove some pieces and let the skillet recover heat -> the next piece should sizzle immediately on contact.*

3. Load the Instant Pot and deglaze
   - **Layer** the sliced onion in the bottom of the Instant Pot — 6 QT.
     - Add the seared pork chunks.
     - Check the fill level before adding liquid; the pot must remain below the pressure-cooking max-fill line.
   - **Deglaze** the skillet over **medium heat** with about 1/2 cup of the measured stock or water.
     - Scrape browned bits for 1 to 2 minutes, until the bottom of the skillet is mostly clean.
     - Pour this deglazing liquid into the Instant Pot.
     - Add the remaining stock or water, apple cider vinegar, and Worcestershire sauce.
     - Do not add BBQ sauce before pressure cooking.
     - **Checkpoint:** pork is packed but not compressed above the max-fill line, and thin liquid is visible at the bottom.
     - *If the pork is above the max-fill line -> remove enough pork to stay below the line -> do not force the 6 QT batch.*

4. Pressure-cook
   - **Pressure Cook** on **High Pressure** for **65 minutes**.
     - Let pressure release naturally for **20 minutes**.
     - Quick-release any remaining pressure.
     - Open the lid carefully, tilting it away from you.
     - Test a center chunk and a top chunk for tenderness.
     - This fixed 65-minute timing sits between common pressure-cooker pork-shoulder references: 60 minutes plus natural release, 75 minutes plus natural release, and 60 minutes for pork shoulder-style pressure-cooker methods [2][3][4].
     - **Checkpoint:** pork should pull apart easily.
     - *If the pork mostly shreds but has a few tight spots -> close the lid and rest in the hot liquid 10 minutes -> the residual heat should loosen the tight spots.*
     - *If several chunks resist shredding -> pressure-cook 10 more minutes on High Pressure, then natural release 10 minutes -> pork is fixed when fibers separate without force.*

5. Shred and reduce the jus
   - **Transfer** pork to Sheet / roasting trays and rest 10 minutes.
     - Keep the pork in a shallow layer; use two trays if needed.
     - Remove obvious large fatty pockets while shredding.
     - Strain or scoop onion solids from the cooking liquid if you want a cleaner jus.
   - **Reduce** the cooking liquid uncovered on Instant Pot Sauté mode for 10 to 18 minutes.
     - Stir occasionally.
     - Stop when the liquid tastes concentrated and lightly glossy.
     - Skim visible fat from the top, or chill and remove hardened fat if making ahead.
     - Pressure-cooker pork commonly produces excess liquid; reduction and controlled add-back prevent watery final pork [6][7].
     - **Checkpoint:** reduced jus tastes savory and porky, not watery or oily.
     - *If the liquid tastes greasy -> stop reducing and skim more fat -> the jus should coat a spoon lightly without tasting oily.*

6. Finish and taste-adjust
   - **Moisten** the shredded pork gradually with reduced jus.
     - Start with 3/4 cup reduced jus.
     - Add more by 2-tablespoon increments only until the pork glistens.
     - Do not dump all cooking liquid back into the pork.
     - Keep homemade BBQ sauce separate until service, or toss in just enough sauce to lightly coat right before serving.
     - Taste-adjust loop: reduced jus for savoriness/moisture -> apple cider vinegar for brightness -> pinch of salt if flat -> black pepper or cayenne if dull -> sauce only after the pork already tastes good.
     - **Checkpoint:** final pork is tender, juicy, seasoned through, and not sitting in a puddle.

## Common Issues

- **Rub scorches during sear:** Sugar and paprika are burning -> lower heat and shorten contact time -> next batch smells roasted, not bitter.
- **Pork is tough after 65 minutes:** Pieces are large or the pot was densely packed -> add 10 minutes high pressure with 10 minutes natural release -> pork shreds without force.
- **Pork is watery:** Too much unreduced liquid was added back -> reduce jus harder and add back gradually -> no liquid pools under the pork.
- **Pork tastes flat:** Cooking liquid diluted the rub -> reduce jus, add vinegar, then salt cautiously -> pork tastes seasoned before sauce.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 5.
  - Store shredded pork and reduced jus separately in shallow containers.
  - Holds: 3 to 4 days refrigerated [9].
  - Serve/Reheat: reheat pork covered with a few spoonfuls of defatted jus until it reaches 165°F, then taste-adjust and serve with homemade sauce [9].

- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: after shredding and moistening lightly with reduced jus, before BBQ sauce.
  - Portioning: freeze in flat bags or 1- to 2-cup portions.
  - Freeze: best quality within 3 months.
  - Thaw: overnight in refrigerator.
  - Reheat: covered, with a splash of jus or water, until hot throughout; then refresh with vinegar, salt, pepper, and sauce as needed.

## Troubleshooting

- Pork tastes bitter -> rub scorched during skillet sear -> remove blackened patches and finish with brighter sauce/acid -> bitterness is no longer dominant.
- Pork is greasy -> too much rendered fat added back -> skim or chill-separate jus before moistening -> liquid tastes meaty instead of oily.
- Pork is dry after reheating -> reheated without enough covered moisture -> add defatted jus, cover, and warm gently -> strands loosen and glisten.
- Pork is mushy/wet -> too much liquid was mixed back in -> spread on trays 5 to 10 minutes to steam off, then sauce lightly -> pork holds loose strands again.

## Geometry Notes

- **Pot size used:** Instant Pot — 6 QT.
- **Batch size:** 6 lb raw boneless pork butt is a one-batch capacity push.
- **Target chunk size:** 2.5 to 3 inches.
- **Crowding rule:** pork may be snug in the pot, but it must not be compressed above the max-fill line.
- **Liquid depth:** liquid should sit below the top of the pork, not submerge it.
- **Tray depth:** shredded pork should sit in a shallow layer, ideally under 1 inch deep, while resting or cooling.
- **Searing rule:** sear 2 sides per chunk; full browning on all sides is not worth the time or scorch risk for this batch size.

## Scaling & Batch Size

- This recipe is locked for **one 6 lb raw boneless pork butt batch** in an Instant Pot — 6 QT.
- Do not use this exact recipe as-is for 4 lb or 8 lb.
- For 8 lb, split into two separate pressure-cooker batches instead of forcing the pot.
- Cook time scaling should follow chunk size and pot crowding, not pounds alone.

## Special Notes

- **Sauce strategy:** Sauce belongs after cooking. Pressure-cooking BBQ sauce risks scorching, dilution, and muddy flavor.
- **Best serving setup:** Keep pork warm with a little defatted jus; serve sauce, slaw, pickles, and buns separately.
- **Holding:** Hot pork for service should stay at **140°F or warmer** [9].
- **Reheating leftovers:** Reheat leftovers to **165°F** [9].
- **Texture expectation:** Instant Pot pulled pork is a wet-environment method. It gives tenderness, not real bark. If you want browned edges, use the optional tray finish; method tests and user reports both point toward post-cook browning/reduction as the fix for pressure-cooker softness [6][8].

## Variations

1. **Crisped tray finish:** After shredding, toss pork with reduced jus, spread on Sheet / roasting trays, and broil briefly until edges brown. This improves texture, but it adds a day-of finishing step.

2. **Pepper-forward Carolina-compatible pork:** Increase black pepper to 2 tablespoons and reduce brown sugar to 2 tablespoons. This works better with vinegar or mustard-based BBQ sauces.

3. **Sweeter cookout pork:** Increase brown sugar to 1/4 cup, but sear over medium heat instead of medium-high. This gives a rounder sweet BBQ profile but increases scorch risk.

4. **No-sear fallback:** Skip skillet searing and pressure-cook after the overnight rub, then reduce the jus more aggressively. The pork will be tender and easier, but less browned and less BBQ-like.

## Safety & Correctness Notes

- Keep hot pork at **140°F or warmer** during service, refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, use refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days, and reheat leftovers to **165°F** [9].
- Dry-rubbing overnight is doing real work here: it improves seasoning depth and helps the pork surface brown better before pressure cooking [1].
- Pressure-cooking is not a bark-building method. The skillet sear and optional tray finish are included to compensate for that limitation [6][8].
