# Date-Night Integrated Runbook

## Pan-Seared New York Strip with Shallot–Cabernet Reduction + Instant Pot Red Wine Risotto with Small Taleggio Finish

## Purpose

This is a single execution document for cooking both dishes together, targeting a **comfortable 90-minute start-to-plate workflow** for **one cook**.

The workflow is built around one principle: do the lower-risk prep while sauces or appliances are working, then reserve full attention for the steak sear. The Cabernet sauce base is made before the steak goes in the pan. The risotto is pressure-cooked before the steak sear but finished during the steak rest so it stays loose, glossy, and side-dish-like rather than stiff.

The steak method depends on a **dry surface, high heat, neutral oil first, butter late, and frequent flipping after the initial sear**. That combination gives a better crust with less gray band.

---

# Read This First

- **Sauce base early, finish late:** reduce Cabernet, shallot, thyme, and beef stock early; add steak resting juices and cold butter only at the end.
- **Risotto cooks before steak, finishes during rest:** pressure-cook the risotto before the steak sear, but add butter, Parmesan, Taleggio, and final hot stock during the steak rest.
- **Steak sear is no-multitask:** once steaks hit cast iron, do not work on risotto or sauce.
- **Thermometer over clock:** timing tables are useful, but steak pull temperature controls doneness.
- **Do not flood the risotto:** Cabernet sauce belongs on the steak side of the plate.
- **Start the 90-minute clock only after the Before You Start checklist is physically visible.**

This is the station map. The timeline below tells you when to do each task, but this section tells you what “ready” means.

- Wire rack is inside the 13×9 metal tray.
- Steaks are salted and air-drying.
- Cast iron skillet is clean and dry.
- Tongs, thermometer, avocado oil, butter, and thyme are within reach.

- Sauce shallot is minced.
- Cabernet and beef stock are measured.
- 2 thyme sprigs are ready for the sauce.
- Cold butter is held cold for the finish.

- Instant Pot insert is clean and dry.

- Parmesan is grated.

- Stock is warmed, divided, and kept **hot but not boiling**:

  - 1/2 cup hot stock reserved for finishing
- Final butter, cheese, and reserved hot stock are not added until steak rest.

# Target Service Model

## Serves

- **Steak:** 2 servings
- **Risotto:** 4 side servings
- Expect leftover risotto.

## Target eating model

Steak-forward plated dinner with risotto as a rich but restrained side.

## Texture goals

- **Steak crust:** dark brown, not black
- **Steak center:** warm red-pink medium-rare, springy with give
- **Sauce:** glossy, savory, lightly clingy, not harsh or broken
- **Risotto:** loose, glossy, all’onda / wavy; spreads slowly, not stiff like mashed potatoes

## Primary risks

- Wet steak surface
- Under-reduced wine in either sauce or risotto
- Overheating buttered Cabernet sauce
- Finishing risotto too early and letting it tighten
- Too much Taleggio making the risotto dominate the steak
- Losing time by doing prep serially instead of during reduction / pressure-cook windows

If the Instant Pot is still releasing at **T-26**, finish the release and stir the risotto first, then start cast iron preheat. Service shifts **3–8 minutes**. Do not manage hot pressure release and cast-iron setup at the same time.

# T-90 to T-86 — Salt Steaks and Refrigerate

## Steak dry-brine

1. Set **Wire racks** inside the **Sheet / baking tray — 13×9, metal, light**.
2. Put steaks on the rack.
3. Season all over with:
   - **1 1/4 teaspoons kosher salt**, about 7 g
   - **1/2 teaspoon black pepper**, about 1 g
4. Refrigerate uncovered.

**Checkpoint:** steaks are salted, uncovered, and air-drying in the fridge.

## Brine timing guardrail

If you miss this opening dry-brine window, salt the steaks **immediately before they go into the pan**. Do **not** salt them 5 to 30 minutes before cooking; that is the worst window for browning.

---

# T-86 to T-82 — Mince Sauce Shallot and Pull Sauce-Critical Tools

## Sauce shallot

1. Finely mince **1 large shallot** for the Cabernet sauce.
2. Keep it by the stainless steel pan.

## Pull only what you need for the next block

- Stainless steel pan
- Avocado oil
- Cabernet
- Beef stock
- Thyme
- Whisk or spoon
- Cold butter, still cold and portioned if possible

**Checkpoint:** the first sauce step can begin immediately at T-82.

---

# T-82 to T-55 — Make Cabernet Sauce Base + Prep Risotto

This is the main consolidation block. The sauce has simmer windows; use those windows for risotto prep.

## T-82 to T-79 — Start sauce base

In a small stainless steel pan:

1. Heat **1 teaspoon avocado oil** over **medium heat**.
2. Add:
   - **1 large shallot**, finely minced, about 1/3 cup
3. **Saute** 2–3 minutes, stirring often, until softened and lightly golden.

## T-79 to T-65 — Reduce Cabernet

Add:

- **1 cup dry Cabernet Sauvignon**
- **2 thyme sprigs**

Simmer over **medium to medium-low heat** for **10–14 minutes**, until reduced to about **3 tablespoons** and no longer raw or boozy.

## While Cabernet reduces

Prep risotto components and measure the next sauce liquid:

1. Dice the risotto shallot very finely.
2. Grate Parmesan.
3. Cube Taleggio small.
4. Measure rice.
5. Measure risotto wine.
6. Measure **3/4 cup low-sodium beef stock** for the sauce.
7. Portion butter:
   - risotto base butter
   - risotto finish butter
   - steak basting butter
   - sauce finish butter

**One-cook guardrail:** check and stir the sauce every 2–3 minutes. Do not walk away from a reducing wine sauce.

## T-65 to T-55 — Add beef stock and finish sauce base

Add:

- **3/4 cup low-sodium beef stock**

Simmer **8–12 minutes**, until reduced to about **1/2 cup** and lightly coating a spoon.

## While beef stock reduces — mandatory risotto stock setup

1. Measure and warm **4 cups risotto stock**.
2. Separate:
   - **3 1/2 cups hot stock** for pressure-cooking
   - **1/2 cup hot stock** reserved for finishing
3. Keep both portions **hot but not boiling**.
4. Put cheeses back in the fridge if the kitchen is warm.
5. Set Instant Pot area clean and ready.

## At T-60 — pull steaks from fridge

1. Pull steaks from the refrigerator.
2. Leave uncovered on the rack at room temperature.
3. Do not pat dry yet.

**Checkpoint:** steaks have had about 30 minutes uncovered in the fridge and will get about 40 minutes at room temperature before searing.

## Finish sauce base

When sauce base is reduced to about **1/2 cup**:

1. Remove and discard the thyme sprigs.
2. Take pan off heat or hold on very low.

## Stop sauce here

Do **not** add:

- steak resting juices
- final cold butter
- final vinegar

**Checkpoint:** sauce base is done, thyme is removed, risotto ingredients are staged, stock is hot.

## Sauce base corrections

**If sauce tastes harsh:**  
Simmer 1–2 minutes longer.  
Cue it is fixed: raw alcohol edge drops away.

**If sauce gets too tight before butter is added:**  
Whisk in 1–2 tablespoons water or stock.  
Cue it is fixed: it loosens back to a light sauce consistency.

---

# T-55 to T-44 — Build Risotto Base

Use **Instant Pot — 6 QT** on **Saute / medium-high**.

## Add

- **1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil**
- **1 tablespoon unsalted butter**
- **1 large shallot**, very finely diced

**Saute** 2–3 minutes, until translucent but not browned.

## Then

1. Add **1 1/2 cups arborio or carnaroli rice**.
2. **Toast** 1–2 minutes, stirring, until glossy and some edges turn slightly translucent.
3. Add **3/4 cup dry red wine**.
4. **Cook** 1–2 minutes, stirring and scraping, until wine is mostly evaporated and no longer raw-smelling.
5. Add:
   - **3 1/2 cups hot stock**
   - **1/2 teaspoon kosher salt**
   - **1/2 teaspoon black pepper**
6. Scrape the bottom thoroughly.
7. Lock lid and set valve to sealing.

**Checkpoint:** rice is glossy, bottom is cleanly scraped, and wine no longer smells raw.

## Risotto base corrections

**If shallot starts browning too hard:**  
Drop in 1 tablespoon hot stock and lower heat briefly.  
Cue it is fixed: pot smells sweet and winey, not toasted or sharp.

**If bottom has stuck-on rice before sealing:**  
Scrape thoroughly after adding stock.  
Cue it is fixed: bottom feels smooth with no rice glued down.

---

# T-44 to T-30 — Pressure-Cook Risotto + Prep Steak Station

## Risotto

1. Pressure Cook High for **5 minutes**.
2. Quick-release immediately.
3. Open lid.
4. Stir risotto vigorously for **30–45 seconds**.
5. Turn Instant Pot off.
6. Leave unfinished.

## Do not add yet

- final butter
- Parmesan
- Taleggio
- reserved stock
- vinegar

**Checkpoint:** rice should look a little wetter than “done.” That is correct.

## Immediate post-opening corrections

**If rice seems slightly firm after opening:**  
Do not panic. It will continue softening slightly during the short hold and finish.

**If rice seems very stiff already:**  
Add **1–2 tablespoons hot stock**, stir, and leave it loose.  
Cue it is fixed: the rice is no longer sitting as a dense mound before the final cheese finish.

## Pressure-cook adjustment

If you know your Instant Pot runs cool, or you prefer slightly softer rice, use **6 minutes** instead of 5.

Cue it is fixed: grains are still intact, but risotto needs less finish-stock to loosen.

## While risotto cooks

This is a safe parallel window.

1. Clear steak-sear area.
2. Confirm sauce base is nearby.
3. Confirm steak tools are in hand:
   - tongs
   - thermometer
   - basting butter
   - thyme
   - avocado oil

---

# T-30 to T-26 — Timing Buffer / Steak Station Reset

Use this short buffer to prevent the workflow from becoming chaotic.

## Do

- Confirm risotto is cooked, stirred, and being held unfinished.
- Confirm sauce base is warmable and unfinished.
- Confirm steaks are still uncovered on the rack.
- Confirm cast iron is clean and dry.
- Confirm oil, butter, thyme, tongs, and thermometer are within reach.

**Checkpoint:** no active pressure release is happening before cast iron preheat starts.

---

# T-26 to T-20 — Cast Iron Preheat + Final Steak Setup

This block protects the full pan preheat.

## T-26 — Start pan

1. Put cast iron skillet over **high heat**.
2. Preheat **4–5 minutes**, until glossy-hot with faint wisps of smoke.

## While pan preheats

1. Pat steaks very dry on all sides.
2. Keep them on the rack until the pan is ready.
3. Put Cabernet sauce base over **low heat** just to loosen.
4. Do not add final butter to sauce.
5. Do not simmer sauce hard.

## T-21 to T-20 — Oil pan

1. Confirm pan is fully hot.
2. Add **1 tablespoon avocado oil**.
3. Swirl to coat.

**Checkpoint:** steak surface is dry and tacky; pan is fully hot; sauce base is warm but unfinished.

---

# T-20 to T-10 — Sear Steaks

During this block, do **nothing else**. Steak is the bottleneck.

## Pull temps

| Thickness  | Pull temp |
| ---------- | --------: |
| 1 1/4 inch | 120–123°F |
| 1 1/2 inch | 122–125°F |

Thermometer overrules clock.

## Approximate timing

| Steak thickness | First side |    Second side |   Flip/baste phase | Total pan time |        Rest |
| --------------- | ---------: | -------------: | -----------------: | -------------: | ----------: |
| 1 1/4 inch      |      2 min | 1 1/2 to 2 min | 1 1/2 to 2 1/2 min | 5 to 6 1/2 min |  7 to 8 min |
| 1 1/2 inch      |      2 min |          2 min |     2 1/2 to 4 min | 6 1/2 to 8 min | 8 to 10 min |

Use this table only for **1 1/4- to 1 1/2-inch steaks** in a fully preheated cast iron skillet with no crowding. Thermometer overrules clock.

## Method

1. Lay steaks into hot cast iron with space between them.
2. **Sear** first side for **2 minutes** without moving.
3. Flip.
4. **Sear** second side for **1 1/2–2 minutes**.
5. Lower heat to **medium-high**.
6. Add:
   - **1 tablespoon unsalted butter**
   - **2 thyme sprigs**
7. Flip every **30–60 seconds**.
8. Baste during the final phase.
9. Sear fat edge **20–30 seconds** if needed.
10. Transfer steaks to rack.
11. Rest **7–10 minutes**.

**Checkpoint:** crust is deep brown, not black; center still has springy give.

## Steak corrections

**If butter starts smoking hard or darkening:**  
Lower heat to medium.  
Cue it is fixed: butter foams instead of blackening.

**If steaks color too fast before the center catches up:**  
Keep flipping and lower slightly to medium-high.  
Cue it is fixed: browning stays deep, not burnt.

**If steaks are not browning:**  
Pan was underheated or steak surface was wet. Continue in 30-second increments, but prioritize pull temperature.

**If steak has a thick gray band:**  
It likely sat too long on each side. For future: flip more frequently after the first crust forms so the center stays redder.

**If steak timing feels off:**  
Thickness or starting temperature differed from the table. Trust pull temperature and steak feel over the clock.

---

# T-10 to T-5 — Finish Risotto During Steak Rest

Use the steak rest window.

## Add to risotto

1. Stir vigorously **45–60 seconds**.
2. Add **1 tablespoon unsalted butter**.
3. Add **1/3 cup finely grated Parmesan**.
4. Stir until melted.
5. Add **2 ounces Taleggio**, cubed small.
6. Stir **30–45 seconds**, just until mostly melted.
7. Add reserved hot stock **1–2 tablespoons at a time** until loose and glossy.

## Taste-adjust

- **Acid:** a few drops red wine vinegar only if flat or cheese-heavy
- **Salt:** only after cheese is in
- **Heat:** black pepper
- **Herbs:** thyme if using

**Checkpoint:** risotto moves in a slow wave. It should not mound.

## Risotto corrections

**If risotto is stiff and mound-like:**  
Add hot stock 1 tablespoon at a time and stir vigorously.  
Cue it is fixed: it settles into a soft wave.

**If Taleggio flavor jumps out too aggressively:**  
Add 1–2 tablespoons hot stock and 1 tablespoon Parmesan.  
Cue it is fixed: finish smells creamy and savory, not pungent.

**If risotto tastes too salty:**  
Use hot unsalted stock or water to loosen slightly.  
Cue it is fixed: saltiness is diluted and texture is glossy.

**If wine tastes harsh:**  
For now, balance with a small amount of butter or cheese and avoid extra acid.  
For next time, reduce wine longer before sealing.

---

# T-5 to T-2 — Finish Cabernet Sauce

## Add

- warm Cabernet sauce base
- steak resting juices

## Finish

1. Keep sauce over **low heat** or off heat.
2. Whisk in **2 tablespoons cold unsalted butter**, piece by piece.
3. Add remaining black pepper as needed.
4. Add red wine vinegar a few drops at a time only if rich but flat.
5. Add up to the remaining **1/4 teaspoon kosher salt** only if the sauce or final plate tastes underseasoned.
6. Do **not boil** once butter is in.

**Checkpoint:** sauce is glossy and lightly clings to a spoon.

## Sauce corrections

**If sauce looks greasy or broken:**  
Whisk in 1 tablespoon water over very low heat.  
Cue it is fixed: sauce comes back together and turns glossy.

**If sauce tightens while holding:**  
Whisk in 1 teaspoon water at a time.  
Cue it is fixed: sauce loosens without losing gloss.

**If sauce tastes harsh or boozy:**  
If butter has not yet been added, simmer very briefly before adding butter.  
If butter is already added, avoid boiling; use very low heat and a small splash of water if needed.

---

# T-2 to T — Plate and Final Taste-Adjust

## Conditional plating rule

If the steak is **unsauced or only lightly finished**, spoon risotto onto warm plates first, then slice and set the steak partly beside or just over the edge.

If using the **Cabernet pan sauce**, establish the steak side of the plate first and spoon sauce on the steak side only so it does not flood the risotto.

## For this sauced steak

1. Establish the steak side of the plate first.
2. Spoon risotto beside it, not underneath the sauce path.
3. Place steak whole or sliced across the grain.
4. Spoon Cabernet sauce under and around steak.
5. Keep sauce off the main body of the risotto.
6. Serve immediately.

## Final taste-adjust loop

Adjust in this order:

1. **Acid:** a few drops red wine vinegar if the sauce or risotto tastes rich but flat
2. **Salt:** remaining kosher salt only if needed
3. **Heat:** black pepper
4. **Herbs:** thyme if using

**Final checkpoint:** steak is medium-rare with crisp crust and red-pink center. Sauce is glossy and deep rather than sharp. Risotto is loose, glossy, and supportive rather than dominant.

# Equipment and Tools

## Critical

- **Cast iron skillet** — critical for steak sear and reliable crust
- **Instant Pot — 6 QT** — required for this risotto batch size and liquid ratio
- **Stainless steel pots and pans** — for Cabernet sauce base, gentle sauce reheating, and optional stock warming
- **Sheet / baking tray — 13×9, metal, light** — for dry-brining, resting, and catching steak juices
- **Wire racks** — keeps steak surface exposed and dry during brining/resting
- **Thermometer** — strongly preferred; steak timing table is useful, but thermometer pull temp should overrule the clock

## Required / useful prep tools

- **9-in chef knife** — for shallot prep
- **Tongs** — for steak flipping and fat-cap searing
- **Whisk** — for mounting Cabernet sauce with cold butter
- **Wooden spoon or silicone spatula** — for risotto scraping and stirring
- **Small bowls** — for staging measured ingredients
- **Microwave / convection oven combo** — preferred for warming risotto stock

## Optional / substitutes

- **Carbon steel skillet** can substitute for cast iron for the steak. It heats faster and works similarly, but lower heat slightly sooner if fond or butter darkens too quickly.
- **Stainless steel pan** can substitute for cast iron only if needed, but it has lower heat retention and higher sticking risk. Preheat well and use adequate fat.
- **Stainless steel pots and pans** can warm risotto stock if the microwave / convection oven combo is occupied.
- **Ceramic nonstick should not be used for the steak sear.** It is not a high-heat searing tool.

# Allergy and Dietary Notes

## Contains

- Dairy
- Alcohol

## Easy swaps that keep technique intact

### Steak sauce

- **Beef stock → low-sodium chicken stock**  
  The sauce will be slightly lighter and less steakhouse-like. No technique change beyond tasting for salt later.

- **Butter finish → 1 teaspoon avocado oil**  
  The sauce will be less glossy and less rounded. Do not boil after adding.

### Risotto

- **Chicken stock → vegetable stock**  
  The finish will be slightly cleaner and less beef-adjacent. No method change.

- **Taleggio 2 ounces → 1 ounce Taleggio + a little extra Parmesan**  
  The result will be milder, firmer, and more Parmesan-led. No method change.

## Hard swaps / not recommended

### Steak

- **Thin steaks under 1 inch** are not recommended. The timing window becomes much tighter, and the timing table is no longer reliable. Buy thicker steaks instead.
- **Sweet red wine** is not recommended. It pushes the reduction sweet and muddy. Use dry Cabernet or another dry red.

### Risotto

- **Long-grain rice** is not a substitute for arborio or carnaroli. It breaks the creamy suspended-starch texture and pressure timing. Use risotto rice.
- **A large Taleggio increase** is not recommended. It pushes the side toward a richer standalone risotto and makes it compete with the steak. If that is the goal, make a stovetop Taleggio risotto instead of this steak-side build.

# Common Issues

## Weak steak crust

**Cause:** steak surface was wet or pan was underheated.  
**Fix:** dry steak better and preheat longer.  
**Quick check:** browning should happen faster and darker.

## Sauce tastes too winey or tannic

**Cause:** wine did not reduce far enough before stock.  
**Fix:** reduce longer before the stock stage.  
**Quick check:** raw edge should disappear.

## Sauce looks oily

**Cause:** sauce over-reduced or got too hot after butter.  
**Fix:** whisk in water over low heat.  
**Quick check:** sauce re-emulsifies and turns glossy.

## Risotto bottom sticking during sauté

**Cause:** not enough scraping after wine or stock went in.  
**Fix:** scrape thoroughly before sealing.  
**Quick check:** bottom should feel smooth with no rice glued down.

## Risotto too salty at the end

**Cause:** stock, Parmesan, and Taleggio stacked up.  
**Fix:** use low-sodium stock and hold back extra salt until the finish.  
**Quick check:** final seasoning happens after cheese is in.

## Sauce flooding the risotto

**Cause:** steak sauce and risotto were plated without separation.  
**Fix:** plate sauce on steak side only.  
**Quick check:** risotto stays glossy, not diluted.

# Troubleshooting

| Symptom                         | Likely cause                                          | Primary fix                                                              | Recovery cue                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Sauce is harsh or boozy         | Wine did not reduce far enough                        | Simmer a little longer before adding stock or before adding final butter | Sharp alcohol note fades                              |
| Sauce broke after butter        | Overheated or over-reduced                            | Whisk in 1 tablespoon water over very low heat                           | Sauce turns glossy again                              |
| Sauce tightens while holding    | Reduction cooled or over-concentrated                 | Whisk in 1 teaspoon water at a time                                      | Sauce loosens without losing gloss                    |
| Steak has a thick gray band     | It sat too long on each side                          | Flip more frequently after first crust forms                             | Center stays redder with less overcooked band         |
| Steak timing felt off           | Thickness or starting temperature differed from table | Trust pull temperature and steak feel over clock                         | Medium-rare lands more reliably                       |
| Risotto is stiff and mound-like | It sat too long or finished too dry                   | Add hot stock 1 tablespoon at a time and stir vigorously                 | It settles into a soft wave                           |
| Risotto wine tastes harsh       | Wine did not reduce enough before pressure-cooking    | For future: simmer wine longer on Saute before sealing                   | Aroma shifts from boozy to mellow                     |
| Taleggio takes over the plate   | Too much washed-rind character for a steak side       | Keep Taleggio small and lean on Parmesan for structure                   | Cheese reads as a finish note, not the whole identity |

# Geometry Notes

## Steak

- **Pan/pot size used:** 12-inch cast iron skillet for steaks; small stainless steel pan for sauce
- **Target steak thickness:** 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches
- **Crowding rule:** 2 steaks max only if they do not touch
- If steaks crowd the pan, cook 1 at a time.
- Carbon steel skillet is the best substitute.
- Stainless steel pots and pans work as a pan fallback only with higher sticking risk and less heat retention.

## Risotto

- **Pan/pot size used:** Instant Pot — 6 QT
- **Target final texture:** loose enough to spread slightly when spooned; not soupy, not scoopable like mashed potatoes
- **Crowding/batch rule:** do not push past 1 1/2 cups risotto rice in this format if the goal is polished steak-side texture
- This recipe is written specifically for Instant Pot — 6 QT. Pan swaps are not recommended.

# Scaling and Batch Size

## Steak

- The sauce doubles easily.
- The steak cook does not scale as easily.
- For 4 steaks, use 2 skillets or cook in batches and hold the first batch only briefly.
- If cooking in batches, expect some quality loss on the first batch unless timing is tight and rest is controlled.

## Risotto

Halves cleanly to:

- 3/4 cup rice
- 6 tablespoons wine
- 1 3/4 cups stock in the pot
- extra hot stock for finishing

For more than 4 side servings, a second batch is safer than scaling up aggressively.

Do not exceed the Instant Pot 6 QT geometry if texture matters.

# Make-Ahead, Storage, Freezer, and Reheat Notes

## Steak fridge plan

- Stop after sauce base reduction or after salting steaks.
- Sauce base holds **2 days**.
- Steaks hold **up to 24 hours** uncovered on the rack.
- Serve/reheat: rewarm sauce base over low heat until fluid, then finish with resting juices and butter at the end.

## Steak freezer plan

- **Best freeze point:** raw steaks, individually wrapped
- **Portioning:** 1 steak per package
- **Freeze:** quality best within several months; frozen beef remains safe indefinitely at 0°F
- **Thaw:** overnight in refrigerator
- **Reheat:** not applicable; cook from thawed using the recipe

## Steak leftovers

- Refrigerate promptly.
- Reheat gently.
- Sauce may need 1 to 2 teaspoons water or stock when rewarmed because buttered reductions tighten as they sit.
- Do not aggressively boil the sauce when reheating.

## Risotto fridge plan

- Stop before building the risotto base.
- Complete gather/stage fully, then refrigerate diced shallot and cheeses separately.
- Keep measured rice, wine, and stock ready to go.
- Holds: 1 day before cooking.
- Serve/reheat: finish from fresh.

## Risotto leftovers

- Finished risotto is best fresh.
- Leftovers hold 1 day.
- Reheat gently in stainless steel pots and pans or on Saute / Low with a splash of hot stock or water until glossy and loosened.
- Expect softer rice than fresh.

## Risotto freezer plan

- **Best freeze point:** not recommended
- **Portioning:** skip freezing if texture matters
- **Freeze:** quality loss is noticeable
- **Thaw:** overnight in fridge if you must
- **Reheat:** low heat with hot stock or water until loosened; expect softer rice than fresh

# Special Notes

## Steak service

- Leftover steak should be refrigerated promptly and reheated gently.
- Best serving ideas if adding another side:
  - pommes purée
  - mashed potatoes
  - roasted asparagus
  - green beans
  - sharply dressed salad

## Steak sodium

- Main sodium drivers are steak seasoning and stock.
- Use low-sodium stock.
- Do not salt the sauce early.

## Risotto service

- Get the steak into its rest window first, then finish the risotto and plate immediately.
- That sequencing keeps the steak juicy and the risotto fluid at the same time.

## Risotto sodium

- Biggest salt drivers are stock, Parmesan, and Taleggio.
- Use low-sodium stock.
- Season at the end, not the front.

## Wine choice

- Use a dry red you would actually drink.
- Avoid wines that are very tannic, very acidic, or aggressively oaky; those traits concentrate as the wine reduces.

# Safety and Correctness Notes

## Steak

For whole-muscle beef steaks, USDA consumer guidance is **145°F with a 3-minute rest**.

This runbook intentionally targets a more traditional medium-rare eating result for quality, with pull temperatures around **120 to 125°F**. Use a thermometer and your own judgment.

## Risotto

Traditional Taleggio risotto for four can run much heavier on the cheese: one traditional-style reference uses **12 oz Carnaroli rice** and **7 oz Taleggio**, while a radicchio-Taleggio version uses a smaller **2.1 oz / 60 g Taleggio** finish plus Parmesan and added hot liquid to correct final consistency.

This recipe deliberately follows the lighter finish-cheese direction because the risotto is meant to sit beside NY strip steak, not compete with it. The lighter Taleggio ratio is a deliberate side-dish adaptation, not a claim that heavier Taleggio risotto is wrong.

# Emergency Compression Plan

## If everything is running late, priority order is:

1. Finish sauce base.
2. Start or finish risotto pressure-cook.
3. Dry and sear steaks.
4. Finish risotto during steak rest.
5. Finish sauce last.

## What can be sacrificed

- Optional thyme garnish
- Optional vinegar adjustment
- Perfectly warm plates
- Slicing steak before plating

## What should not be sacrificed

- Dry steak surface
- Full pan preheat
- Steak rest
- Risotto final loosening
- Low heat after butter goes into Cabernet sauce
- Immediate serving after risotto finish
