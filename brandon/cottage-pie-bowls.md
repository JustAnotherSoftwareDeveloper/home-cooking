# Classic Two-Phase Cottage-Pie Bowls

## Title & Overview

This is a bowl-style cottage pie: creamy mashed potatoes with a thick ground-beef, carrot, pea, garlic, and tomato-paste filling spooned over the top. Because the protein is beef, "cottage pie" is the more precise name; shepherd's pie traditionally uses lamb, while cottage pie uses beef [1].

This version is designed for an Instant Pot-only kitchen. It uses a two-phase workflow: first cook and mash the potatoes, then make the beef filling in the same pot. The method avoids an oven, avoids onion and mushroom, keeps the filling thick rather than stew-like, and uses frozen vegetables to reduce shopping and prep.

## Ingredients

- 2 1/2 lb Yukon Gold or russet potatoes, peeled and cut into 1 1/2-in chunks
- 1 cup water, for cooking potatoes
- 1/2 cup milk, preferably lactose-free if needed
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1/4 cup plain Greek yogurt
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt, divided
- 1 tsp avocado oil or neutral oil
- 1 1/4 lb 90% lean ground beef
- 3 garlic cloves, minced, or 1 tbsp garlic paste
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tbsp low-sodium tamari or low-sodium soy sauce
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 1/2 tsp sweet paprika
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 3/4 cup water
- 12 oz frozen peas and carrots
- 1 tbsp cornstarch
- 2 tbsp cold water, for slurry

## Allergy & Dietary Notes

- **Contains:** beef, dairy, soy if using tamari or soy sauce. Contains wheat if using regular soy sauce instead of gluten-free tamari.
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Low-sodium tamari -> low-sodium soy sauce -> similar flavor, less gluten-friendly.
  - Frozen peas and carrots -> frozen carrots and green beans -> less sweet, potentially gentler if peas bother the eater.
  - Greek yogurt -> more milk -> milder, looser mashed potatoes.
  - 90% lean beef -> 85% lean beef -> richer, but drain more fat before building the filling.
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - Instant mashed potatoes -> much easier, but the texture is thinner and less meal-like.
  - Raw diced potatoes in the beef filling -> turns the dish toward hash or stew and makes the filling harder to control.
  - Gravy packet -> many contain onion, mushroom, high sodium, or vague flavorings.
- **Diet toggles:**
  - Low-sodium: reduce tamari to 2 tsp, use 1/2 tsp kosher salt total, and increase garlic, thyme, and paprika slightly.
  - Gluten-free: use tamari labeled gluten-free.
  - Low-FODMAP-leaning: omit garlic and rely on tomato paste, thyme, paprika, tamari, and dairy for flavor. Do not assume onion-family substitutes are tolerated.
  - Dairy-free: use olive oil instead of butter, skip yogurt, and use unsweetened soy milk or extra potato cooking water. Texture will be less creamy.

## Quick Overview

- **What you are making:** Cottage-pie bowls with creamy mashed potatoes and thick ground-beef vegetable filling.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** Potatoes should mash easily, and the beef filling should be thick enough to mound on a spoon.
- **Workflow:** Pressure-cook potatoes, mash them in a bowl, then use the same Instant Pot to brown beef and make the filling.
- **Make-ahead:** Portion mashed potatoes and filling together for easy meals, or separately if you want cleaner texture.
- **Reheat plan:** Reheat covered with a splash of water or milk until steaming, then stir only enough to loosen.
- **Primary risks:** Watery filling, scorched tomato paste, bland potatoes, and over-stirred gummy mash.

## Gather & Stage

- Peel potatoes and cut them into 1 1/2-in chunks.
- Mince garlic or measure garlic paste.
- Measure tomato paste, tamari, thyme, paprika, salt, and pepper into small piles or cups.
- Keep frozen peas and carrots frozen until needed.
- Stir cornstarch with 2 tbsp cold water in a small cup; keep it near the pot.
- Set out the large bowl, strainer or colander, and fork or potato masher.
- **Checkpoint:** potatoes are cut, seasonings are measured, slurry is mixed, and the holding bowl is ready.

## Instructions

1. Pressure-cook the potatoes
   - Add potatoes and 1 cup water to the Instant Pot.
     - Lock the lid and cook on **High Pressure** for 5 minutes.
     - Quick-release the pressure when the cook time ends.
   - Drain the potatoes well.
     - Transfer potatoes to the large bowl.
     - Empty and dry the Instant Pot insert enough that no puddle remains.
   - **Checkpoint:** potatoes break easily when pressed with a fork.
     - *If potatoes resist mashing -> return them to the pot with 1/4 cup water -> cook on High Pressure 2 more minutes -> cue is fork-tender centers.*

2. Mash and hold the potatoes
   - Add milk, butter, Greek yogurt, and 1/2 tsp kosher salt to the hot potatoes.
     - **Mash** until mostly smooth but not gluey.
     - Taste and adjust with a small pinch of salt if needed.
   - Cover the bowl loosely and set aside.
     - Do not whip aggressively; overworked potatoes can turn gummy.
   - **Checkpoint:** potatoes are creamy, spoonable, and thick enough to hold soft ridges.
     - *If potatoes are too stiff -> add milk 1 tbsp at a time -> cue is soft spoonable mash.*
     - *If potatoes are too loose -> let them stand uncovered 5 minutes -> cue is visible steam escaping and texture tightening.*

3. Brown the beef and build flavor
   - Set the Instant Pot to **Saute Normal**. Add avocado oil.
     - Add ground beef and remaining 1/4 tsp kosher salt.
     - **Brown** 5 to 7 minutes, breaking it into small crumbles, until no pink remains and some browned spots form.
   - Drain or spoon off excess fat if there is more than 1 tbsp in the pot.
     - Add garlic, tomato paste, tamari, thyme, paprika, and black pepper.
     - **Saute** 1 to 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until tomato paste darkens slightly and smells savory.
   - **Checkpoint:** beef smells browned and tomato paste is no longer raw-smelling.
     - *If browned bits stick hard -> add 2 tbsp water -> scrape until the bottom feels smooth -> cue is no rough patches under the spatula.*

4. Simmer the filling
   - Add 3/4 cup water and scrape the bottom thoroughly.
     - Stir in frozen peas and carrots.
     - **Simmer** on **Saute Normal** for 4 to 6 minutes, until vegetables are hot and the liquid looks lightly beefy and red-brown.
   - Stir the cornstarch slurry again, then pour it into the pot.
     - **Simmer** 1 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the filling thickens.
     - The texture should be closer to loose sloppy joe filling than soup.
   - **Checkpoint:** filling mounds on a spoon and leaves a clean trail for 1 second when scraped across the bottom.
     - *If filling is watery -> simmer uncovered 2 to 4 minutes -> cue is glossy gravy clinging to beef.*
     - *If filling is too thick -> add water 1 tbsp at a time -> cue is spoonable but not soupy.*

5. Assemble and taste-adjust
   - Spoon mashed potatoes into 6 meal-prep containers or bowls.
     - Top each with beef filling.
     - For cleaner storage, keep potatoes on one side and beef filling on the other rather than fully mixing.
   - Taste-adjust loop:
     - Need more savory depth -> add 1 tsp tamari to the filling.
     - Need brightness -> add 1 tsp Greek yogurt on top when serving.
     - Need more roundness -> add 1 tsp butter to the potatoes.
     - Need more herb flavor -> add a small pinch of thyme.
   - **Checkpoint:** each bowl has creamy potatoes, thick beef filling, and no pooled liquid at the bottom.

## Common Issues

- **Watery filling:** too much water or vegetables released moisture -> simmer uncovered and thicken with slurry -> filling should mound on a spoon.
- **Bland potatoes:** under-salted mash -> add a pinch of salt and 1 tbsp yogurt or milk -> potatoes should taste good alone.
- **Burn risk:** tomato paste stuck before liquid was added -> deglaze before simmering -> pot bottom should feel smooth.
- **Greasy filling:** beef released excess fat -> spoon off fat before adding tomato paste -> filling should be glossy, not oily.

## Make-Ahead Notes

- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 5 and portion into shallow containers.
  - Holds: 3 to 4 days.
  - Serve/Reheat: reheat covered with 1 to 2 tbsp water or milk until steaming hot; stir gently after heating.
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: after Step 5, fully cooled.
  - Portioning: freeze in individual containers, with potato and filling side by side.
  - Freeze: up to 2 months for best texture.
  - Thaw: overnight in the fridge.
  - Reheat: covered microwave or Instant Pot Saute Low with a splash of water or milk until steaming; stir gently to reset texture.

## Reheat Plan

- **Best method:** microwave covered -> 90 seconds, stir gently, then 45 to 90 seconds more -> until steaming hot throughout.
- **Instant Pot method:** **Saute Low** -> add one portion plus 1 to 2 tbsp water or milk -> 3 to 6 minutes, stirring gently -> until steaming.
- **Texture reset:** add water to the beef side and milk to the potato side before reheating when possible.
- *If potatoes reheat stiff -> add 1 tbsp milk and fold gently -> cue is creamy mash again.*

## Troubleshooting

- Filling turns soupy -> vegetables released water or slurry was too weak -> simmer uncovered, then add 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 1 tbsp cold water -> recovery cue is filling that leaves a trail.
- Filling tastes flat -> not enough browning or tomato paste was not cooked -> simmer 2 more minutes with 1 tsp tamari and a pinch of thyme -> recovery cue is savory beef flavor.
- Potatoes turn gummy -> overmixed after adding dairy -> stop mashing and fold in 1 tbsp warm milk -> recovery cue is looser texture without more beating.
- Filling tastes too salty -> tamari plus salt stacked too hard -> add plain mashed potato or extra frozen vegetables -> recovery cue is balanced savory flavor.

## Geometry Notes

- Pan/pot size used: Instant Pot -- 6 QT.
- Target thickness / layer depth:
  - Potatoes: one loose layer in the pot is fine for pressure cooking.
  - Filling: should sit below the halfway mark after vegetables are added.
- Crowding rule:
  - Do not double this in a 6 QT Instant Pot.
  - Browning 1 1/4 lb ground beef is the practical upper limit before the pot steams more than browns.
- Covered/uncovered plan:
  - Potatoes pressure-cook covered.
  - Beef filling is finished uncovered on Saute so excess moisture can evaporate.

## Scaling & Batch Size

- Scaling notes:
  - To halve: use 1 1/4 lb potatoes, 1/2 cup water for pressure cooking, 10 oz beef, 6 oz frozen vegetables, and halve the seasonings.
  - To increase: do not double in one 6 QT batch. Make two batches or use shortcut mashed potatoes.
- Batch strategy:
  - This recipe is calibrated for 6 realistic meal portions, not 10 small portions.
- Pot swaps:
  - In an 8 QT Instant Pot, add 2 to 3 tbsp extra water when cooking potatoes and monitor the beef filling more closely because the wider base evaporates faster.

## Special Notes

- Storage & reheat: Cool in shallow containers, refrigerate promptly, and use fridge leftovers within 3 to 4 days. For longer storage, freeze portions; reheat to 165 deg F if using a thermometer [7].
- Serving ideas: plain cucumber slices, soft-cooked green beans, extra Greek yogurt, or a small sprinkle of cheddar after reheating.
- Sodium notes:
  - Top sodium drivers: kosher salt, low-sodium tamari, and dairy/ground beef brand variation.
  - Sodium levers: reduce tamari to 2 tsp, use 1/2 tsp kosher salt total, avoid bouillon/gravy packets, and finish with yogurt instead of more salt.
  - Approximate sodium density: about 1,625 mg sodium per 1,000 kcal using the estimate above.

## Variations

1. **Cheddar-mash version:** Stir 1/2 cup shredded cheddar into the mashed potatoes after mashing. This makes the bowls richer and more comfort-food-heavy, but sodium and saturated fat increase.

2. **Green bean version:** Replace peas and carrots with frozen carrots and green beans. This is less sweet and may be gentler if peas are not tolerated.

3. **Shortcut mash version:** Make only the beef filling in the Instant Pot and serve it over prepared mashed potatoes. This is less good texturally but is the easiest version for a novice.

4. **Tomato-heavier version:** Increase tomato paste to 3 tbsp and add 1 to 2 tbsp extra water. This makes the filling more savory and red-sauce-like, but it must be scraped carefully to avoid sticking.

## Safety & Correctness Notes

This is a cottage-pie bowl, not a broiled casserole and not a stew. Traditional pressure-cooker shepherd's or cottage pie methods often cook meat filling and potatoes together using a trivet, then optionally broil the assembled pie [2][3][4]. This recipe intentionally uses a two-phase Instant Pot-only path because it avoids requiring a trivet or oven and gives better control over mashed potatoes and filling thickness.
