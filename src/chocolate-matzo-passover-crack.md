# Classic Pecan Chocolate Matzo Crack

## Title & Overview
This is the classic Passover bark built for your **13×9 light metal tray**: broken matzo in a single layer, a thin brown-sugar butter toffee, melted semisweet chocolate, chopped pecans, and a little flaky salt. It is meant to eat crisp and snappy, not thick or fudgy. Because you are using a **13×9** rather than a larger rimmed sheet, the batch is scaled down so the caramel stays thin and sets cleanly. Pecan-topped versions are already standard in this recipe family, so the nut swap does not require a structural method change. [1][2][3][4][8]

## Ingredients
This keeps the consensus **1:1 butter-to-brown-sugar** toffee and the usual chocolate finish, but scales the batch to your **13×9** geometry. The larger published versions commonly use about **4 to 6 sheets of matzo, 1 cup butter, and 1 cup brown sugar** on a bigger rimmed baking sheet, so halving that structure is the safer fit here. Pecan-topped versions are directly attested in multiple recipes in this family. [1][2][3][4][8]

- about 3 matzo sheets, broken as needed to cover the tray in one flat layer
- 1/2 cup unsalted butter, cut into chunks
- 1/2 cup packed dark brown sugar
- 1 cup semisweet chocolate chips, or 6 ounces chopped semisweet chocolate
- 1/2 to 2/3 cup pecans, **coarsely chopped**, lightly toasted if desired **[pre-prep]**
- 1/4 teaspoon flaky sea salt or kosher salt, plus a little more for finishing if needed

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** dairy, wheat/gluten, tree nuts
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - pecans -> walnuts -> slightly less sweet, a little more assertive bitterness -> no timing change [1][4]
  - pecans -> sliced almonds -> slightly firmer, more brittle top -> no timing change, but keep the almond layer light [2][4]
  - semisweet chocolate -> bittersweet chocolate -> less sweet, slightly firmer set -> no timing change [1][4]
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - butter -> margarine or non-dairy sticks -> workable, but the toffee can spread less evenly and set less cleanly -> if you must, keep whisking and expect a less classic buttercrunch result [3][5]

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** a thin, crisp matzo bark with toffee, chocolate, pecans, and salt
- **Texture and doneness cues:** the toffee must be **bubbling all over** before it leaves the oven; that is the main crispness cue
- **Workflow:** line the tray, patch in the matzo, make the toffee, **Bake**, melt the chocolate on residual heat, then top and chill
- **Make-ahead:** this is a set-and-serve dessert, so it is well suited to earlier prep
- **Primary risks:** too-thick caramel from an oversized batch, underbaked toffee, or chopping the pecans too fine so the top feels dusty instead of crunchy

## Gather & Stage
- Line the **13×9 light metal tray** with foil, then cover the foil with parchment.
- Break the matzo into pieces and fit it into the tray in **one flat layer**.
- Patch the tray like a mosaic; **small gaps are fine, overlap is not**.
- Put the butter and brown sugar together in the saucepan.
- Coarsely chop the pecans and keep them in a small bowl with the salt beside the tray.
- Open the chocolate before you start cooking the caramel.
- Set out a basic spreading utensil.
- **Checkpoint:** the tray is lined, the matzo fully covers the base in one layer, and every topping is ready before the sugar starts cooking.

## Instructions

1. **Prep the tray and oven**
   - **Preheat** the oven to **350 deg F**.
     - Keep a rack in the middle position.
     - Confirm the matzo is arranged in a **single flat layer** in the **13×9 light metal tray**.
     - Small gaps are acceptable; overlap is not.
     - **Checkpoint:** the tray is fully built and ready before the toffee starts.
     - *If a piece rides up over another piece -> re-break it and flatten the layer -> cue it is fixed: the base lies flat and looks patchworked rather than stacked.* [1][4]

2. **Make the toffee**
   - **Cook** the butter and brown sugar over **medium heat**, stirring or whisking constantly until the mixture comes to a full boil.
     - Once boiling, **boil uncovered** for **3 minutes**, still stirring, until the mixture looks **foamy, glossy, and slightly thickened**.
     - Immediately pour it over the matzo and **spread** it quickly into a thin, even layer.
     - **Checkpoint:** the matzo is covered edge to edge with a thin caramel film, not thick pools.
     - *If the mixture looks greasy or separated -> keep whisking over the heat -> cue it is fixed: the caramel comes back together into a unified glossy boil.*
     - *If the caramel starts setting before you finish spreading -> work from the center outward and prioritize the barest spots first -> cue it is fixed: only tiny thin gaps remain, not major uncovered areas.* [1][2][3][4]

3. **Bake the toffee layer**
   - **Bake uncovered** at **350 deg F** for **8 to 10 minutes**, until the toffee is **crackled and bubbling all over**.
     - Start checking at 8 minutes; a 13×9 pan can finish quickly once the surface is fully active.
     - Pull it when the color is deep caramel, not dark mahogany.
     - **Checkpoint:** the tray is bubbling broadly across the surface, not just in isolated spots.
     - *If only the edges are bubbling -> give it another 1 to 2 minutes -> cue it is fixed: the center is bubbling too.*
     - *If the corners smell bitter or darken too quickly -> pull the tray immediately -> cue it is fixed: the batch still smells like toffee, not burnt sugar.* [1][2][3][4]

4. **Melt and spread the chocolate**
   - Remove the tray to a rack, scatter the chocolate evenly over the hot surface, and let it sit **3 to 5 minutes** until soft and shiny.
     - **Spread** the chocolate into a thin, even layer.
     - Do not press so hard that you tear the toffee underneath.
     - **Checkpoint:** the chocolate spreads smoothly without dragging crumbs or tearing the candy.
     - *If the chips still resist spreading -> wait another minute -> cue it is fixed: they smear cleanly into a continuous layer.* [1][3][6][8]

5. **Finish and set**
   - **Top** with the pecans and a light sprinkle of salt, pressing gently so the nuts adhere.
     - Let the tray cool **15 to 20 minutes**, then **Chill** until firm, about **45 minutes**.
     - Break into shards or cut into small squares once fully set.
     - Taste-adjust loop: salt -> add a tiny extra pinch only if the chocolate tastes flat; acid -> skip; heat -> skip; herbs -> skip.
     - **Checkpoint:** the finished bark is firm and **snaps cleanly** instead of bending. [1][3][5]

## Common Issues
- **Chewy instead of crisp:** the toffee did not bake long enough -> bake until it is bubbling all over -> a chilled piece should snap [1][3][7]
- **Patchy caramel layer:** the batch was too thick for the pan or the caramel set too fast -> keep the batch scaled for the tray and spread immediately -> the pre-bake layer should look thin and even [1][2][4]
- **Chocolate tears the surface:** the chips were spread too early -> wait until they turn soft and glossy -> the chocolate should smear cleanly [1][6][8]
- **Oily-feeling nut top:** the pecans were chopped too fine or toasted too aggressively -> use a coarse chop and toast lightly if at all -> the top should stay crunchy, not pasty [1][3][7]

## Make-Ahead Notes
- Fridge plan:
  - Stop after Step 5: cool briefly, then chill until fully firm
  - Holds: about 1 week airtight
  - Serve/Reheat: no reheat; serve straight from the fridge or let sit 5 to 10 minutes at room temperature [1][3][5]
- Freezer plan:
  - Best freeze point: after Step 5, once fully set and portioned
  - Portioning: layer with parchment between pieces
  - Freeze: up to **3 months** [5]
  - Thaw: a few minutes at room temperature, or move to the fridge before serving
  - Reheat: none

## Troubleshooting
- Toffee looks split in the pot -> the butter-sugar emulsion has not come together yet -> keep whisking over **medium heat** -> it returns to a glossy, unified boil [1][2][3]
- Bark bends instead of snapping -> the toffee was underbaked -> next time wait for bubbling across the whole tray -> chilled pieces break sharply [1][3][7]
- Chocolate layer looks lumpy -> the chips did not soften long enough -> wait another minute on the hot tray -> the surface spreads smoothly [1][6][8]
- Pecan layer feels too dense -> nuts were chopped too small or added too heavily -> use a coarse chop and keep the layer loose -> you should still see chocolate between the pecan pieces [1][3][7]

## Geometry Notes
- Pan/pot size used: **Sheet / baking tray — 13×9, metal, light**; medium saucepan
- Target thickness / layer depth: one single layer of broken matzo with a **thin** caramel film and a **thin** chocolate layer
- Crowding rule: matzo may be broken to fit, but pieces must stay flat and not overlap

## Scaling & Batch Size
The common published “full” formulas are written for larger rimmed sheets, typically around **11×17** or similar. That is why this version is scaled down; if you want the usual 1-cup-butter / 1-cup-sugar batch, move to a larger rimmed sheet instead of forcing it into a 13×9. [1][2][4]

- Scaling notes: double this recipe only if you also move to a larger rimmed baking sheet
- Batch strategy: for your current tray, keep the single scaled batch
- Pan swaps: if you later use a dark metal pan, reduce oven temperature by about **25 deg F** or start checking early [4]

## Variations
1. **Walnut version:** Swap the pecans for walnuts for a slightly more bitter, less buttery nut profile that still works cleanly in the same method. [1][4]
2. **Almond version:** Use a lighter layer of sliced toasted almonds for a more brittle, buttercrunch-like finish; keep the almond layer restrained so the top does not get crowded. [2][4]
3. **Salt-forward version:** Keep the pecans but use bittersweet chocolate and a slightly more assertive flaky-salt finish for a more adult sweet-salty profile. [1][3]
