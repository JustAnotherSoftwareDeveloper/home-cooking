## Finocchiona, Coppa, Mozzarella, and Shaved Fennel Pressed Slab Sandwich

### Title & Overview
This is the slab-bread version of the sandwich: finocchiona, hot coppa, mozzarella, pesto, and a very lightly dressed shaved-fennel and arugula layer pressed inside a dense focaccia or schiacciata slab. It is intentionally more salumi-forward than Giada’s lighter picnic loaf, but still restrained enough to press, chill, slice cleanly, and eat from a cooler. The key difference from the loaf version is structural: with slab bread, you do **not** hollow the interior. You keep the filling shallower, use cheese and pesto as moisture buffers, and press the whole sandwich flat for cohesion. [Food Network — Marinated Salumi Sandwich](https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/giada-de-laurentiis/marinated-salumi-sandwich-3364318)

- **Yield:** 4 substantial sandwiches or 8 picnic halves
- **Active Prep:** 25 minutes
- **Inactive Prep / Hands-Off / Rest:** 2 to 4 hours; up to 12 hours
- **Cook:** 0 minutes
- **Total:** 2 hours 25 minutes to 12 hours 25 minutes
- **Make-ahead:** Best assembled at least 2 hours ahead and pressed under weight. The sweet spot is about **4 to 12 hours**; longer than that softens the bread more than it improves the sandwich. ## Ingredients
- 1 dense focaccia slab, schiacciata slab, pizza bianca slab, or seeded semolina slab/low-profile round, about 9×13 in and 18 to 20 oz
- 1/3 cup basil pesto
- 8 oz low-moisture whole-milk mozzarella, sliced thin
- 8 oz thinly sliced finocchiona
- 8 oz thinly sliced hot coppa
- 1 1/2 cups baby arugula
- 1/2 small fennel bulb, very thinly sliced
- 2 tsp fresh lemon juice
- 1 tsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt, plus more only if needed
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

## Allergy & Dietary Notes
- **Contains:** wheat/gluten, dairy, pork, tree nuts *(if using standard pesto)*
- **Easy swaps (keep technique intact):**
  - Nut-free pesto -> same structure -> no method change
  - Mild soppressata or sweet coppa -> same layering -> less heat
  - 4 oz provolone + 4 oz low-moisture mozzarella -> firmer, slightly saltier sandwich -> slightly better moisture barrier
- **Hard swaps (not recommended / major change):**
  - Burrata or loose stracciatella -> too wet for the pressed slab path -> use only for same-day, unpressed sandwiches
  - Very airy ciabatta or soft sandwich bread -> weaker press and worse moisture control -> use dense slab bread instead

## Quick Overview
- **What you are making:** A pressed slab sandwich with salumi, mozzarella, pesto, and a restrained fennel-arugula layer.
- **Texture and doneness cues:** The finished sandwich should feel **flat, firm, and compact**, not puffy; the bread should be seasoned and softened, not wet or collapsing.
- **Workflow:** Slice the slab horizontally, spread pesto, put **cheese against both bread faces**, layer salumi in the middle, add a light fennel-arugula layer, wrap tightly, then **Chill** under weight.
- **Make-ahead:** Several hours of pressing improves flavor and sliceability, but going much past 12 hours increases softness risk.
- **Primary risks:** Wet cheese, overdressed greens, or too much filling height are the main failure modes. [Project Meal Plan — Pesto Chicken Overnight Pressed Sandwiches](https://projectmealplan.com/pesto-chicken-overnight-pressed-sandwiches/)

## Gather & Stage
- Measure and group all ingredients by phase; place them within reach.
- Slice the mozzarella and let it sit on paper towels while you prep everything else.
- Slice the fennel as thinly as possible.
- Set out the **Sheet / baking tray** and **Baking pan** now so the pressing step is immediate.
- Cut two large sheets of parchment for wrapping.
- **Checkpoint:** everything needed for Step 1 is ready and within reach.

## Instructions

### 1. Prep the bread
- **Slice** the slab horizontally through the middle to create a top and bottom half.
  - Do **not** hollow it out; this is the slab path.
  - If the interior is very airy or springy, press it lightly with your palm to flatten the crumb a bit rather than removing it.
  - **Spread** the pesto evenly over both cut faces in a thin layer.
  - **Checkpoint:** the slab is evenly split, the crumb is intact, and the pesto is a coating rather than a heavy smear.
  - *If the bread feels very airy and rebounds hard -> compress the cut faces lightly with your hands before adding fillings -> cue it’s fixed: the slab closes more neatly and does not spring open.*

### 2. Dress the fennel and arugula lightly
- **Toss** the arugula and shaved fennel with the lemon juice, olive oil, salt, and black pepper.
  - The goal is a **light gloss**, not a true salad.
  - There should be no visible puddling in the bowl.
  - **Checkpoint:** the greens look seasoned and lively, not slick or wet.
  - *If the mixture looks shiny enough to drip -> add a little more dry arugula or blot briefly with paper towel -> cue it’s fixed: the leaves separate easily and do not leave liquid behind.*

### 3. Build the sandwich
- **Layer** the sandwich with bread protection in mind.
  - On the bottom half, arrange half the mozzarella directly against the bread.
  - Layer all the finocchiona, then all the coppa, folding the slices rather than stretching them flat so the center still feels meaty after pressing.
  - Add the fennel-arugula mixture in a restrained, even layer.
  - Finish with the remaining mozzarella directly under the top bread half.
  - Close the sandwich and **Press** firmly with both palms.
  - **Checkpoint:** the stack is even and relatively shallow; nothing is spilling from the sides.
  - *If the greens push out the edges -> remove a little before wrapping -> cue it’s fixed: the top slab sits flat without rocking.*
  - *If the mozzarella is shedding noticeable liquid -> unwrap, blot it, and rebuild -> cue it’s fixed: the bread face looks coated, not slick.*

### 4. Wrap and press
- **Wrap** the sandwich tightly in parchment, then in plastic wrap.
  - Put it on the **Sheet / baking tray**.
  - Set the **Baking pan** on top; add the **cast iron skillet** only if the sandwich still feels tall and loose after 10 to 15 minutes.
  - **Chill** for at least 2 hours and ideally 4 hours; stop before about 12 hours.
  - **Checkpoint:** the sandwich feels flatter and denser when lifted.
  - *If the slab still feels loose after 2 hours -> keep pressing another 1 to 3 hours -> cue it’s fixed: the sandwich holds together as one block instead of feeling fluffy.*

### 5. Slice, pack, and serve
- **Slice** the sandwich into 4 large portions or 8 picnic halves with a gentle sawing motion.
  - Rewrap the cut pieces in parchment and place them in a rigid container.
  - Pack the container near the top-center of the cooler with ice packs above and below, separated by a towel so condensation does not soak the wrapping.
  - Taste-adjust loop before final wrap: **acid** -> a few drops more lemon only if flat; **salt** -> only if the mozzarella is muting the salumi; **heat** -> more black pepper if needed; **herbs** -> a few extra arugula leaves if it wants freshness.
  - Keep it at **40°F or below**, and once served, do not leave it out more than **2 hours**, or **1 hour above 90°F**. [Project Meal Plan — Pesto Chicken Overnight Pressed Sandwiches](https://projectmealplan.com/pesto-chicken-overnight-pressed-sandwiches/)
  - **Checkpoint:** each piece stays together when lifted and the bread is supple, not soggy.

## Common Issues
- **Bread softens too much:** Too much liquid from cheese or greens -> dry the mozzarella more aggressively and dress the greens more lightly -> the crumb stays intact.
- **Sandwich feels underbuilt after pressing:** Meats packed too flat -> fold or drape the salumi rather than plastering it flush -> the center reads meaty instead of compressed-flat.
- **Sandwich slides when bitten:** Wettest ingredients too close to the bread -> keep cheese directly against both bread faces and greens in the middle -> the bite feels locked together.

## Make-Ahead Notes
- **Fridge plan:**
  - Stop after Step 4: keep the sandwich tightly wrapped and pressed.
  - Holds: best within 24 hours; ideal pressing window is 4 to 12 hours.
  - Container guidance: after pressing, keep it wrapped and store it in a rigid container so the slab cannot be crushed.
  - Serve: cold from the cooler or after a short 10-minute sit in the shade.
- **Freezer plan:**
  - Best freeze point: not recommended for the assembled sandwich.
  - Freeze: pesto only, if needed.
  - Thaw: overnight in the refrigerator.

## Troubleshooting
- Sandwich turns soggy overnight -> Wet toppings or overlong press -> keep the pressed build free of juicy tomatoes and stop before 12 hours -> the bread stays seasoned but not limp.
- Slab bread compresses too much -> Bread was too airy or too soft -> use denser focaccia/schiacciata next time and press with less weight -> the crumb stays chewy rather than pasty.
- Flavor feels muted cold -> Chilling dulled the acid and pepper -> add a small squeeze of lemon and extra black pepper before final wrap -> the fennel and salumi wake back up.

## Geometry Notes
- **Bread used:** one dense slab, about **9×13 in** and **18 to 20 oz**
- **Target thickness:** about `2 1/4 to 2 1/2 in` before pressing; about `1 1/2 to 1 3/4 in` after pressing
- **Crowding rule:** keep the greens shallow; the slab path wants width, not height

## Scaling & Batch Size
- **Scaling notes:** This version is calibrated for 4 substantial sandwiches.
- **Batch strategy:** For 6 to 8 sandwiches, make **two separate slabs** instead of one oversized sandwich so the pressing stays even.
- **Pan swaps:** If needed, press with a second tray and a heavy book or skillet rather than stacking another sandwich on top.

## Special Notes
- **Bread examples that fit well:** dense bakery focaccia slab, schiacciata, pizza bianca, or a seeded semolina slab/low-profile round with some chew. These all map better to the pressed slab workflow than airy ciabatta does. [Project Meal Plan — Pesto Chicken Overnight Pressed Sandwiches](https://projectmealplan.com/pesto-chicken-overnight-pressed-sandwiches/)
- **Picnic pack order:** parchment first, then plastic wrap, then rigid container, then cooler with cold packs above and below.
- **Serving ideas:** chips, grapes, marinated olives, or a tart sparkling drink all fit.
- **Sodium notes:** finocchiona, coppa, pesto, and mozzarella all contribute; do not oversalt the greens.

## Variations
1. **Mortadella, pistachio, and stracciatella slab:** Replace the finocchiona and coppa with mortadella, use pistachio cream or pistachio pesto, and use stracciatella only for same-day assembly. This is the more Florence sandwich-counter version rather than the pressed picnic-deli version. [The New Yorker — The Irresistible Sandwiches of All’Antico Vinaio](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/04/25/the-irresistible-sandwiches-of-allantico-vinaio)

2. **Hot soppressata and provolone slab:** Replace the coppa with hot soppressata and some or all of the mozzarella with sharp provolone. Keep any roasted peppers separate until just before serving if you want them.

3. **Finocchiona, pecorino, and pepper-jelly slab:** Replace the mozzarella with thin pecorino or provolone and add only a very thin swipe of pepper jelly. This pushes the sandwich more toward wine-bar sandwich-shop energy and away from the softer picnic-loaf profile.
